# Abstography — Working Definition

**Status:** Coherent working definition, revised 8 October 2026.

**Purpose:** To define what Abstography is, why that productive organization warrants a concept of its own, and how its ground-up constitution of a domain's possible structures and possible work establishes a productive capability. The explanation connects worlds, Abstract Constructors, heterogeneous constructive means, substrates and products, using the deliberately chosen lithography analogy and Constructor Theory framework to make their relationships intelligible. On that foundation, the document introduces the Abstographic machine as the operative totality realizing such a capability.

This document states the best current conceptual understanding. Its commitments define what a developed account must explain and establish. They are not claims that a completed mathematical theory or a present implementation already realizes the whole capability. The mathematical notation below clarifies obligations; it does not settle the eventual foundations or prescribe a software architecture.

**Reading sequence.** §1 introduces the method, the reason for the concept, and the roles of its name, analogy and explanatory framework. §§2–4 establish its levels, possibility space and attainment claim. §5 distinguishes domain, universe, world, shared object and current state. §6 explains Abstract Constructors and their constructive participation. §7 develops the Constructor Theory-informed account of tasks, constructor kinds, repertoires, access and heterogeneous composition; §8 explains its connection to condition-governed activity. §§9–12 connect internal construction, substrate realization and the recursive formation of products and productive means. §§13–15 develop stability, domain mapping and the mathematical questions of coherent assembly. §16 then defines the Abstographic machine on those foundations. §§17–19 collect commitments, open work, sources and synthesis. The conceptual explanations establish what each question means before the notation develops its more precise obligations.

## 1. The central definition

**Abstography is a formal constructive method for constituting and exercising productive capabilities over bounded, infinite possibility spaces. It develops a domain's possible structures and possible work together: its Abstract Constructors and their meanings, worlds and relationships, task families and transformations, constructor kinds and repertoires, and the interfaces and bridges through which they become productive. Their lawful organization and operative realization establish a capability able to attain any admitted requirement within its declared space through condition-governed recursive construction.**

This is a ground-up constitution of the capability. The intended productive purpose helps determine what must become an object, what differences matter, what tasks can be formed, which kinds of constructive means they require and what their results make possible. Existing knowledge and capacities may supply material and realizing means, but their domain-specific roles and connections must receive an explicit constitution. The domain is not presumed to arrive with its ontology and possible work already completed.

**Its formal universe constitutes both the space of possible constructions and the semantic organization of the productive apparatus. That universe can contain genuinely different worlds which share objects or entire populations while organizing them differently. Processes and Conditions can concern one world or jointly constituted configurations spanning several. Their unity is constructive: shared identities, local meanings, interfaces and internal bridges make their contributions coherently usable together.**

**The capability can also form a shared semantic and constructive centre among multiple substrates. Substrate-originating objects, occurrences, relationships and changes receive their formal place in the appropriate worlds and their connections; they can occasion construction inside that formal universe, whose products are then realized in the same substrate or in other substrates according to the design. Work can also begin within the formal universe. These interactions belong to one coherent productive organization without requiring one uniform ontology or one causal starting point.**

An Abstract Constructor's contribution can concern the product being formed, the repertoire of transformations available to a configured constructor, the particular constructive process undertaken, or the situation making further work applicable. The same constituted object can participate in several of these ways, including through realization in another substrate.

Three commitments give this definition its particular meaning:

1. **A constituted space of possibilities.** The domain's objects, meanings, task families, transformations and productive means are defined together through laws admitting a delimited class of constructions, including cases never individually designed or instantiated.
2. **Constructive coverage of that space.** The apparatus must be able to attain the admitted requirements throughout that class, through its own established organization and supported means of further construction.
3. **Integrated formal and substrate realization.** The productive apparatus, what it acts upon, and what occurs or is created in its incorporated substrates have semantic and ontological representation in the formal universe throughout their participation. That representation can be constituted across several worlds and supports constructive interaction regardless of where the initiating occurrence originates.

A capability that designs a particular class of factories illustrates the level involved. Its object is the constructive possibility space of those factory designs. A particular factory design is one possible product within that space; the factory it describes has its own productive repertoire. The capability of designing the class and the capacity of operating a factory described by one of those designs are different levels of construction.

### 1.1 In what sense this is a formal constructive method

The current working designation is **formal constructive method**.

It is *formal* because the objects, identities, relationships, admissible compositions and transformations through which capability exists are given explicit constitution. Formality supplies a basis for determining what a construction means, how it can participate in further construction and which claims follow from the governing laws.

It is *constructive* because that constitution must support the formation of products and productive arrangements. The account must connect what is admissible with how it can be realized.

It is a *method* because it concerns the principled constitution and operation of such capability: moving from an intended productive purpose toward an adequate domain and apparatus, and from a proposed constitution toward an account of its constructive reach. Objects and tasks are developed reciprocally. A task can require a previously unconstituted subject or intermediate; an object can enable new tasks whose means and results then require definition. Domain mapping develops this relationship (§14.4); its work includes designing the possible domain activity, not only identifying its vocabulary.

“From the ground up” identifies responsibility for the whole included constitution. It does not require rediscovering every mathematical result or recreating every realizing mechanism. Adopted knowledge, reusable constructions and existing capacities must have a justified place in the domain, with the meanings and powers attributed to them made explicit. Constitutive design, factual knowledge and evidence of realizability retain their different grounds.

Abstography also has an essential processual aspect. Its apparatuses operate through successive, potentially branching and concurrent constructions; products can change what subsequent work is possible or required. Whether the final terminology should name Abstography a method, a process, or a method with a constitutive manufacturing process remains open. The substantive definition here includes both establishing capability and organizing its productive exercise.

### 1.2 Production and manufacture

*Production* means the realization of a required object, structure, arrangement or other specified outcome. A product can be a design, a program, a formal construction or a further productive arrangement.

*Manufacture* emphasizes dependable capability across a space of requirements. It concerns an apparatus able to produce members of its admitted class repeatedly, rather than one successful act of creation.

Physical manufacture can become part of an abstographic capability through suitable additional substrates and bridges. The definition already applies to productive worlds whose constructions are formal, informational or computational.

### 1.3 Why a distinct concept is warranted

The subject being designed is a productive capability, not only its individual products or the procedures used on particular occasions. Its constitution connects what can exist and what it means with what can be asked, transformed and produced, and with the means through which those accomplishments become possible. These relationships make the domain's formal organization and its productive apparatus parts of one account.

Naming this subject makes the whole available for explanation, design and criticism. We can ask what makes a domain productively operable, how the joint organization establishes its reach, which requirements belong to its possibility space, and what must change to extend that capability. The concept gives those questions a common subject rather than leaving them scattered among descriptions of individual components.

Its distinctive commitment is generative productive coverage: a bounded constitution can admit infinitely many cases, and its realized organization must support their admitted requirements under its operating premises. Particular tasks and routes need not have been individually anticipated. The meanings and powers through which unfamiliar cases can be handled must nevertheless belong to that constitution. This is the relationship that the concept of Abstography identifies and that a particular Abstographic machine must realize.

### 1.4 Why this name and explanatory framework were chosen

**The name and explanatory choices are deliberate means of making this organization understandable.** Lithography supplies a useful conceptual introduction to coordinated formation: a designed apparatus brings an organization into realized form through suitable formative means and receiving structures. The initial idea can be grasped without knowing the technical details of that industry. Abstography develops the reader's understanding from that intelligible relationship toward the formal constitution of productive possibility itself (§14.3).

Within the chosen name, *abstract* identifies the Abstract Constructors whose semantic organization participates in construction. *Graphy* evokes giving that organization realized form. The association with lithography helps readers understand the organized process and apparatus involved; **Abstract Light** makes the formative participation of the objects and their compositions intelligible (§6.8). This is an explanation of the intended naming and analogy, not a claim that abstract construction has literal optical properties.

Constructor Theory was chosen as a powerful, economical organizing framework. Its language lets us ask, in an accessible way, what accomplishment is required, what capacity can perform it, what undergoes transformation and how capacities can be combined or configured. These questions organize the account before its detailed mathematical development. The framework supplies substantive distinctions as well as a way to communicate them (§7).

| Part of the explanation | Its role |
| --- | --- |
| Abstography as a concept | Identifies the integrated constitution and exercise of generative productive capability as the subject to understand and develop. |
| Lithography and Abstract Light | Make coordinated formation, constructive participation and realization intuitively accessible. |
| Constructor Theory | Supplies an organizing language for tasks, repeatable capacities, programs, substrates and possible transformations. |
| Mathematical development | Gives the required relationships exact definitions, establishes their consequences and limits, and supports justified capability claims. |

The governing principles should be explainable without first requiring fluency in the eventual mathematical language. Simple organizing principles can require sophisticated mathematics to define and realize rigorously. Accessibility concerns understanding what the relationships are and why they matter; rigor concerns defining and establishing those relationships adequately. The account must provide both, with each formal construction answering an intelligible question rather than substituting technical vocabulary for its explanation.

## 2. The levels of the account

These terms identify connected aspects of the same subject.

| Term | Meaning in this account | Relationship to the other terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abstographic method | The principles and organized work through which a formal productive capability is constituted and exercised. | Establishes and governs the relationship between the possibility space and its realization. |
| Abstographic capability | The standing ability to realize admitted requirements throughout a specified possibility space. | What an adequate apparatus makes available under its stated operating premises. |
| Designed domain | The connected constitution of subjects, meanings, possible work and productive means for an intended capability; it may combine or reorganize several fields. | Determines what the capability can distinguish, ask, transform, construct and preserve. |
| Formal universe | The coherent constitution of the domain's worlds, objects, constructive possibilities and inter-world relationships, together with the account of their actuality. | Supplies the integrated formal basis of the capability. |
| Formal world | A differentiated semantic, ontological and constructive context within that universe. | Can contain distinct objects, share objects with other worlds, or organize the same population differently. |
| Current state | What is presently instantiated and how it is organized at a specified coherent stage or context. | Interprets the standing constitution without being identical to it. |
| Productive apparatus | The constituted arrangement of formal objects, constructors of the required kinds, programs, interfaces, substrates and bridges. | The organized means through which the capability exists and operates. |
| Abstographic machine | The operative totality realizing a designed domain's capability through that apparatus and its integrated formal universe. | Names the whole capable organization, developed in §16; it is not another primitive alongside its components. |
| Productive process | The causally connected transformations, decisions and constructions through which work develops. | Exercises the capability and can construct further supported productive means. |
| Productive occurrence | A particular exercise of the capability from particular requirements and circumstances. | Has its own intermediate states, transformations and outcomes. |
| Product | An object, structure or arrangement attained through construction. | May satisfy a requirement and may become a constituent of subsequent construction. |

These are roles and levels of explanation, not necessarily disjoint classes of entities. A program can be a product, an Abstract Constructor and a means of configuring another constructor. A productive process can produce a new apparatus component. “Universe” names the integrated formal totality here; its use does not yet select a set-theoretic universe, a particular category or a topos as the final foundation.

“Apparatus” emphasizes the organized means; “machine” emphasizes that whole organization as an operative unit of capability. Their referents may coincide. A **task** states an accomplishment or continuing obligation under defined circumstances. A **Process**, capitalized where useful, denotes a constituted productive responsibility together with its input, transformation and result account; it can address a family of tasks and admit several constructive routes. A **productive process** is the organized course of construction, and a particular **occurrence** exercises that organization on actual subjects. These distinctions permit a task, its means and its performance to be explained separately without assuming one program or fixed route per task (§7.1).

The distinctions also locate the strength of the central claim. Producing one object establishes something about that occurrence. An abstographic capability concerns the organized ability to produce across its entire admitted space.

## 3. The bounded, infinite possibility space

### 3.1 A boundary of constructive kind and reach

The possibility space concerns both what can be constituted and what can be accomplished: objects and configurations, requirements and task families, transformations and their compositions, programs and further productive arrangements. Their meanings and relationships are jointly designed for the intended capability. Structural expressibility, supported constructive means and actual attainment are connected claims with different burdens (§4).

Its boundary is supplied by such things as:

- The kinds of objects and structures available.
- Their admissible relationships and compositions.
- The differentiation and overlap of worlds, including which identities, populations and commitments are shared.
- The task families, their permissible effects and the transformations through which they can be performed.
- The kinds of constructor, their repertoires, available observations and admissible interventions.
- The programs and further constructive arrangements that can be formed.
- The capacities and restrictions of incorporated substrates.
- What the bridges and interfaces preserve and make usable.
- The operating premises under which the capability is claimed.

Here, **bounded** means delimited in constructive kind and reach. It is not a claim about metric boundedness, a finite catalogue or a fixed maximum number of objects.

An apparatus for designing a specified class of factories can support infinitely many distinct admissible designs while excluding other kinds of factories. The boundary concerns what the apparatus is constituted to design, not whether its repertoire has been enumerated.

### 3.2 Infinity through lawful generation

A finitely described system of objects and composition rules can generate an infinite class of possible structures. For example, a capability may allow arbitrary permitted finite assemblies without imposing one fixed upper bound on their size.

Each particular assembly can be finite while the class of such assemblies is infinite. A present world can likewise contain finitely many actual objects without exhausting its constitutive possibility space.

Infinity must arise from the actual laws of the capability. A small vocabulary does not by itself establish an infinite repertoire, and an infinite vocabulary does not establish productive coverage. The relevant question is what the constituted organization permits and can realize.

An abstographic capability therefore need not pre-store every product or every production path. It must possess the organization through which new admitted cases can be constructed.

This generative constitution includes possible work. A declared family of subjects can admit future task instances; a constructed arrangement can give rise to new comparison subjects; supported composition can form a specialized program or a further task specification. Each admitted case must obtain its meaning from the constitution and its supported constructive development. “All possible tasks are defined” concerns this account of kinds, formation, variation and lawful use, not an enumeration of every future occurrence.

### 3.3 The boundary belongs to the whole arrangement

The possibility space is jointly constituted. It cannot generally be obtained by separately listing what each component can do.

One stage may produce geometric structure, while a later stage requires geometric structure together with retained identities and supporting relationships. Their productive composition depends on preserving all the organization required by that continuation.

The same principle applies to programs, Conditions, substrates and bridges. Two worlds can each admit a construction without their joint use yet being constituted. A shared identity, compatible interface, cross-world dependency or adequate passage may be essential to that use. Likewise, a substrate's native power enters the abstographic capability through an adequate integration of that power. This integration must match objects and their possible compositions with suitable transformations, invocation circumstances, results, and passages in both directions. It includes the situations that the substrate can originate as well as the work that can be realized within it. The mere presence of a powerful external tool does not establish that all its capacities have become available within the productive universe.

Consequently, the account of the boundary must include interactions, dependencies and exclusions as well as individual capacities. A failed route does not by itself establish that a task is impossible: another lawful joint construction may attain the required effect. Equally, meaningful composition syntax alone does not establish that the corresponding constructive means exist.

### 3.4 Requirements, designs and products

A *requirement* specifies what must be attained. A *design* constitutes an intended organization. A *product* is an attained construction, which may itself be a design.

One requirement may admit many acceptable products. A more exact requirement may select one particular design, or one equivalence class of realizations.

This distinction is essential to the meaning of “any.” If a capability claims to realize every admissible design, it must preserve the distinctions needed to request those designs. Producing any convenient member of a broad acceptable family would establish a weaker capability.

### 3.5 Operating premises

A productive claim has operating premises: available resources, suitable starting circumstances, adequate representations and the capacities that the apparatus presupposes.

Where substrates can develop through their own processes or other participants' actions, these premises must also explain how such continuing change relates to the claimed attainment. The account must establish what progress and completion mean under the admitted interactions, rather than silently assume exclusive control of every event.

An infinite possibility space need not be realizable using a single fixed finite resource allocation. Larger admitted constructions may require larger allocations. The relationship between product size, resource needs and constructive possibility belongs to the account.

Formal and computational construction can avoid the need to physically fabricate an intermediate object at every design iteration. That is an important source of recursive flexibility. It does not remove computational, energetic, informational or physical resource limits.

## 4. What it means to realize anything within the space

The distinctive ambition is **domain-relative constructive universality**: one constituted capability supports the realization of any admitted requirement within its bounded domain.

This phrase names the intended property. Establishing it requires more than a vocabulary or a collection of examples.

| Level | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Expressive admissibility | The formal universe can constitute the requirement and the relevant kinds of result, including their local and cross-world structure. |
| Constructive availability | The apparatus supplies the means and lawful compositions through which such results can be reached. |
| Operative attainment | Its productive organization can actually bring the required construction to an acceptable outcome under the stated premises. |

These levels must connect. Expressing a possible object does not manufacture it; possessing individually useful transformations does not yet organize them into successful production.

### 4.1 A schematic statement of the obligation

For an apparatus \(A\), let:

- \(\mathcal R_A\) be its independently specified space of admitted requirements;
- \(\mathcal P_A\) be its space of possible products;
- \(\operatorname{Sat}_A(r,p)\) mean that product \(p\) satisfies requirement \(r\).

Then the acceptable products for a requirement are:

\[
\operatorname{Sol}_A(r)
=
\{p\in\mathcal P_A\mid \operatorname{Sat}_A(r,p)\}.
\]

For a terminal productive task, a simple functional presentation would describe the realized productive procedure as:

\[
K_A:\mathcal R_A\rightharpoonup\mathcal P_A.
\]

The intended coverage obligation would include:

\[
\operatorname{dom}(K_A)=\mathcal R_A,
\qquad
K_A(r)\in\operatorname{Sol}_A(r)
\quad\text{for every }r\in\mathcal R_A.
\]

Here \(K_A\) stands for a procedure realized by the apparatus, not a function postulated merely because satisfactory products exist. Writing the equations does not supply that procedure or prove its adequacy.

The same apparatus must support the different cases, although it may construct case-specific programs, intermediates and configurations using its established capability. An unexplained external invention for each new case would not establish the stated coverage.

This presentation illustrates terminal tasks with a selected result. Branching, interactive, nondeterministic and continuing productive activity may require relations, histories or other behavioral structures. Their corresponding attainment and progress obligations must be defined according to their nature.

### 4.2 Finding a way is itself part of the capability

An apparatus capable of meeting a class of requirements must have adequate means to develop the work toward them. Depending on the capability, this can involve constructing candidates, discovering missing constituents, producing intermediate programs, comparing alternatives or revising earlier arrangements.

The mere existence of a successful path through a transformation system is weaker than a capacity to find and carry out an appropriate path. Likewise, the existence of one successful strategy is different from a guarantee that every possible strategy succeeds.

The developing theory must specify the appropriate relationship between:

- Admitted requirements.
- Available transformations and constructive means.
- The organization of their selection and composition.
- Progress toward the required outcome.
- The recognition of completion.

Conditions participate in this organization, but conditions alone do not supply all the constructive knowledge necessary to reach a result.

### 4.3 The boundary must be intelligible before success

The admitted space must have an account independent of the claim that its examples happened to work. Its requirements, success meanings and variation are themselves constituted through domain definition; “independent” means independent of retrospective success selection, not outside that constitutive work.

Otherwise “the apparatus can realize anything it can realize” would replace the substantive commitment with a tautology. The purpose is to understand a capability well enough to reason about unfamiliar requirements within its scope.

Examples, counterexamples, implementations and experiments can expose deficiencies in that account. General coverage requires an argument appropriate to the actual mathematical and operative structure.

## 5. Domain, formal universe and constituent worlds

### 5.1 A designed domain and its dimensions

An **abstographic domain is a connected, designed constitution of what can exist, what it means and what constructive work is possible for an intended productive capability**. Its subjects, task families, transformations, constructor responsibilities and resulting structures are developed together. It may draw upon several established disciplines, reorganize parts of them or introduce relationships and intermediate objects that belong to none of them separately.

The intended capability is constitutive, not merely a filter selecting from an already finished domain. The same source knowledge can support different domains because different productive purposes require different individuation, composite subjects, task meanings and constructive arrangements. Their definition must explain both the adopted distinctions and the operations those distinctions make meaningful. No unique domain constitution follows automatically from the purpose alone.

“Arbitrarily defined” means freedom from a predetermined catalogue of domains or ontologies. The general method is not restricted to the domains accommodated by a current implementation. Each particular capability nevertheless has its own definite constitution and reach. Representing a domain and possessing every desired productive power over it remain distinguishable achievements (§4).

The constitution can require several connected dimensions:

| Dimension | What it explains |
| --- | --- |
| Ontological | Kinds of entities, identity, constituents, relationships and dependencies. |
| Semantic | What those structures mean, under which contexts and interpretations, and how their meanings bear on construction. |
| Structural and logical | Admissible formations, applicable laws, exclusions and consequences. |
| Constructive | Task families, substantive transformations, constructor kinds and repertoires, programs, products and means of further construction. |
| Situational | Configurations, changes, absences or unresolved requirements that matter to further work, and their recognition through Conditions. |
| Developmental | How constructions change current states, create subjects of work and affect existing results. |
| Interfacial | How constituents become accessible and usable within worlds, across worlds and through substrate bridges. |
| Realizational | How this organization becomes operative and how its actual effects remain formally represented. |

These are dimensions of an account, not eight compulsory modules. Their intersections may themselves require objects, interfaces and local theories. The intended capability helps determine their necessary depth; the current vocabulary does not set that depth in advance.

### 5.2 One universe can contain heterogeneous worlds

The **formal universe** is the integrated constitution through which the domain's possible and actual objects, worlds, transformations and productive relationships have their formal place.

A **formal world** is a differentiated semantic, ontological and constructive context within that universe. It can have its own object kinds, relations, classifications, interpretations, formation laws and transformations. Its objects need not belong exclusively to it. Its identity as a world comes from its constituted organization, not simply from having a separate collection of records.

Worlds can be complementary, overlapping, differently expressive, or related as alternative scenarios. Different schemas are an initial illustration of different world constitutions; the general notion is not limited to a schema technology or to several populations of one schema. A world may contain requirements, another arrangements, and another operative behavior, while their cross-world relationships constitute what those contributions mean together.

A world's role is not exclusive. The same world may formally constitute a substrate's programs and dependencies alongside requirements, domain objects and generated analysis structures. Several worlds can jointly represent one substrate; one world can participate in the formal account of several substrates. “Domain world” and “substrate-description world” are therefore possible roles, not a mandatory partition.

The universe has two inseparable aspects:

1. **Constitution of possibility:** what worlds, objects, relations, overlaps, tasks and productive arrangements are admitted, what their transformations mean, and how they may compose.
2. **Constitution of actuality:** which particular objects, world participations, relationships, states and productive occurrences exist at a specified stage.

The second develops within the first. A newly formed object need not pre-exist as an instance. Its nature and participation must have an adequate constitution, or that constitution must undergo an explicit supported development. One world can be sufficient for a particular domain; plurality is an available form of organization, not a requirement to multiply worlds without purpose.

### 5.3 Shared identity and differently organized populations

**One formal object can participate in several worlds and remain the same object.** In one world it may enter spatial relationships; in another it may enter functional dependencies or constructive arrangements. Its different participations need not create different object identities.

The account must distinguish:

| Notion | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Object identity | Which object this is, under its constituted identity criteria. |
| World participation | The object's belonging to, or use within, a particular world's organization. |
| World-relative structure | The relations, classifications, interfaces and operations constituted around that object in that world. |
| Representation | An expression or realization through which the object or its participation is presented. |
| Substrate realization | The operative substrate structure related to the formal object through the specified realization account. |

The world context identifies the context of use; it does not automatically create another object. Conversely, related objects are not made identical merely by matching names. Shared identity must belong to the constitution, whether its realization uses shared storage, several presentations or another mechanism. No globally flat identifier format is prescribed here.

For one shared family, let \(P\) denote its population. A simple illustration of different organization over the same population is:

\[
W_1=(P,\mathcal S_1),\qquad W_2=(P,\mathcal S_2).
\]

Here \(\mathcal S_i\) denotes the relationships and interpreted structure contributed by world \(W_i\). This is schematic: it neither makes every world a one-sorted structure nor claims that identity criteria can be separated from all constitutive laws. It shows that **identical object populations need not mean identical worlds**. Both local structures must respect the shared objects' defining commitments while being free to supply different compatible organization.

Two worlds may share their whole population, share only specified families or partially overlap. Additional world-specific objects can coexist with a shared family. Relations can themselves be constituted as objects where appropriate; which families are identical must then be stated with that depth, rather than assuming that identical entity populations also identify all relations or generated objects.

Population sharing can be a maintained law, not merely equality at one moment. If \(P_1(t)=P_2(t)\) is required at every admitted coherent stage \(t\), creation, disappearance and membership change must preserve that law. This does not require identical additional relations, nor does the symbol \(t\) prescribe a physical synchronization architecture.

### 5.4 Common commitments and contextual differences

Shared identity does not make every predicate or relationship globally shared. The constitution must distinguish the following cases:

| Case | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A common fact | Its meaning and interpretation agree wherever that fact is represented. |
| A world-relative relationship | It concerns the object in a particular context without being asserted in every world containing that object. |
| Different presentations of one fact | Their interpreted content agrees under the defined translation or identification. |
| Different claims, alternatives or historical states | Their scope and standing preserve why their contents differ. |

An absent relationship in a world's vocabulary is not automatically a negative assertion about the object. Different classifications can express different criteria. But incompatible assertions about the same common property, in the same respect and state, cannot be made coherent merely by placing them in different worlds.

A change to common content must retain its common meaning across its presentations. A change to a local relation need not copy that relation into other worlds, although its consequences may propagate through defined dependencies. Local constitutions and their overlap laws must therefore be developed together.

The unified universe preserves both identity and legitimate difference. It need not replace all local ontologies with one uniform schema. Some worlds may be alternatives; their differences remain scoped as alternatives rather than being asserted as one undifferentiated actuality.

### 5.5 Joint contexts and coherent states

The universe includes structures through which contributions from several worlds become one subject, input, Condition operand or product. These may involve shared identity, correspondence between distinct objects, dependency, comparison, realization or joint participation. None is supplied merely by the fact that two worlds belong to the same domain.

A coherent universe state includes local states **and their connecting structure**. It is therefore not generally just an unconstrained tuple of world populations. It must make clear which objects are shared, which relations connect distinct objects, and which states or revisions can legitimately be interpreted together.

For later schematic expressions, \(\Omega\) denotes such a relevant coherent configuration. It may concern one world, several overlapping worlds and their connections, or the universe at the required scope. It does not imply that every construction reads all worlds or that the machine keeps one physically centralized snapshot.

These distinctions are productive. A Process can consume different aspects of the same object from several worlds; a Condition can recognize a configuration that no single world's structures alone determine. Their particular constitutions are developed in §§6.6 and 8.2.

### 5.6 Incomplete work and the standing of products

The universe must represent requirements, partial candidates, unresolved alternatives, incomplete structures, failures and attained results. An unfinished design can be a well-constituted object of work while failing the requirement it is being developed to satisfy.

The account distinguishes laws of meaningful formation, requirements of the intended product, statements or proposals, what has been established, and intended, pending or accomplished transformations. These distinctions may themselves require structured objects and relationships.

A product's constructive meaning can include its subject, grounds, assumptions, scope, dependencies and permitted constituent uses. A comparison establishing several admissible alternatives does not thereby supply a unique selected alternative to a consumer that requires one. A useful result may establish obstruction, insufficiency or legitimate underdetermination rather than a completed target design.

Actual occurrence must be represented. That representation preserves what occurred and the standing of its results; it does not automatically establish every claim made by those results. Nor does this distinction impose a universal human approval step. The appropriate grounds and use premises belong to each construction's meaning.

### 5.7 Formal primacy and operative realization

The formal universe is primary in the semantic constitution of the capability. Its local and cross-world laws organize what the productive apparatus is and what its work means. No single constituent world must occupy a privileged position above the others.

This is **semantic primacy, not a requirement that every event originate there**. An action or transformation in an incorporated substrate can initiate a situation. Its bridge gives that occurrence its proper formal constitution; the apparatus can then perform construction within or across the relevant worlds.

This internal work can form or revise objects, relationships, designs, programs and other products. Their substrate realizations follow through the appropriate bridges, into the same substrate or other substrates according to the design. Incorporation of an occurrence and productive construction from it have distinct meanings: representing what happened establishes material for work; the work can construct something further.

The *centre* is the integrated semantic and constructive organization. Its physical distribution remains an architectural question. The formal universe itself must have an operative realization: definitions do not execute independently of the mechanisms that interpret them, maintain their actuality and perform transformations. Complete formal representation does not require duplicating every included structure in every constituent world.

## 6. Abstract Constructors, composition and Abstract Light

### 6.1 Semantic objects are Abstract Constructors

In this account, the semantic objects of the formal universe are **Abstract Constructors**. Their defined nature and organization are constructively consequential: they participate in determining what a suitable receiving capacity produces, which transformations it undertakes, and how further productive work becomes applicable. This includes objects constituted within worlds and the objects or relationships through which worlds participate together.

An opening in a wall, for example, can be constituted through dimensions, placement, orientation and relationships to surrounding structures. Those constituents organize what can be realized from it. The name “opening” alone does not carry the same constructive content.

The object remains an Abstract Constructor while not currently being used. Its constructive nature belongs to its constitution; its particular effect is realized when it participates in an appropriate task and capacity.

These Abstract Constructors supply the substantive meaning of the *abstract* part of Abstography: the method works through their constitution, composition and realization. Their constructive organization is central, rather than incidental information attached to an otherwise independent machine. The name joins that basis with the formative sense of *graphy* and the deliberately chosen lithographic introduction (§1.4).

### 6.2 Composition contributes its own organization

A composite is constituted by its objects **and their connecting structure**.

A wall, an opening and a relation placing that opening in the wall do not have the same constructive meaning as the two object descriptions without that relation. Sharing, position, role, grouping, dependence and relevant ordering can all matter.

Accordingly, a composite's constitution may require:

- Constituent objects and their identities.
- Maps or relations connecting them.
- Indexing and scope.
- Compatibility and dependency laws.
- The distinctions between shared and repeated constituents.
- The interfaces through which parts can participate in further construction.

Where composition is order-sensitive, reversing it can change or invalidate the result. Where an order is merely incidental, it should not acquire artificial semantic importance. These properties must follow from the relevant constructions.

### 6.3 Interfaces belong to constructive meaning

An interface specifies how a constituted object or capacity can be accessed or used, and what that access guarantees.

It may expose an individual constituent, an indexed family, a relation, a transformation or an available part of a larger object. Its mathematical nature need not always be a total single-valued function.

An adequate interface account must explain what is accessible, under which premises, with which identities and scope, and what meaning is preserved for the receiving construction.

This applies both between large stages and between objects generated inside a construction. Internal structure is part of what makes higher construction possible.

### 6.4 Several constructive roles can belong to the same object

An Abstract Constructor can be consequential in several connected ways. These describe contributions to construction, rather than an exhaustive classification into separate kinds of object.

| Constructive role | How the object's constitution participates | What can consequently differ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization of a product | Its constituents, constraints and relationships supply organization to be realized. | The structure, identity and relationships of the resulting product. |
| Constitution or exercise of a transformation repertoire | A program can organize a repertoire; an input composition can select, configure or support the construction of a process within it. | The operations, intermediate structures, compositions and productive routes undertaken. |
| Determination of further work | Its state and relationships participate in a Condition's recognition of a situation and in the apparatus's connection of that situation to appropriate work. | Which work becomes applicable, what it concerns and which constructor or kind of constructor is called upon. |
| Operative realization in another substrate | Its constructive organization is re-expressed in a form that the receiving substrate can realize. | The substrate structures, capacities or transformations constituted through that realization. |

These roles can coincide. A generated configuration might shape the transformations of a receiving constructor, establish the situation that makes another task applicable, and supply organization for a program realized elsewhere.

The account must distinguish **constituting a repertoire** from **determining its exercise in a particular occurrence**. A program can specify a broad repertoire of behavior; a particular composition of input objects can make one path, one combination of paths or one newly constructed case-specific procedure appropriate within that repertoire.

The same Abstract Constructor can participate through different world-specific interfaces without becoming a new object. Its spatial organization, functional dependencies and transformation-related structure can separately or jointly shape a receiving capacity's work. The context and its connecting laws explain the contribution; a world label alone does not.

This is a deeper form of constructive participation than filling values into an otherwise invariant product template. The objects can affect the nature and organization of the work itself.

### 6.5 One declared program can support many population-dependent processes

A **transformation repertoire** is the organized range of transformations and constructive processes a configured capacity supports over its admitted inputs and circumstances. It can include alternative operations, their lawful compositions, and the construction of case-specific productive means.

Consider one fixed program together with a fixed declaration of how its inputs are obtained. The declaration determines what is to be acquired and how it is organized. The relevant coherent configuration of the universe determines the actual objects and compositions supplied. As participating populations, relationships or shared states change, that composition can change while both the input declaration and the program remain unchanged.

For example, a programmed capacity receiving structured material through a fixed acquisition interface may use one admitted composition to form a missing organization, another to revise an existing organization and another to compare alternatives. These different productive courses belong to the repertoire constituted for that responsibility. The example identifies a general relationship between program, supplied objects and construction; it does not prescribe a particular realizing technology.

The program must be constituted for the range it claims to handle: **any admitted population composition supplied through those declared inputs**, rather than only the particular populations encountered during its design. The actual constructive process can depend deeply on that composition. A stable declaration therefore need not imply a fixed sequence of operations or one unchanging intermediate structure.

Schematically, let \(R\) be a fixed input declaration, \(\Omega\) an admitted configuration in the sense of §5.5, \(P\) a fixed program and \(K\) its receiving capacity. Write, where the input's formation and availability premises hold:

\[
i_\Omega=\llbracket R\rrbracket_\Omega,
\qquad
\mathsf{Beh}_{K,P}(i_\Omega)
=
\text{the supported productive behaviors for that supplied composition}.
\]

Here \(\llbracket R\rrbracket_\Omega\) means the interpretation of the input declaration over its specified worlds and connections. \(\mathsf{Beh}_{K,P}(i_\Omega)\) describes the supported transformations or processes, not just their final values. Different supplied compositions can support different such behaviors under the same program. Interpretation can have defined partiality or other result structure; the notation does not turn unavailable input into an ordinary empty result.

The notation leaves deterministic, branching and other behavior open to their proper definitions. Actual performance must satisfy the program's task and preservation obligations; a set of described possible behaviors alone would not establish operative attainment.

The repertoire is exercised through the organization actually supplied by the input interface. The definition must therefore establish that the supplied information is adequate for the distinctions the program is expected to make. A fixed acquisition declaration, its varying results, the program's interpretation of those results and the actual process performed are four connected but distinct parts of the account.

Stateless operation is compatible with this variability: each occurrence can be governed by its current supplied composition without requiring private memory of earlier occurrences. Any relevant history supplied as input is part of that occurrence's information. Other constructor profiles can obtain further observations during their operation or retain specified state; their access and occurrence laws require the additional account in §7.4.

Selecting or constructing a different process within a repertoire is an exercise of capability. Whether a change introduces genuinely new primitive powers is a separate question, addressed by the distinction between use, supported reconfiguration and extension in §12.3.

### 6.6 Joint inputs across worlds

A Process can receive contributions from several worlds, including different constituted aspects of the same object. Its input may concern an arrangement, the requirements governing it and the constructive means available for it. Some contributions can be independent; others are meaningful only through their exact correlation.

An input's constitution must therefore preserve what the responsibility requires: object identity, world-relative roles, shared commitments, connecting relations, available constituents and the state relationships making their joint interpretation valid. Several acquisitions attached to one Process do not by themselves establish a join, a shared variable or an appropriate state relationship.

Where an object \(x\) is shared, a joint input can expose both its \(W_1\)-specific and \(W_2\)-specific structure while retaining that both concern \(x\). Where objects are distinct, their correspondence must instead be stated. Flattening both cases into unrelated records would erase constructive information; treating all related records as identical would invent it.

The composite input can itself be a constituted object with interfaces for further access. Its organization can require several contributions jointly without importing their entire originating worlds. Internal bridges (§10.1) supply any necessary re-expression or construction; direct shared identity need not be replaced by an artificial translation step.

### 6.7 The substantive meaning of a transformation

Input and output types locate a transformation but need not fully explain its productive responsibility. The account must establish the constructive knowledge through which supplied objects can become an appropriate result.

Depending on the responsibility, this can require defining how inputs are interpreted, which principles apply, how simultaneous requirements interact, which alternatives are admissible, what warrants a selection, what must be preserved, and what gives the result its particular meaning and use. A name such as “analyze” or “construct appropriately” does not provide these relationships.

Some transformations can be realized by exact procedures; others may involve search, design, inference or other supported means. Their accounts need not prescribe one fixed sequence for every occurrence. They must nevertheless connect those means to the responsibility's admitted input range, output constitution and attainment obligations.

This is the connection between representing domain knowledge and possessing a capacity to construct through it. A constructor's invocation does not fill a semantic gap left by the domain definition. A newly formed product can, however, supply previously unavailable constructive knowledge within a capability expressly constituted to develop it. The task specifies the required accomplishment; a Process's substantive transformation account explains the constructive responsibility and the means justified for discharging it. These accounts must agree without confusing the task with one chosen route (§7.1).

### 6.8 Abstract Light

**Abstract Light names Abstract Constructors and their compositions considered in their constructive role.**

The light analogy makes their formative participation easier to understand. In the optical comparison, organized light participates in a designed apparatus through which a pattern is realized in a receiving material. In the abstographic account, constituted objects and their connecting structure supply organization through which a suitable receiving capacity forms products, undertakes transformations or prepares further productive work. The operative relationship among that organization, the receiving capacity and its circumstances matters; none of these contributions can be understood adequately in isolation.

This refers to all the constructive roles in §6.4, not only the shape of a final product. An abstract composition can configure a repertoire, determine an input-dependent course of work or constitute a situation that makes another task applicable. What the receiving arrangement realizes can itself be a program or further productive means. Calling this *Light* provides an intelligible analogy for the contribution; the object's semantic constitution and lawful use determine its actual nature.

An Abstract Constructor retains that nature when it is not presently participating in an occurrence (§6.1). It need not be emitted, travel or behave like a physical wave to be constructively significant. Internal activity in a receiving apparatus can realize its organization; that activity is distinct from the organization being realized. The exact effects and preservation laws belong to the defined objects, interfaces and capacities, not to an assumed importation of optical laws.

Distinctions such as *semantic light* or *vector light* may become useful for different constructive modes. Their classification remains to be developed: it could concern the objects, their representations, the receiving capacities or the transformations involved.

The constructive meaning is established here; a taxonomy of light types is not yet established.

## 7. Tasks, constructor kinds and the constitution of possible work

The domain must constitute its possible work with the same depth as its possible objects. A transformation needs meaningful subjects, admissible circumstances and an account of what it accomplishes; its realization needs appropriate constructive powers. These develop together. A task can expose a missing subject or capacity, while a newly constituted object or capacity can open a further family of tasks.

| Role | Positive definition | Contribution to the apparatus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task | A constituted accomplishment or continuing obligation, with admitted starting circumstances, permissible outcomes or behaviors, and preservation requirements. | Determines what a constructive occurrence must achieve, without identifying that achievement with one particular procedure. |
| Constructor | An entity or organized capacity able to perform a relevant task while retaining the capacity for further appropriate performances, subject to its operating premises. | Makes a class of transformations repeatably available. |
| Constructor kind | A specified form of constructive capacity, distinguished by consequential laws of configuration, observation, intervention, state or result formation. | Identifies powers and interaction forms that a domain responsibility can require. |
| Program | Constituted organization directing or configuring a constructive capacity, or organizing several capacities into a productive process. It may govern a repertoire of input-dependent transformations rather than one fixed operation sequence. | Gives operative structure to how work is performed across its admitted input compositions. |
| Substrate | That in or upon which a transformation is realized and whose relevant states participate in that transformation. | Supplies the medium and state-bearing objects of realization. |
| Constructive substrate arrangement | A substrate together with capacities and operating organization that can derive or transform structures within it. | Contributes productive power to the integrated capability. |
| Surface | A defined means of expressing, presenting or manipulating structures in a particular setting. | Makes particular representations available for construction or access. |
| Interface | A specified relation of access or interaction, with premises and preservation obligations. | Makes lawful composition and constituent use possible. |
| Bridge | A designed integration making the required passages and constructive relationships operative between differently organized contexts or realizations. | Can support internal world-to-world construction, joint use of heterogeneous contributions, or formal/substrate realization and incorporation, with specified preservation and change laws (§10). |

A code text becomes productive through a suitable execution arrangement. The text, the execution capacity, the substrate state and the resulting transformation perform different roles, even where they belong to one system.

These roles can overlap without becoming interchangeable. A program can be a product and an Abstract Constructor; a product can become a substrate for another task; a constructor can itself be constituted from other constructors.

Constructor Theory is deliberately used here as an organizing framework: tasks distinguish the required accomplishments, constructors the repeatable powers to achieve them, substrates what undergoes transformation, and programs how suitable capacities can be configured. Composition then asks how these contributions make further work possible. These are accessible questions about productive organization, with substantive distinctions that the more detailed account must preserve.

The framework also turns attention from what happened on one occasion toward what a capacity can accomplish across its admitted cases. That is central to the difference between an individual product and a productive capability (§4). Its compact conceptual language supports understanding without making prior mastery of the eventual mathematical machinery a requirement. The following sections deepen that language where Abstography needs task formation, heterogeneous powers and recursive construction.

The exact mathematical relationship between Constructor Theory and our use of *Abstract Constructor* remains work to develop. The framework does not by itself establish our attainment claim or prescribe one universal internal architecture. Its chosen distinctions guide the account; the abstographic constructions, their laws and their realizations require the corresponding definitions and grounds.

### 7.1 Tasks are constituted accomplishments, not names for procedures

A task's definition must make the requested transformation intelligible independently of the identity of the means chosen to perform it. It identifies its subjects and circumstances, the results or behavior that count as accomplishment, the changes permitted, and what must be preserved. Necessary auxiliary inputs, consequential effects and relevant resources belong in that account where they affect its meaning or feasibility.

For a terminal task \(\tau\), a schematic presentation is

\[
T_\tau\subseteq I_\tau\times O_\tau,
\]

where \(I_\tau\) is the admitted input-configuration space and \(O_\tau\) the result-configuration space. Membership \((i,o)\in T_\tau\) means that \(o\) constitutes an acceptable accomplishment from \(i\), including the preservation and effect requirements represented in those configurations. These can be local or cross-world structures, not merely isolated values. The substantive definition lies in the relation, not just the names of its source and target.

Several results may be legitimate for one input. Several different procedures may realize the same task. Conversely, one programmed capacity may handle a family of different task instances. A representation that requires only a final result is insufficient for a task whose permitted intermediate activity matters: such a task needs an account of admissible histories, observations or ongoing behavior as appropriate.

A **Process** supplies a constituted responsibility for a family of such tasks, together with its input organization, transformation principles, product constitution and intended uses. A program or constructive arrangement supplies means of performing that work. A productive occurrence is an actual exercise of those means. The task's required effect, the Process's responsibility, the program's organization and the occurrence's history must remain connected but distinguishable. No one-to-one correspondence among them is imposed.

Constituting a task can require constituting new objects. A comparison needs a subject, alternatives and a comparison basis; a refinement needs the structure to refine and the properties it must retain. Where these subjects are absent, naming the task has not made it available. Likewise, a prerequisite requires a justified formation path rather than assuming the task's own future achievement already exists. These are reciprocal obligations of domain constitution, not incidental execution details.

The task family is defined before its particular future occurrences. Its formation and binding laws determine which new subjects and requirements can instantiate it. Those laws can themselves support the construction of further task specifications; that is a substantive productive power to define, not permission for unexplained work outside the established capability.

### 7.2 Constructor kinds express different forms of productive power

Constructors can differ in how they are configured, what information becomes available to them, what transformations they can undertake, how their work continues and which effects they can produce. A kind is useful when it captures such consequential differences through stated laws, rather than merely naming a technology.

Possible forms include capacities that transform a supplied input into a bounded result, capacities that carry out constituted operations over structured states, and capacities that alternate observation with intervention while developing their course of work. These are illustrative profiles along several axes, not three disjoint or exhaustive classes. The same capacity can combine features, and a composite arrangement can introduce further forms.

| Dimension of a constructor's constitution | What must be explained where consequential |
| --- | --- |
| Configuration | What is fixed by its constitution, what a program may determine and which changes constitute a new configuration. |
| Observation | Whether information is supplied initially, obtained during work or maintained through defined state, and how its meaning is preserved. |
| Intervention | Which objects, relations, states or productive means can be created, modified, removed or invoked through its available operations. |
| Continuation | How subsequent work can depend on inputs, observations, results and retained state, including any required interaction or termination behavior. |
| Result formation | What it produces, with which constituents, relationships, standing and interfaces for use. |
| Continuing capacity | Which premises permit further appropriate performances, including supporting means and their replenishment where required. |

These dimensions are questions of constructive meaning, not mandatory fields in a software record. Their appropriate mathematical forms can differ. A capacity restricted to a supplied input and result surface has a different operative scope from one allowed to acquire further observations and intervene during an occurrence, even if they share a realizing mechanism.

Constructor kind and domain task are independent distinctions that must be related. One kind can support many responsibilities; one responsibility can require cooperation among kinds. Identifying a kind does not establish adequacy for a task: its configured repertoire, inputs, available operations and actual realization must support the required accomplishment.

### 7.3 Repertoire, configuration, process and occurrence

A constructor's **repertoire** is the class of tasks it is capable of performing under specified configuration and operating premises. For a programmable constructor, the account must distinguish the tasks reachable through its admitted programs from the repertoire of a particular configuration. A specified or intended repertoire is a claim to establish, not evidence of capability merely because it is declared.

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Programmable repertoire | The tasks accessible through the constructor's admitted configurations and supporting means under the stated premises. |
| Configured repertoire | The tasks and constructive behaviors supported by a particular program and arrangement. |
| Input-dependent process | The course of work selected or constructed for the supplied objects and circumstances within that repertoire. |
| Productive occurrence | The actual observations, interventions, intermediate formations and results of one exercise. |

For non-programmable capacities, the first distinction can collapse into their fixed constitution. For composite capacities, configuration can include the organization of several constructors and their passages. Any comparison of repertoires must keep their operating premises explicit: supplying a new capacity is different from selecting a program under unchanged means.

The fixed-program variability of §6.5 belongs within this larger account. A program may support many processes without prescribing each occurrence, while a higher constructive capacity may form specialized programs or arrangements as products (§12). Changing an input, changing a program and extending primitive powers are different developments.

Domain definition must give every included constructive responsibility an adequate account at the relevant levels. A borrowed realizing capacity can be used, but it does not supply missing task semantics simply by being available. The domain's objects, task meanings, configuration and result obligations must establish why and how that capacity participates.

### 7.4 Access and effects over variable configurations

A constructor's productive scope includes what it can receive or observe and what it can cause through its admitted operations. These are designed relationships to its subjects, resources and supporting means. They can be declared over kinds, bindings and structural relations before particular populations exist.

For a configured constructor \(K\), coherent configuration \(\Omega\) and admitted subject binding \(b\), write schematically

\[
\operatorname{Obs}_K(\Omega,b),\qquad
\operatorname{Act}_K(\Omega,b)
\]

for its defined observation opportunities and admissible interventions in that context. Their exact mathematical forms remain to be supplied; the notation need not describe finite lists or globally total functions. The standing laws can remain fixed while their actual subjects, available observations and possible interventions change with \(\Omega\).

**Information adequacy** asks whether the constructor can obtain the distinctions its task requires. **Operative scope** asks which acts belong to its constituted activity. Having a needed fact does not supply a missing power to act; possessing an operation does not supply the information required to use it meaningfully. Both must connect to the task and its realization.

Some constructors receive their complete occurrence input through a prescribed interface. Others can observe, act, receive changed circumstances and choose further work under a defined program. For the latter, the intermediate actions and observations belong to the productive account, including consequential effects before any final result. An end-state comparison alone cannot establish fidelity to an occurrence whose allowed course matters.

Where state persists between occurrences, its identity, lifetime and effect on further capability require definition. Where it does not, relevant history can instead be supplied as constituted input. Neither arrangement is universal. Access to supporting means, the distinction between observing and modifying, and any ability to configure or invoke another constructor must follow the particular constitution.

### 7.5 Constructor kind, world participation and substrate realization

Different kinds of constructor can operate in the productive organization of the same formal universe. Their subjects can lie in one world, in several overlapping worlds or in constituted joint configurations. Kind does not determine location: an interpretive, operational or interactive mode of construction need not belong exclusively to one side of a substrate bridge.

The formal universe includes the account of the constructors, programs, arrangements and occurrences participating in its capability. Through their operative realizations, those constructors perform work upon its objects and can prepare work for capacities in other substrates. The formal world is productive through this realized organization; a declaration of transformation does not execute independently of its realizing means.

A world can organize material required by several constructors without itself being another constructor. An arrangement involving a world and its productive means may, in turn, have the coherent repeatable capacity required to be treated as a composite constructor. That status requires its own task and realization account; it is not conferred by calling a collection a world.

Formal identity, constructor kind, domain responsibility and substrate realization therefore provide different coordinates of the same account. The same program object may participate in several worlds, and a responsibility may have distinct realizations. Their identities and correspondence laws must explain those relationships rather than infer them from similar names.

### 7.6 Heterogeneous composition has its own constitution

Composing constructors requires defining the relationship through which their powers jointly become useful. One constructor can supply material, constitute a task, configure a capacity or contribute to building a further constructor. The distinct productive achievements involved are developed in §12.2.

For any intended composition, the account must establish:

- What the contributing products and interfaces constitute, including their identities, bindings, grounds and availability.
- What the receiving constructor requires and which transformation that supplied organization supports.
- Which observations, interventions and supporting means belong to the resulting activity.
- Which state, preservation, ordering and resource premises must hold jointly.
- How intermediate and resulting effects retain their formal place and their intended uses.

Compatible input and output types are one part of this account. They do not alone establish adequate information, preserved standing, lawful effects or repeatable productive capacity. A proposal for an action, a program specifying it, an operative means of performing it and its actual performance are different constituents even if a presentation uses similar symbols for them.

Composition can establish productive reach beyond that of any isolated constituent. This reach belongs to the jointly constituted arrangement; it must not be inferred by adding individual claims or by silently enlarging one participant's scope. Conversely, failure of one decomposition need not exclude the task if another lawful composition realizes its required effect.

A composite that supplies a defined repeatable capacity under its premises can itself be treated as a constructor in further compositions. Its internal kinds and organization remain relevant wherever their effects, dependencies or constituent interfaces matter. The whole does not have to erase its structure to become a productive unit.

## 8. Condition-governed recursive construction

### 8.1 Conditions discriminate meaningful situations

A Condition constitutes a way of recognizing a specified situation within the productive universe. Its scope can be local to one world or concern a joint configuration across worlds.

The relevant situation may concern an available object, a relationship, a change, an unresolved requirement, the state of a construction, or a structured combination of these. Recognizing it can make a particular productive continuation applicable.

An object's constructive contribution here lies in its participation in that situation. Its nature, state and relationships can be decisive for what work should happen next and what that work should concern. The Condition and the associated productive organization give this contribution its operative consequence, including the selection of a constructor or kind of constructor.

The account of a Condition can therefore require more than a final truth value. It may require the objects supporting that judgment, their identities and bindings, the relevant current or reference states, and the constituents that subsequent work needs.

No particular current catalogue of Condition types determines the general definition of Abstography. The necessary forms follow from the productive capability being constituted.

### 8.2 Cross-world Conditions and their generated constituents

A Condition may depend on objects from several worlds, on different aspects of a shared object, or on relationships constituted between distinct objects. It need not reduce to a conjunction of independently evaluated local truths.

For illustration, let \(A\) be an assembly family in one world, \(B\) an analysis family in another, and \(R\subseteq A\times B\) the constituted relation identifying which analysis concerns which assembly. At an appropriate coherent configuration, a Condition could have the meaning:

\[
C(a)\iff\operatorname{Ready}(a)\land
\exists b\in B\;\bigl(R(a,b)\land
\operatorname{Meets}(b,\operatorname{Requirement}(a))\bigr).
\]

The predicates and relation are illustrative; their definitions and availability premises belong to the chosen domain. The formula's point is correlation: a satisfactory analysis of another assembly does not satisfy this construction. Changing \(R\) can change the result without changing either object's local payload.

When the same object \(x\) participates in several worlds, a Condition can instead combine their respective aspects of \(x\) directly, under the shared-identity and state laws. No artificial second object is required. Where a shared family is counted, object identity must distinguish counting its members from counting their world participations.

A construction may constitute supporting configurations rather than only a truth value. In the example, consider:

\[
S=\{(a,b)\in A\times B\mid
\operatorname{Ready}(a)\land R(a,b)\land
\operatorname{Meets}(b,\operatorname{Requirement}(a))\}.
\]

The family \(S\), its projection \(\pi_A:S\to A\), its selected assembly family \(\pi_A(S)\), and its fibers \(S_a=\{s\in S\mid\pi_A(s)=a\}\) provide different constituents. Counting \(S\) differs from counting \(\pi_A(S)\) when several analyses support one assembly. A later Condition can consume a specified constituent while preserving its identity, scope, availability and supporting relationships.

This illustrates how Conditions over Conditions can retain cross-world structure through interfaces. Their declarations specify admissible operands and meanings before future populations exist; later interpretations supply the particular families and results. An unavailable construction, a defined empty family and a defined false requirement must retain their respective meanings wherever that distinction affects a consumer.

Input constructions and Conditions have their own semantic roles. Both can use the same local or cross-world foundations without one theory being defined through the other. These general possibilities are not claims that a current database-level implementation already realizes them.

### 8.3 Recognition connects to construction

The operative organization must connect a recognized situation with appropriate constructive work. That includes constituting the particular task or task binding, supplying its inputs, making a suitable configured capacity available and giving the resulting changes their proper place within and between worlds. Suitability concerns its repertoire, operative scope and current premises, not merely a matching constructor-kind name.

There are two connected levels of determination. The recognized situation can make a constructor's invocation applicable; the composition supplied to that constructor can then determine the process it performs within its repertoire. One need not change the selected constructor or rewrite its program for the second level to vary. Defining their relationship does not identify the Condition with the input declaration: they have their own respective meanings and roles.

A simplified occurrence can be indicated as:

\[
\Omega
\;\longrightarrow\;
I
\;\xrightarrow{K}\;
O
\;\longrightarrow\;
\Omega'.
\]

Here:

- \(\Omega\) is the relevant coherent configuration, including any participating worlds and their connecting structure.
- \(I\) is the organized input made available for a particular task.
- \(K\) is the configured constructive capacity.
- \(O\) comprises the resulting objects or changes.
- \(\Omega'\) is the configuration with those results coherently incorporated.

The first passage depends on the applicable situation and task; the last depends on the nature of the result and its realization. The diagram illustrates a segment of productive activity, not a mandatory centralized or sequential execution architecture. For an interactive constructor, the central passage can contain successive observations and interventions whose effects require incorporation along the way. The complete occurrence must retain that structure where it matters rather than treating every constructor as a one-shot transformation.

The situation represented in \(\Omega\) may itself originate in a substrate: an actual occurrence is incorporated, becomes available to Conditions and constructors, and occasions construction inside the formal universe. The resulting products can subsequently be realized in that same substrate, another substrate or several substrates, according to the design. A diagram beginning with a formal configuration does not make the formal universe the original cause of the situation it represents.

### 8.4 Recursion develops the field of constructive work

Once productive results or substrate-originating occurrences are incorporated, the universe's actual organization has changed. It can contain new objects, new relationships, new evidence, revised candidates or newly constructed productive means. These can make further Conditions applicable and enable further tasks within or across worlds.

Recursion therefore concerns the development of constructive circumstances, not simply repetition of one fixed operation. Several results can jointly enable another construction; one result can participate in several subsequent constructions.

Construction can also create what further work is about. Forming an arrangement can constitute its interfaces and thereby instantiate compatibility questions that did not exist as particular subjects beforehand. Products can generate dependencies, comparisons, obstructions or opportunities rather than merely advance a pre-enumerated task list.

The standing possibility space, current objects and current field of constructive requirements are therefore distinct. A newly instantiated question can belong to a previously constituted capability; its novelty need not extend that capability's boundary. The laws forming its subject, task, means and possible results give it that place even when the designers did not individually foresee the occurrence. This is part of how the apparatus can find its way through unfamiliar admitted requirements.

The causal force comes from the operative apparatus realizing these relationships. Formal Conditions specify meaningful applicability; the configured machinery turns applicable situations into actual productive activity.

Every included occurrence, change and result must receive its formal place. Whether it should trigger further work depends on the defined productive organization. Recursion can therefore weave together internally organized construction and independently initiated substrate activity.

### 8.5 Revision and progress

Recursive work can add, remove, replace, reorganize or invalidate structures. A useful development may reduce the number of objects by resolving alternatives or eliminating an unsuitable candidate.

Progress must therefore be understood relative to the task and the constructive state, rather than measured by accumulation alone.

Likewise, optimization requires an account of what is being improved, what is preserved and how competing criteria relate. Recursion creates the possibility of repeated refinement; it does not by itself prove convergence, optimality or completion.

## 9. Constructive substrates participate in the formal universe

An incorporated substrate contributes more than a destination for exported artifacts. Its productive organization participates in the abstographic capability.

That includes the objects and relationships through which it works, its relevant capacities, the transformations it performs and the actual structures it derives.

### 9.1 Operational difference, semantic integration

A substrate can have its own representations, execution mechanisms and internal organization. Those differences enable it to contribute a distinct constructive capacity.

Its inclusion in the abstographic apparatus means that this capacity and its exercise receive a semantic and ontological account in the formal universe. The substrate remains an operative realization with its own mechanisms; its formal account participates in the domain's integrated constructive organization. A substrate may support several constructor kinds, and one kind may have different substrate realizations. Domain definition establishes the required task meanings and constructive roles; the realization account must establish which of those powers the incorporated substrate actually supplies.

The substrate can also originate activity through its own processes, interactions among its objects or other participants. Its contribution need not be commissioned by an earlier instruction from the formal universe. Incorporation establishes how that activity belongs to the shared productive organization; it does not imply exclusive operational control of the substrate.

The capability boundary must state what has been incorporated. Once incorporated, those parts cannot be treated as an opaque extension whose internal productive objects and changes have no formal counterpart.

### 9.2 Full representation within the incorporated scope

**The formal universe must have a complete representation of what exists and happens within the incorporated productive scope, at the semantic and ontological depth that scope requires.** This is an obligation of the integrated universe; it does not require a complete duplicate in every constituent world.

This includes the substrate's productive constitution, its instantiated objects and relationships, its actions and occurrences, its intermediate constructions, its resulting structures and changes to their actual state.

The obligation applies to included actuality whether it was requested by the apparatus or originated elsewhere in the substrate. An unexpected change, an unsuccessful action or an effect conflicting with an intended result must have its appropriate formal meaning and standing. Actual occurrence, intended occurrence and satisfactory outcome remain distinct.

Completeness is not achieved merely by storing input and output files. Nor is it sufficient to represent only the properties a current consumer happens to request. The represented whole must support the identity and meaning of the incorporated productive organization itself.

The exact scope and depth require explicit definition. They should establish what belongs to the integrated capability and how it is constituted. They should not quietly weaken the commitment into “whatever information is convenient to copy.”

A computational substrate's incorporation does not automatically make every physical microstate of its host a domain object. If physical states become relevant objects of the capability, their incorporation introduces additional representational and productive obligations.

### 9.3 Worlds representing substrates can also contain other domain structures

A world's constitution can include a substrate's object kinds, dependencies or transformations and also the requirements, products and domain relationships through which those substrate structures become constructively meaningful. Its purpose need not be an isolated mirror of the substrate.

For example, the formal object representing a particular program may participate in one world through its body and dependencies, and in another through the productive responsibilities it realizes. It can be the same formal program object in both worlds, with different surrounding structure. Its relationship to an actual executing substrate object is a further realization relationship (§10.4).

Several worlds can jointly supply the formal account of one substrate. Their overlaps must preserve common identity and content, while their distinct contributions constitute the required whole. Distributed representation is adequate only when that joint account is complete at the incorporated scope; scattering partial descriptions without their connections does not satisfy the obligation.

A substrate-originating change can therefore affect several worlds and their shared structure. The relevant incorporation must preserve what changed, where its formal consequences belong, and which dependencies become affected. It need not copy every local relation into every participating world.

### 9.4 Representation of the apparatus itself

Programs, constructors, bridges and other incorporated productive arrangements have their own formal constitution and representation. Their kinds, configurations, available means, intended repertoires and actual occurrences require their appropriate distinctions and relationships. They belong to what the universe must account for, alongside the objects upon which they operate.

This gives the apparatus a basis for reasoning about or constructing parts of its productive means. The represented arrangement and the machinery realizing its representation need not be identical entities or execute twice.

How far the apparatus can use those representations to reason about or transform itself, and which foundations make those operations coherent, remain important questions. Mandatory representation establishes a basis for such activity; the supported reflective capacities must be defined in their own right.

## 10. Bridges: internal construction, substrate passages and continuing coherence

**A bridge is a designed integration through which differently organized contexts or realizations participate in the required constructive passages. It comprises the relevant identity or correspondence relationships, interfaces, transformations, circumstances of applicability, and laws preserving the meaning and actuality involved.**

This definition includes the operative means of crossing as well as the relationship between representations. A bridge may itself contain several constructors, programs, interfaces and Conditions. Its particular assembly remains to be defined according to the spaces it connects.

An **internal bridge** supports required re-expression, transformation or joint construction between formal worlds. A **substrate bridge** connects formal constitution with another operative realization. These are roles of bridges, not necessarily disjoint physical components. Directly shared identity is already a relationship between worlds; it need not be manufactured by translating an object into another object.

Substrate passages have their own constructive meanings. **Substrate-to-formal incorporation** gives substrate actuality its constituted place in the formal universe. **Construction inside the formal universe** can then form or transform objects within or across worlds. **Formal-to-substrate realization** gives a product its intended operative form in the same substrate or other substrates according to the design. These distinctions make internal work explicit while permitting an episode to begin on either side.

The word *return* describes the history of a particular episode. Results can return to the formal universe after a construction is realized elsewhere; a product constructed within it can also return to the substrate whose activity initiated the work. The directional definitions impose neither sequence. An internal bridge need not support every reverse transformation; each admitted direction has its own obligations.

| Aspect of a bridge | What it must establish |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutive relationship | Which objects, relationships, capacities and states are shared or correspond, and whether the relationship is identity, interpretation, realization, refinement or another defined relation. |
| Receiving constitution | How the required constructive organization becomes meaningful and usable in the receiving world or substrate. |
| Operative powers | Which receiving tasks, configurations, observations and effects the passage realizes, and which premises or limits their use retains. |
| Transformations within passage | What is re-expressed, assembled, decomposed, derived or otherwise changed by the bridge itself, and why the result has the intended meaning. |
| Applicability and activation | Which situations make a passage appropriate, what information and resources it requires, and how the corresponding work is invoked. |
| Results and their formal place | What the passage constitutes, which identities and supporting relationships it retains, and how any new actuality acquires its appropriate formal place. |
| Continuing coherence | How the correspondence remains meaningful as both sides develop, including modification, replacement, deletion and failure. |

These aspects must be designed together. An object is bridgeable within a particular capability when the whole admitted passage—including its input composition, transformations, applicability and resulting use—has an adequate realization and corresponding formal meaning.

**Matching “in all possible ways” means covering every constructive dimension required by the admitted bridge capability, across its admitted cases and compositions.** This includes object constitution and relationships, transformations and their premises, activation circumstances, new results and subsequent changes. The match is between the intended meanings and effects; different internal representations or mechanisms can realize that match.

The bridge's declared domain must express the capability genuinely being claimed. Restricting it after a failure merely to the cases that happened to pass would not establish coverage. Its exact mathematical and operational realization remains work to develop.

### 10.1 Internal bridges and their composition

An internal passage can interpret one world's structures in another, construct a refinement, make heterogeneous contributions jointly usable or form a new cross-world product. These relationships need not be equivalences. A requirements world and a geometric world can contribute to one construction without expressing the same theory.

Shared objects retain their identity through the passage where that identity is part of its constitution. Different local presentations may require re-expression; distinct objects may instead require a correspondence. The account must state which case holds, what connecting structure is consumed, and what the receiving construction obtains.

For a joint Process or Condition, an internal bridge can provide the integration needed to use several worlds together. The result need not be a flattened replacement ontology. It can retain local structure and expose a constituted joint interface, including the shared identities and correlations required by the consumer.

Composition is itself substantive. A passage from \(W_1\) through \(W_2\) to \(W_3\) must preserve what its full intended use requires, not merely what each stage happens to output. Two routes must agree where a shared-identity or preservation law requires agreement; otherwise they may express genuinely different transformations. Meaningful order must not be erased, and incidental order must not become an invented requirement.

The existence of separate adequate passages does not prove that arbitrary chains, cycles or joint combinations are adequate. Their domains, availability, retained constituents and coherence require their own laws. Mathematical directions for such composition and assembly appear in §15.

### 10.2 Formal-to-substrate realization

A representation can change while the constructive organization relevant to the task is preserved.

To “clean an object of its formal representation” means to replace its particular expressive carrier with an expression appropriate to the receiving substrate, while carrying the defining constructive organization through the bridge. The formal object and its full account remain in the universe, potentially through several world participations. Its outward realization can become operative code, an executable arrangement or another substrate-specific constructive form.

For a formal composition \(\Gamma\), one passage can be written schematically as:

\[
K\bigl[\rho_K(\Gamma)\bigr]\leadsto P.
\]

Here \(\rho_K\) presents the relevant organization for receiving capacity \(K\), which produces \(P\). The applicable objects and premises determine where this passage is defined.

Adequacy concerns the actual structure required by the task: identities, constituents, relationships, constraints, dependencies and meaningful ordering. It also concerns the productive role of what crosses. An operand supplied to an existing constructor, a configuration determining its behavior and a newly realized constructive arrangement have different obligations (§12.2). A syntactic resemblance alone establishes little about that adequacy.

The required preservation must be explicit. An interface may intentionally expose part of a larger object, while the whole incorporated object retains its full formal representation across the appropriate worlds and their connections. Such a partial interface is adequate only for tasks whose required organization it supplies; it cannot replace a promised full-object realization.

### 10.3 Substrate-to-formal incorporation can initiate productive work

A substrate can originate an action, interaction, object or change that was not previously instantiated in the formal universe. Its processes or participants can initiate this occurrence independently of any prior instruction from the abstographic capability.

The bridge constitutes what happened through the appropriate formal objects and relationships. An occurrence, the objects affected and their resulting states may require distinct but connected representations. The result is meaningful constructive material: Conditions can recognize the represented situation, and constructors can use its constituents in subsequent work.

Incorporation can introduce new identities, relationships, intermediate products or established properties. It is not generally the inverse of an earlier formal-to-substrate passage, and such an earlier passage need not exist.

The possible kinds and constructive relationships must have an adequate place in the formal constitution. The particular result may be genuinely new. If an included substrate produces a structure for which the universe has no adequate representation, that exposes a deficiency or a required extension of the incorporated capability.

This direction has its own complete construction. It must determine what substrate actuality is being incorporated, perform the necessary representational transformations, establish or recover its identities and relationships, and make it available with the correct meaning in the appropriate worlds. Encountering an already shared object through another substrate passage must not silently create a new identity. Any Conditions or other applicability arrangements governing that passage belong to its definition.

Work performed inside the formal universe from that incorporated situation can produce a genuinely new product, including through joint cross-world construction. The design determines how that product is then realized in the originating substrate or in other substrates. The relationship is between an initiating occurrence and the construction it occasions, not necessarily between an exported object and its modified copy.

### 10.4 Identity and equivalence across realizations

Correspondence need not be a literal one-object-to-one-object copy.

Shared formal identity across worlds (§5.3) is distinct from the relationship between a formal object and its operative substrate realization. Neither relationship should be substituted for the other. A single formal program object can participate in several worlds while having several distinct execution occurrences, each requiring its own appropriate formal account.

One domain object may have several realization objects in a substrate. Each incorporated substrate object must itself have an adequate formal counterpart, together with the relationship expressing how it realizes the domain object.

Conversely, a substrate structure can jointly realize several formal constituents.

The theory must distinguish:

- Identity of a formal object.
- Identity of a substrate object or occurrence.
- A representation of an object.
- Equivalence for a specified task.
- Preservation of a specified semantic structure.

Two programs can satisfy the same task while remaining distinct programs with different bodies, dependencies or intermediate behavior. Their task equivalence does not erase those distinctions.

Where a bridge realizes productive means, the correspondence must include the observations and effects attributed to those means. Preserving a description while changing its operative scope would change the capability. Intended limits, available powers and actual effects must have their respective formal meanings; recording an unexpected effect does not retrospectively make it an admitted one.

### 10.5 State correspondence must survive change

Let \(\Omega\) be a relevant coherent formal configuration (§5.5), \(s\) an incorporated substrate state, and \(C(\Omega,s)\) their specified correspondence. The formal side can involve several worlds and their shared structures; it need not be one database population.

For a substrate transformation

\[
s\xrightarrow{\tau}s',
\]

whether initiated within that substrate or through an earlier formal construction, the integrated account must provide a corresponding formal development

\[
\Omega\xRightarrow{\widehat{\tau}}\Omega'.
\]

For every included substrate transition from corresponding states, the obligation can be expressed as:

\[
C(\Omega,s)\;\land\;(s\xrightarrow{\tau}s')
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists \Omega',\widehat{\tau}\;
\bigl((\Omega\xRightarrow{\widehat{\tau}}\Omega')\land C(\Omega',s')\bigr).
\]

The bridge must realize this correspondence through actual incorporation. Its laws must also relate the content of the two transformations, including their objects, identities, relationships and effects. The endpoint equation alone does not specify that content.

This correspondence explains how a substrate change gains its formal place. The further constructive work it may occasion inside or across formal worlds is an additional productive development, with its own meaning and resulting products. Those products can then be realized through the designated substrate bridges.

Correspondence must account for actual creation, modification, disappearance and failure, as well as the distinction between an intended transformation and one that occurred.

The requirement is that no incorporated actuality lacks its proper formal representation. This includes the intermediate states and changes belonging to the incorporated scope, not only a final snapshot. Its precise temporal meaning—how observations, execution boundaries and intermediate states remain coherent—must be defined rather than hidden inside the word “instantaneous.” This document does not select a synchronization mechanism or claim physically zero-latency communication.

Where several substrates share objects or dependencies, their correspondences must agree on that shared structure. Separately adequate views do not automatically establish one coherent whole.

The same requirement applies to internal world sharing. Common facts and declared population-sharing laws must remain coherent; world-specific relations retain their own scopes while their defined consequences propagate. This is a meaning-preservation obligation, not a requirement to copy every local change everywhere.

### 10.6 Whole substrate-passage compatibility in both directions

The bridge must connect applicability, transformation and result in each direction. Their agreement is an obligation of the complete passage, not a conclusion obtained from testing an isolated conversion.

| Dimension | Formal universe toward substrate | Substrate toward formal universe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objects and compositions | Every admitted formal input has the substrate constitution required for its intended realization. | Every included substrate object, occurrence or composition has an adequate formal constitution, including independently initiated activity and newly derived structures. |
| Relationships and identity | Roles, sharing, dependencies and relevant identity distinctions survive the realization with their specified meaning. | Their actual substrate counterparts are incorporated without confusing new objects, shared objects or changed relationships. |
| Applicability and invocation | The recognized situation is connected to an appropriate receiving capacity under explicit premises. | The circumstances requiring incorporation are connected to the appropriate formal-constitution process, whether or not a prior formal instruction exists. |
| Bridge transformations | Re-expression and any further derivation are defined operations whose effects match the intended passage. | Reconstruction or interpretation of substrate structure preserves its actual meaning and records the effects of the incorporation transformation. |
| Constructor capabilities | Configured means have the required repertoire and operative scope; a described means is distinguished from its operative realization. | Actual capacities, configurations, observations and interventions receive their formal account, including relevant intermediate effects. |
| Result and constructive use | The realized structure supports the substrate activity for which it was produced. | The incorporated structure supports the formal access and further work for which its constituents are required. |
| Continuing change | Subsequent formal changes can be realized through the supported passages with their dependencies intact. | Actual substrate changes remain represented, including intermediates, replacement and disappearance. |

One possible episode begins with a formal object helping satisfy a Condition that calls for a program to be realized in a substrate. The bridge establishes the operative arrangement and incorporates the structures it subsequently derives.

Another begins with an independently initiated substrate occurrence. Its incorporation makes a new situation available inside the formal universe. Construction within or across its worlds forms a product, which is then realized in the same substrate or other substrates according to the design. In both episodes, object constitution, applicability, transformation and effect must match throughout the complete passage. The directions themselves impose no fixed causal starting point.

The triggering arrangements on the two sides can differ. Their job is to make the intended corresponding passages happen under the right circumstances, not to duplicate identical Condition objects in every substrate. Likewise, complete correspondence permits new results and many-to-one or one-to-many representations; it need not take the form of inverse copying.

Mandatory incorporation and further productive activation retain their distinct meanings: every included occurrence, change and result receives its formal place, while subsequent work occurs according to the situations and continuations defined for it. This distinction must survive the bridge's design.

### 10.7 One constructive centre among multiple worlds and substrates

An abstographic capability can integrate several substrates through their respective bridges. Its formal universe supplies the coherent organization of shared identities, differentiated worlds and cross-world relationships through which their contributions can enter productive construction.

A situation originating in one substrate can be combined inside the formal universe with structures already represented from others. Conditions and constructors can act on that joint configuration to form further objects, relationships, designs or productive means. The resulting products are then realized in the originating substrate, a different substrate or several substrates, according to the design.

The defining sequence for such an episode is:

1. **Substrate-originating actuality:** something occurs or changes in an incorporated substrate.
2. **Formal incorporation:** that actuality receives its proper objects, relationships and meaning in the appropriate worlds and their connections.
3. **Internal constructive work:** where the defined situations call for it, the productive apparatus performs construction within or across those worlds, including through joint inputs and Conditions.
4. **Designed substrate realization:** the resulting products become operative in the same substrate or in other substrates through the designated bridges.

The formal universe is therefore a productive space, not merely an interchange that routes messages between external tools. As part of this recursive work, a design may also call upon another substrate's constructive capacities and incorporate what they derive, making it available for further formal construction.

Thus the complete productive process may cross several bridges. It need not decompose into independent pairs of “send to this substrate, then receive from that same substrate.” The identity of a source does not determine the destination of the product.

The *centre* is this shared semantic and constructive integration. It does not require one privileged formal world, one physical location, one executable or one universal scheduling authority. Those architectural decisions must follow from the capability's requirements.

Joint coherence requires more than separately adequate bridges. The account must establish how shared identities and cross-substrate dependencies agree, which actual states a construction uses, and how simultaneous or conflicting changes are represented and handled. The governing laws and appropriate mechanisms remain to be developed. They must preserve the distinction between what actually happened, what is being requested and what has been constructed.

Every incorporated occurrence must have its formal place, but not every occurrence needs to activate work or reach every other substrate. The Conditions, constructors and bridge relationships define the productive continuations that the capability supports.

## 11. Code as a constructive substrate: one integrated example

Consider a formally constituted network of components and connections. A program in another substrate partitions that network into groups and derives the relationships between those groups.

The formal universe must account for the whole incorporated construction. The required account may be distributed across worlds without fragmenting its identities or connecting meaning.

| Constituent | What its formal representation must explain |
| --- | --- |
| The domain network | Component identities, connection identities, structure and applicable laws. |
| The program | Its constituted operations, bindings, dependencies and meaning as a transformation. |
| Its substrate realization | The code structures and execution arrangement through which the program operates. |
| Particular runtime objects | Which objects and relationships actually exist in this execution, including their correspondence to domain objects. |
| The productive occurrence | Which input state was used, what transformation occurred and its actual status. |
| Intermediate and resulting structures | The generated groups, memberships, inter-group relationships and other incorporated results. |

**All code belonging to the incorporated apparatus must have its formal place with the required semantic and ontological depth.** Representing a filename, an opaque source blob or only a final result does not by itself constitute that account.

### 11.1 An incorporated code object has a formal counterpart

A particular incorporated code object must be represented with the identity and relationships appropriate to its constitution. Its relationship to a domain object is then represented explicitly, not assumed to be identity merely because the names resemble one another.

If two code objects share a reference to one underlying object, that sharing can be constructively significant. A representation that silently duplicates the underlying object would change the represented structure.

This is one reason that deep representation must preserve relationships as well as properties.

### 11.2 A represented program carries its actual organization

The formal account of a program must be adequate to its incorporated constitution: the operations and relationships by which it produces the effects attributed to it.

Depending on the program, this may require structured bodies, bindings, control relationships and effects, rather than only a declaration of intended input and output types.

Representing that constitution is different from proving every behavioral property of the program. It is also different from running a second identical implementation inside the formal universe. The account must distinguish representation, interpretation, execution and proof.

### 11.3 Generated structures are incorporated as constructive material

When the program derives groups, their memberships and their interconnections, those results become formally constituted objects and relationships.

They can then support further work: checking a requirement, comparing alternatives, selecting a group for transformation or constructing another program over the derived structure.

Incorporation constitutes what was actually constructed, making its objects and relationships available for further work.

### 11.4 Change affects the standing of derived results

If the underlying network changes, the formal account must distinguish the previous partition, the state from which it was derived and whatever relationship it now has to the changed network. Such a change can originate in the substrate itself, become formally incorporated and initiate the next constructive episode.

A result may remain valid, require revision or cease to apply. The relevant dependency and preservation laws decide which. Automatic disposal of every old result would be as arbitrary as assuming all results remain applicable forever.

The point of the example is the integration: domain objects, programs, substrate objects and derived results participate in one coherent formal universe. A world concerned with the program's structure can contain domain requirements too; another can organize the same component population through the derived group memberships. These are possible organizations, not a mandatory split into separate worlds. Shared objects remain shared while each constituted relation retains its appropriate meaning.

## 12. Recursive formation of products and productive means

### 12.1 Products participate in subsequent construction

A constructed semantic object is an Abstract Constructor through its nature, whether or not it is immediately used again. It can participate in subsequent construction through that nature, its constituents and its interfaces.

One result can be used by several later tasks. Several independently produced results can be assembled into one higher construction. A later constructor can use a constituent of an earlier result without consuming the whole object.

The theory must explain these relationships according to their actual structure, including the availability and meaning of the parts used.

The recipient can belong to another world or be a joint cross-world construction. A product's standing and dependency structure (§5.6) must survive that use: representing a claim, establishing it under premises and using it where those premises hold are connected but different acts. The relevant qualifications may themselves become operands of subsequent work.

### 12.2 Productive means can themselves be products

An apparatus may construct a program, a specialized constructor arrangement, a new representation or a bridge configuration as an intermediate product. Different constructor kinds can contribute to this formation, and the formed means can itself support a different kind of construction.

Preparing something for another constructor can therefore have several meanings:

| Productive achievement | What has been constituted | What it supplies to subsequent work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input formation | An organized composition of operands with their required identities and relationships. | Material an already configured constructor can use. |
| Task formation | A subject-specific accomplishment or obligation under admitted task-formation laws. | What further work must achieve. |
| Programming or configuration | Organization directing or specializing a capacity. | A defined repertoire or mode of operation, when appropriately realized. |
| Construction of productive means | An arrangement with the components and connections needed to realize a capacity. | Further operative power once its realization requirements are satisfied. |
| Bridge construction | A defined passage with its transformations and preservation relationships. | The ability to relate contexts or realizations for supported constructive purposes. |

These are distinctions of achievement, not five mandatory stages or separately allocated components. A construction can accomplish several together. Conversely, producing a task specification does not itself discharge the task; producing a program does not alone establish an operative constructor; producing an input does not configure every possible receiving capacity.

One possible development forms a specialized program, realizes it through an appropriate arrangement, supplies its constituted input, exercises the resulting capacity and incorporates its effects. Other developments reuse existing means or begin with a substrate-originating occurrence. The task and domain laws determine which relationships and ordering are necessary.

This is an important source of reach within an infinite possibility space. The apparatus need not begin with an individually prewritten procedure for every admissible requirement if its established capability can construct the necessary procedures and realizing arrangements. Their formation must carry the task meaning, operative scope and preservation obligations needed by their eventual consumers.

Constructing productive means remains subject to the same requirements of constitution, realization and incorporation as constructing other products. The capacity to form new programs or constructors must itself belong to the capability; it is not inferred merely from representing those objects.

### 12.3 Use, supported reconfiguration and capability extension

| Situation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| New productive occurrence | Existing capability is exercised on a new admitted requirement or local/cross-world configuration. |
| Supported reconfiguration or further construction | The apparatus forms a new arrangement, program or specialized means using capabilities already included in its constitution. |
| Capability extension | New kinds, primitive capacities, substrate reach or laws are introduced beyond the previously established possibility space. |

These distinctions make recursive development accountable.

Constructing a new program does not necessarily extend the capability boundary: program construction may already belong to the capability. Conversely, adding a previously unsupported substrate capacity does not become an ordinary occurrence merely because it is described as reconfiguration.

The same distinction applies to worlds and domains. A capability may support constructing a new world, revising a world constitution or designing another domain's apparatus. These are substantive meta-constructive powers to define. They do not follow automatically from the method's applicability to arbitrarily designed domains. Where supported, their generated identity, overlap and bridge laws must remain part of the resulting account.

### 12.4 Incorporation and subsequent use are distinct obligations

An included substrate's occurrences, new objects and changes must be formally incorporated, whether they follow from the capability's previous work or arise independently within the substrate.

Their further use depends on whether some constructive continuation calls for them. Some results may remain available without triggering new work; others can immediately change which tasks are applicable.

Mandatory incorporation preserves the coherence of the formal universe. Conditional continuation can initiate construction within or across its worlds. Its products can then be realized in the originating substrate or others according to the design, continuing the integrated productive process.

## 13. Stable manufacture across the possibility space

Stable manufacture means dependable productive capacity relative to admitted requirements and explicit operating premises.

It can permit variation in designs, inputs, execution paths and product representations while preserving the properties that define successful realization.

To give that stability content, the account must establish:

- What satisfies the requirement.
- Which identities, relationships and other invariants must survive.
- Which shared populations and common facts remain coherent across worlds, and which local differences must be preserved.
- What variation is permissible.
- What intermediate structures later construction requires.
- Which constructor kinds and configurations realize the tasks, and which observation, intervention and state laws their cooperation must preserve.
- Which resources and circumstances the capability presupposes.
- How the capacity remains available for further appropriate work.
- How success, incomplete work, failure and unsupported requests are distinguished.

Stability belongs to the coordinated apparatus. An individually reliable component can participate in an unreliable composition if its interface fails to preserve something another stage needs.

A formal proof can establish properties of a defined construction. Evidence about its realization is needed to justify the correspondence with an operative substrate. A tested occurrence establishes facts about that occurrence; coverage of an infinite class requires appropriately general reasoning.

This separation allows the definition to remain ambitious and precise without treating a specification as its own proof.

## 14. Why Abstography is an abstraction over productive methods

A particular productive method organizes particular transformations to obtain a class of results.

Abstography works at the level where **the objects, laws, arrangements and constructive means of such production can themselves be formally constituted, combined and realized as a capability**.

A productive method can therefore enter an abstographic universe in several connected ways:

- As knowledge about a possible transformation.
- As a formally constituted model of that transformation.
- As an operative capacity available through an incorporated substrate.
- As an arrangement that can itself be configured or constructed.

These levels must be connected where the claimed capability requires them.

The additional abstraction is consequently more than describing an existing process from above. It supplies a formal design and construction space in which productive arrangements and their products can be developed before, during and through their realization.

### 14.1 Design capability precedes individual realization

A capability for a class of products must constitute the space in which members can be designed. Its work includes finding and forming a suitable design from the initial requirements.

It also supplies the realization capabilities needed for that design to become an operative product in the relevant setting.

At the level of apparatus formation, the method must support two directions of reasoning: from an intended capability toward the organization that could establish it, and from a proposed organization toward its actual constructive reach. A limitation may belong to a relationship between components rather than to any component considered alone.

Thus the product space, the design activity and the productive means belong to one account. Their joint definition makes it possible to develop the apparatus as a whole.

### 14.2 The chip-design example at the appropriate level

A formal chip design can be bridged into specialized design software. That software may derive placements, connections, analyses or other design structures.

Those derived objects and relationships return to their appropriate formal worlds and connections, where they can affect requirements, further construction and revision. The same design object may participate in distinct structural and functional organizations without becoming two designs.

This is one possible sequence. A design change initiated within the software can also enter the formal universe and occasion constructive work there, whose product may be realized in that software or another integrated substrate.

Both sequences demonstrate cross-substrate recursive production. Connecting physical fabrication and measurements would constitute a further expansion with its own capacities, bridges and representational obligations.

### 14.3 Lithography as a deliberate conceptual introduction

Lithography was chosen as a useful analogous productive method through which to introduce Abstography. The reader can begin with a concrete relationship: an intended organization is brought into realized form through an apparatus whose formative means, transformations and receiving material have been designed to work together. In the optical comparison, light participates in that formation. Understanding this relationship does not require first understanding the specialized engineering of a lithographic system.

The comparison directs attention toward a capability rather than an isolated act. A productive apparatus must accommodate the designs within its reach, organize the necessary transformations, supply suitable intermediates and support dependable realization. Its capability depends on the whole arrangement, including the compatibility of successive stages. These relationships provide a clear introduction to what must be constituted in an abstographic apparatus.

The name then connects that formative picture with Abstract Constructors: formal objects and compositions whose semantic organization can shape products, transformations and further productive work. *Graphy* evokes the giving of realized form; *Abstract Light* makes their constructive contribution intelligible (§6.8). This is a positive relationship between the chosen analogy and the concept, not a requirement to model every abstographic process on an optical sequence.

Abstography develops the account at a further level of abstraction. The formal possibility space in which designs, task families and productive arrangements are constituted is itself part of the method. Construction can form further programs, bridges or operative means; products can participate recursively in subsequent work. Formation can occur within the formal universe, across its worlds or through incorporated substrates. Activity can originate on either side of a substrate bridge and lead to internal construction and further realization (§10).

Lithography thus provides an accessible conceptual entrance into organized formation, while the abstographic definition explains the broader capability being constituted. The analogy helps readers identify the relationships to understand; the domain's actual laws determine their possible forms, compositions and reach.

### 14.4 Domain mapping constitutes the capability, including its worlds and overlaps

**Domain mapping is the reciprocal constitution of a capability's possible structures and possible work.** It develops the objects, worlds, meanings, task families, substantive transformations, constructor kinds and repertoires, products and realizing means through which the intended accomplishment becomes possible. Its object is the domain's productive constitution itself, not a description of an independently completed field.

The purpose defining a capability is distinct from an individual requirement supplied during its exercise. A capability to design a class of factories must be constituted to accommodate the admitted variation among individual requirements. A mapping purpose can also require plural alternatives, comparison or further design; it need not prescribe one fixed final structure.

Mapping has several mutually informing directions:

1. Intended capabilities expose subjects, distinctions and families of work that must be constituted.
2. Task meanings reveal required inputs, intermediate achievements, constructor powers and permissible results.
3. Proposed objects, relationships and productive means reveal which transformations and further tasks they actually support.
4. Intended products and their consumers expose missing constituents, grounds, access or incorporation relationships.
5. World differentiation, heterogeneous composition and substrate realization expose necessary overlaps, shared premises and preservation laws.
6. Those findings revise the earliest deficient constitution, and the consequences must be carried through its actual dependents.
7. The resulting reach is assessed against the capability claim, whose meaning must remain independent of retrospective selection of successful cases.

Necessary objects need not be familiar nouns extracted from a source. A comparison basis, supporting family, shared identity structure, intermediate program or joint subject can become necessary because the intended work cannot be constituted without its meaning. Several well-described objects and pairwise links do not necessarily constitute the composite that a responsibility needs. Similarly, a task can expose a needed constructor profile absent from the initial plan, while a proposed configuration may reveal that the task lacks adequate information or supporting means.

The mapping must give required constituents their formation and use accounts. A subject needed by a task must be obtainable in an appropriate starting situation; a product must have the meaning and interfaces its consumer requires. A construction that can begin only after its own intended first achievement already exists has not established the promised path. These questions concern the constitution of capability before they concern the arrangement of an execution system.

The work must also distinguish an underlying requirement from one chosen means of meeting it. Discovering a missing distinction does not uniquely determine whether the repair is a new object, relation, interface, world or transformation. The design must explain why its chosen constitution supplies the needed capability without importing unnecessary restrictions.

This applies directly to overlaps: mapping must decide which world participations share identity, which populations are maintained in common, which structures remain contextual, and which bridges make joint work possible. Defining isolated ontologies and later matching their labels cannot substitute for that account.

Knowledge sources, existing formalizations and newly constructed results can all inform mapping. Source claims, design choices, formal consequences, empirical claims and unresolved questions retain their different standing. A need for a productive capability does not prove that the proposed means can realize it.

Mapping can itself be undertaken by people, constructive apparatuses or their combination. Its recursive character is governed by the intended capability and justified dependencies, not by endless accumulation. It is complete at a claimed scope only when the objects, transformation meanings, products and connections required there form an adequate account; an implementation-constrained sketch is not the general boundary of Abstography.

## 15. Mathematical directions for a coherent productive whole

Mathematics is necessary because the claimed capability depends on exact relationships: between possible and actual objects, shared identity and contextual structure, local and cross-world organization, composition and behavior, and formal constitution and substrate realization.

The conceptual account makes these questions intelligible before selecting their formal treatment. A reader can understand that the same object must retain its identity across two differently organized worlds before studying the mathematics needed to define and preserve that relationship. Likewise, the difference between describing a task and possessing the power to perform it is understandable before a formal account of attainment is supplied. Advanced mathematical fluency is not a prerequisite for grasping the method's governing principles.

Mathematical development makes those relationships precise, exposes hidden premises, establishes consequences and exclusions, and supports justified claims about the capability. Its appropriate structures must be selected by those obligations. A formalization can also reveal an inadequate conceptual distinction; the explanation must then be improved along with the formal account. Accessible principles and rigorous development therefore inform one another, rather than belonging to unrelated popular and technical versions of the method.

### 15.1 Local compatibility and the whole

Worlds can share objects and common content while retaining different local constitutions. Their integration must state what is common, not infer it from superficial similarity. Conversely, explicitly shared objects are genuinely shared, not merely presumed distinct objects related after the fact.

Suppose local structures \(M_i\) and \(M_j\) have a constituted common interface \(O\), and \(\rho_i,\rho_j\) expose their content at that interface. Their restrictions must agree in the required sense:

\[
\rho_i(M_i)\cong\rho_j(M_j).
\]

The notation is schematic: the requirement could be literal agreement, a specified isomorphism or another justified relation. An isomorphism is not automatically identity; the chosen constitution must explain how its presentations represent shared objects. The agreement concerns \(O\), not every additional relation belonging to either world. It does not require a previously supplied universal schema of which every world is a restriction.

Pairwise agreements do not by themselves establish coherent global assembly. Identifications on successive overlaps must compose as required, and local laws must remain jointly realizable. Where maps \(\alpha_{ij}\) identify common local presentations, a typical coherence obligation on a triple overlap is \(\alpha_{jk}\circ\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{ik}\), with its domain and meaning explicitly defined. Other relationships need their appropriate coherence laws instead.

The global account can retain heterogeneous worlds and their connections. Its unity need not be one homogeneous world obtained by erasing the differences. Existence, uniqueness or availability of an assembly must be established to the extent claimed; the shared population alone establishes none of these stronger properties.

### 15.2 Coverings, sheaves and descent: precise roles rather than labels

A covering expresses that specified local contexts jointly suffice for a stated whole. To use this mathematically, the account must define the contexts, their maps or restrictions, and which families count as covers. A collection of worlds is not automatically a topological cover merely because its members concern one domain.

For an illustrative topological presentation, let opens \(U_i\) cover \(U\), and let \(F(V)\) be the specified data available over a context \(V\), with restriction maps. The sheaf condition requires every family \(s_i\in F(U_i)\) satisfying

\[
s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}=s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}
\]

to arise from a unique \(s\in F(U)\) with \(s|_{U_i}=s_i\). On a site, appropriate covering families or sieves generalize this presentation. The mathematical content is the stated compatibility and unique gluing, not the word “sheaf.”

For Abstography, this raises specific questions: what do the local data constitute; which object identities or relations are common; what does restriction preserve; and when do the local contributions determine a whole? Different local ontologies may first require an explicit common interpretation before this question can even be formulated.

Where the local data are structured objects connected by coherent isomorphisms, descent and stacks can express gluing with that additional structure. Their hypotheses and notion of equivalence must be established. Caramello–Lafforgue's account of sheaves and Caramello–Zanfa's treatment of descent provide relevant directions (§18.3).

These instruments may describe selected parts of a universe rather than the whole apparatus. Shared identity, constructive refinement and complementary cross-world inputs are distinct relationships; none should be forced into a sheaf model if its laws do not fit. Local-to-global existence also does not by itself supply an effective productive procedure attaining a requested result.

### 15.3 What categorical and topos-theoretic bridges might contribute

Categorical logic and topos theory offer ways to study theories through structured semantics, compare different presentations and transfer understanding through suitable common structures.

They can help clarify how apparently different local accounts express related content, or how a global semantic setting organizes them. In particular, different suitable theories can share a semantic structure through equivalent classifying toposes, making appropriate properties transferable between presentations. Complementary worlds need not be equivalent theories to participate coherently in one capability.

Their exact application to Abstography must identify the theories, interpretations, structures and preservation claims involved. An operational bridge to a productive substrate is not automatically a topos-theoretic equivalence, and a classifying mathematical object is not automatically a constructor that can realize every described product.

Likewise, a topology requires a specified mathematical role; calling a whole “topological” does not establish its coherence.

### 15.4 Meaning, operation and knowledge

Three accounts must remain connected and distinguishable:

1. **Meaning:** what the objects, tasks and transformations are.
2. **Operation:** how an apparatus realizes those transformations.
3. **Knowledge of adequacy:** which claims follow from proof, which have supporting empirical evidence and which remain open.

Complete representation of an incorporated structure does not imply decidability of every question about it. A represented program can have a precise meaning while some properties remain unproved or undecidable.

The intended capability must therefore articulate its own achievable obligations rather than depend on an unrestricted assumption that every meaningful property can be automatically settled.

## 16. Abstographic machines: the operative whole

### 16.1 Definition and relationship to Abstography

**An Abstographic machine is the operative whole realizing a designed domain's productive constitution: its formal universe of Abstract Constructors and possible work, its constructors of the required kinds and repertoires, and their integrated arrangements, interfaces and substrate bridges. Their lawful cooperation makes admitted requirements attainable through recursive construction, while maintaining the formal constitution of the objects, means and occurrences participating in that activity.**

Abstography names the method and constructive principles through which such capability is constituted and exercised. The machine names a particular organized whole realizing it. Its possible objects, tasks and productive powers have been constituted together; operation exercises that constitution rather than supplying missing domain meanings anew for each occurrence. The preceding sections define the ingredients and their relationships; this section explains what it means for them to belong to one capable organization.

A description of a machine states its intended constitution. An implementation supplies realizing mechanisms. A claim that it adequately realizes its admitted capability requires the corresponding mathematical and operative grounds (§§4 and 13). These levels allow us to discuss machine designs before their realization without treating a design document as an operating apparatus.

### 16.2 What makes it one machine

Its unity lies in the integrated capability: admitted requirements have a constructive meaning, the relevant objects and means can participate together, and products and changes retain their proper place in the continuing organization.

This unity may include several worlds with different constitutions, shared populations, local structures and joint Conditions. It can include heterogeneous constructor kinds whose observations, interventions and products serve connected responsibilities. It can extend through several substrates and their bridges. Neither the number of databases nor the number of programs, servers or physical locations determines the conceptual number of machines.

The machine boundary must instead identify the productive responsibility, constituted reach, incorporated means and operating premises being claimed together. Some resources may be provided externally under those premises; what is claimed as incorporated retains the full representation obligation. The exact criteria for individuating, nesting or composing machines remain further definition work, not assumptions supplied by a software packaging choice.

### 16.3 Its operation is the life of the constituted organization

An occurrence can begin with a requirement or situation already represented in the universe, or with activity in an incorporated substrate. The machine supplies the appropriate incorporation, recognition, task and input formation, and configured constructive means. Its work can stay within one world, depend jointly on several worlds or pass through further substrate capacities. Different kinds of constructor can contribute through supplied-input transformations, continuing interaction or other constituted forms of activity.

Products can change the current field of work: they can instantiate new subjects, resolve an earlier question, create a dependency or supply a new productive means. Preparing an input, forming a task, configuring a constructor and realizing further operative capacity retain their different meanings (§12.2). The machine's continuing organization gives those changes their defined consequences. An occurrence does not need to follow the same sequence as another occurrence to exercise the same standing capability.

Coherence belongs to that operation. A shared object remains the same object across its participations; common content retains its meaning; local relations retain their scopes; incorporated substrate effects acquire their formal place. Conditions and Processes can consequently respond to the actual constituted situation rather than disconnected descriptions of it.

This explains why the machine is more than a collection of formally described components. Its defining relationships must themselves be realized: supplying a correctly constituted input, maintaining an identity, performing a passage or incorporating a result is part of the capability, not left to an unacknowledged intervention for each occurrence.

### 16.4 How the whole determines productive reach

The machine's possibility space includes what its constituents can accomplish through their lawful joint organization. A Process using several worlds, cooperation between different constructor kinds, a Condition over a generated constituent, or a bridge realizing a constructed program can make a task attainable that no isolated part could accomplish alone. Conversely, a missing shared distinction, required power or adequate passage can prevent attainment despite each component's individual capacity.

The defining ambition remains bounded, infinitely generative capability: the organization supports any admitted requirement under its stated premises. It need not contain a prewritten route for every case, because it can possess supported means of constructing routes, programs or intermediate arrangements. Those meta-constructive powers must themselves have an adequate constitution.

The machine's current population and current activity are therefore not its entire capability. They are particular realizations within its standing space. Supported formation of new worlds or productive arrangements is likewise distinct from introducing powers beyond that space (§12.3).

### 16.5 What later machine examples should make intelligible

Future examples should instantiate this definition by explaining a machine's intended capability, constituted task families, organization of worlds and overlaps, necessary constructor kinds and their cooperation, substrate relationships and one or more productive developments. Their purpose is to reveal how the whole derives its reach, not to make the example's particular technology or object catalogue universal.

The present account deliberately leaves detailed machine forms open. We have established the layer on which those definitions can be developed: a machine is an operative, coherently constituted totality whose heterogeneous and overlapping parts jointly realize a specified productive power.

## 17. Current commitments and the remaining definition work

### 17.1 Commitments of this account

1. Abstography concerns the ground-up constitution and exercise of formal productive capability; domain mapping develops objects, worlds, meanings, task families, constructor powers and their connections together.
2. Its characteristic scope is a bounded, infinite possibility space of structures and possible work, with domain-relative attainment of admitted requirements rather than merely expressive admissibility. General formation and composition laws account for future cases without enumerating their occurrences.
3. A domain's formal universe can contain heterogeneous worlds, shared objects and identical or overlapping populations with different local organization.
4. Shared identity, world participation, contextual structure and substrate realization are distinct. Common commitments and legitimate local differences must remain coherent.
5. Semantic objects and their constituted compositions are Abstract Constructors, with overlapping contributions to products, transformation repertoires, further work and substrate realization. Abstract Light names them in that constructive role.
6. Processes and Conditions can concern local or joint cross-world configurations, including different aspects of the same object and objects generated by earlier constructions. Their own semantic roles remain distinct.
7. Tasks, constructor kinds, configured repertoires, productive processes and occurrences have connected but distinct meanings. Constructors can differ in configuration, observation, intervention, state and result formation; one fixed program can support varying processes without changing its kind or primitive powers.
8. Construction can generate further subjects, tasks, programs and productive means within an established possibility space. Input formation, configuration, operative realization and performance retain distinct achievement meanings. Product standing and constituent interfaces govern subsequent use.
9. Internal bridges support the required passages and joint use across formal worlds. Substrate bridges connect formal constitution with operative realization and incorporation; no direction is required to begin an episode. Their preservation concerns operative scope and consequential effects as well as represented data.
10. Everything incorporated has a complete formal account at its declared semantic and ontological scope. That account can span worlds, including worlds which combine substrate structures with other domain objects.
11. Substrate-originating activity can occasion construction inside the formal universe, with products realized in the same substrate or others according to the design. Actual occurrence, incorporation and further activation retain their different meanings.
12. An Abstographic machine is the operative whole realizing the domain's designed structures and possible work through its heterogeneous constructive organization. Its semantic and constructive unity need not be a uniform ontology, one constructor kind or centralized physical architecture.
13. Stable manufacture concerns dependable coverage and preservation under explicit premises. Proof, specification and evidence of operative realization retain their different force.
14. The general account is independent of a particular implementation, domain catalogue or representation technology. Specific foundations and machine forms remain to be developed.
15. The lithography analogy, Abstract Light and Constructor Theory are deliberate means of making the productive organization intelligible. The conceptual explanation must stand in clear relation to the mathematical development without requiring that development's specialist language as the reader's starting point.

## 19. Synthesis and revision discipline

Abstography constitutes and exercises bounded, infinitely generative productive capability. It develops possible structures and possible work together: the domain's worlds, objects, meanings, task families, transformations, constructor kinds and repertoires, and the interfaces and realizing means required to attain admitted requirements. Its formal universe supplies both that standing possibility space and the account of actual constructive development.

The constituent worlds can differ genuinely while sharing objects, populations and common commitments. Their local structures, interfaces and internal bridges make joint constructive use possible without erasing those differences. Processes can consume cross-world inputs; Conditions can recognize cross-world configurations and constitute further usable objects. Abstract Constructors can participate through several world-specific structures while retaining their identity. Different constructor kinds make those compositions productive through their configured powers, with observation, intervention and continuing behavior defined according to their nature.

Incorporated substrates supply further constructive realizations. Their objects, occurrences and changes acquire their proper formal place even when activity originates there. Work inside the formal universe can produce results which become operative in the same substrate or others according to the design. Representation is mandatory within the incorporated scope; subsequent use follows the defined productive organization.

An Abstographic machine is the operative totality realizing that organization. Its unity is productive as well as representational: **the universe in which capability is constituted, the heterogeneous means through which it operates, and the actual worlds and substrate structures changed by its products remain coherently connected.** Its local and joint constructions can generate further subjects, task instances, programs and productive means within its supported reach. The meanings of that possible work belong to the designed constitution; unfamiliar occurrences exercise it without requiring their missing semantics to be invented anew.

The concept identifies this integrated productive organization as a subject that can be understood, designed and examined as a whole. Its name and chosen explanatory resources make that subject approachable: lithography introduces coordinated formation, Abstract Light illuminates the constructive participation of semantic objects, and Constructor Theory supplies an economical organizing framework. Mathematical development gives the required relationships their exact constitution and grounds. These are complementary contributions to one account, whose principles should remain intelligible as its technical depth develops.

Future revisions must improve this one account from beginning to end. A changed definition must propagate to its examples, mathematical obligations, distinctions and implications. Sound material should be retained in its proper context; superseded claims should be replaced rather than accumulated beside their corrections.

The next definitions should give greater precision to the open fields in §17 and develop machine examples on the foundation of §16. They should preserve the account's positive identity while letting justified mathematical and architectural discoveries determine its exact realization.
