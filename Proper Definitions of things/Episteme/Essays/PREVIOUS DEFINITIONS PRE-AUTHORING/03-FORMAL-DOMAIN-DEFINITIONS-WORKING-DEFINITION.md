# Formal Domain Definitions — Working Definition

**Status:** Substantively revised working definition — 9 October 2026. This account distinguishes source-grounded observations, explanatory reconstructions, proposed domain constitutions and mathematical results established within their stated premises.

**Purpose:** To explain the semantic nature of existing fields and domains; the understanding and capacities their presentations presuppose; and the deeper constitution through which their meanings, subjects and possible work can become explicitly organized and operable.

A developed domain is not merely a collection of information awaiting storage. It already makes things intelligible through concepts, explanations, distinctions, examples, methods and practices. Much of its use can depend on people recognizing what those expressions concern, supplying context, forming intermediate understandings and judging what follows. Some of those responsibilities are already precisely formalized; others are communicated meaningfully without receiving a self-contained formal constitution.

The central subject is the passage between these conditions:

> From a domain that competent participants can understand and work within, toward a domain whose relevant semantic and constructive organization is itself sufficiently constituted to support the intended forms of understanding and operation.

The passage can require recovering existing definitions, making interpretive commitments explicit, investigating what is not known, creating missing objects and relationships, and developing the mathematical and technical means that the intended capability requires. It is not exhausted by adding detail to descriptions or connecting already finished components.

A *constitution* explains what makes the relevant constituents what they are, how their meanings and relationships are determined, and what uses or consequences they support. An *operative realization* supplies means that actually instantiate and exercise that constitution. These terms do not assume one software architecture or that every meaningful responsibility has a deterministic algorithm.

This is a conceptual definition for our own understanding and development, not an essay. It preserves the distinction between existing fields and domains and the deliberately constituted, potentially cross-field domains developed for particular purposes. It does not infer a universal deficiency from one field or make today's implementation repertoire the boundary of future definition.

**Reading sequence:** §1 establishes the general subject. §§2–3 explain existing semantics and interpretive dependence. §4 reconstructs contrasting mathematical, psychological, instructional, narrative and design cases. §§5–13 develop constitution, scope, objects, context, work, change and mapping. §14 gives explicit mathematical constructions and their limits; §§15–17 connect them to derived operability and evidence. §§18–20 state the wider significance, achieved distinctions, unresolved responsibilities and source grounds.

## 1. Fields and domains before deliberate capability design

### 1.1 What a field is

**A field is an organized region of knowledge, inquiry or practice, identified through connected subjects, characteristic questions, concepts, methods and forms of activity.** Its organization makes some objects and differences significant, provides ways of investigating or working with them, and supplies relations through which results can be understood and used.

A field is therefore more than the documents in which its knowledge is recorded or the institution in which it is taught. Those can carry and develop the field; its intellectual and practical organization concerns what is studied or done, which distinctions matter and how its contributions relate. Its subjects may be empirical, formal, interpretive, constructive or combinations of these.

A developed field can contain different approaches, incomplete accounts and disagreements. Its existence does not presuppose one finished theory. The identity and changing boundaries of a field require further explanation where they become consequential; the initial point is that there is already an organization to understand, not an empty space awaiting a productive designer.

### 1.2 What a domain is

**A domain is a distinguishable, relatively bounded region of subjects, knowledge or activity, understood through the objects, relationships, questions and practices that give it a particular scope and internal organization.** In relation to a broader field, a domain usually identifies a more delimited region within it. Its boundary can concern a kind of subject, a family of phenomena or problems, a mode of inquiry or a practice.

A domain need not have been deliberately assembled around a new capability. It can already exist through the development of knowledge and practice, with its own concepts, questions and internal distinctions. Nor must it already possess a complete formal definition to count as a domain. Having an organized subject and giving that organization an adequate explicit account are different achievements.

The boundary makes the scope intelligible; it need not be impermeable or permanently settled. A domain can depend on knowledge developed elsewhere, share objects with neighboring domains or contain disagreements about its own limits. More exact criteria of individuation must be developed when the work requires them.

### 1.3 Their distinction is relative to the organization being considered

*Field* and *domain* do not name two permanently separate kinds of entity. Their use often expresses a difference of scope and level of consideration. A region can be treated as a domain in one wider account and as a field when its own internal regions are being examined.

Materials science can be considered a field relative to regions such as metallurgy, ceramics and composite materials. Within a broader account of engineering, materials science can instead be treated as a contributing domain, whose own internal domains remain distinguishable. Biology, microbiology and genetics similarly illustrate differences of scope without requiring that every disciplinary relationship fit one strict tree.

Nesting is one relationship; overlap and contribution are others. Saying that architectural work draws upon several engineering domains does not make all engineering a taxonomic subfield of architecture. We must distinguish a region's place in an organization of knowledge from its role in a particular undertaking. That distinction will matter when we turn to integration around a selected purpose (§6).

### 1.4 Their existing internal organization

Fields and domains can already possess substantial rational organization, even where that organization is not collected into one formal account. *Rational organization* here concerns the relations through which subjects, reasons, methods and results can be understood and examined. It does not certify every inherited claim or require all disagreements to have been resolved.

| Constituent of an existing field or domain | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Objects and distinctions | What the work concerns and which differences are consequential. |
| Concepts and meanings | How those subjects and relationships are understood. |
| Questions and problems | What is investigated, explained, compared, designed or otherwise worked upon. |
| Theories, models and explanations | Accounts of relationships, behavior, possibilities and limits. |
| Methods and instruments | Ways of obtaining, reasoning with or transforming relevant material. |
| Results and their uses | What counts as an answer, finding, design or other achievement, and how it enters further work. |
| Grounds and standards of assessment | How claims, performances and disagreements are evaluated, and what counts as progress, failure or a need for revision. |
| Relations to other domains | What is borrowed, shared, supplied or reinterpreted across boundaries. |

These constituents have different forms and degrees of development in different subjects. Their relationships make the region more than a list of topics. A method concerns particular objects under particular premises; a result answers a question at a scope; a concept can determine which differences an instrument needs to preserve.

### 1.5 The subject, its knowledge and its definition

We must distinguish **what a domain concerns**, **the knowledge and practices through which it is approached**, and **the account defining that organization**. A subject's empirical properties are not created by writing a definition of them. An inherited account can be incomplete or mistaken while still referring to a real subject. Existing domains can also concern agreements, classifications or roles constituted through prior conventions and decisions. Describing those constitutive rules does not itself enact them. In a deliberately constructed formal domain, some new objects and laws are constituted by the definition being developed; those cases require their appropriate interpretation.

These distinctions let us ask how a domain is organized before asking how to formalize it. They also prevent us from treating an inadequate account as proof that there was no domain or knowledge there in the first place.

## 2. The semantic organization already present in domains

### 2.1 A domain communicates a way of understanding its subjects

A domain's language is not simply a set of labels attached to otherwise self-explanatory things. It makes objects intelligible through relationships: what they are, what distinguishes them, how they arise, what they affect, which questions concern them and what can be done with them.

Understanding *an explanation*, for example, involves more than identifying a passage of text. It involves a question, something to be explained, relationships offered as making it intelligible, and standards for whether those relationships answer the question. Understanding *an interest* involves someone for whom something matters, what it is about that subject that matters, and the circumstances in which the attribution is warranted. Neither concept is exhausted by a name and a numerical property.

Much of this organization is expressed through meaningful prose. Definitions refer to concepts already understood; theories connect them; examples show their application; methods recommend what to attend to. A reader who learns the field progressively acquires the ability to move through that organization.

**Semantic richness is the organization of meaningful distinctions and relationships. Formal constitution makes selected distinctions, relationships and consequences exact under stated rules.** These are related but different achievements. A sophisticated explanation can be semantically rich without specifying all the determinations required to exercise its content in another arrangement. A formal theory also has semantics; formality is not the absence of meaning.

The central contrast is therefore not “meaningful human knowledge versus meaningless formal symbols.” It is between different ways in which meaning is constituted, communicated, supplied and made available for use.

### 2.2 What knowing a concept can involve

Several relationships can participate in the meaning of a domain concept:

| Relationship | What it contributes to understanding |
| --- | --- |
| Referential | Which subjects or occurrences the concept concerns, and how they are identified |
| Contrastive | What must be distinguished from it, including nearby concepts and misleading examples |
| Inferential | What follows from attributing it, under which premises, and what does not follow |
| Explanatory or causal | How it participates in an account of why something occurs or has its character |
| Functional | What role or capacity it has within an arrangement |
| Normative | Which requirements, purposes or standards govern its assessment |
| Modal or constructive | What variations, compositions and transformations are possible or permitted |

These are explanatory dimensions, not a universal classification of all meanings. Their importance is that a definition can depend on several at once.

For example, “this material is relevant” can concern its relationship to a person's purposes, its contribution to answering a question, or its membership in the same topic. These relationships are connected, but they license different uses. Selecting material because it shares a topic is not equivalent to selecting it because it contributes to an explanation.

A competent reader often recognizes the intended relationship through context and examples. A formal account must determine which relationship its object represents, how it is grounded and what subsequent work may infer or construct from it.

### 2.3 Examples and conventions teach distinctions that prose can leave compressed

An example can communicate more than an instance of an already completed rule. It can help constitute the reader's understanding of what the rule means.

A contrast between enjoying an activity and enjoying its reward teaches the reader to distinguish an action from one of its consequences. A contrast between a hurricane's destructive effects and its formation teaches that topic continuity is not continuity of explanatory role. A narrative can make one occurrence background in one telling and a decisive action in another. These contrasts will be examined directly in §4.

The learning depends on capacities that the example does not recreate from nothing. The reader already has some understanding of persons, events, causes, purposes, language and social circumstances. Domain learning specializes and reorganizes that background.

Diagrams and conventions have the same dependence in another form. An arrow may mean temporal succession, physical flow, implication, dependency or a proposed transition. Its visible shape does not determine which. A learned convention can make its meaning very stable without every diagram restating the convention.

This is not adequately diagnosed as mere ambiguity. A source can communicate reliably to its intended community while relying on a developed interpretive capacity outside its explicit statements. The future constitution problem is to identify which of those dependencies must become part of the account or an explicitly supplied realizing capacity.

An illustrative learning cycle makes the role of practice clearer. A novice selects a dramatic hurricane image because it shares the lesson's topic. A more experienced practitioner asks which formation relationship the image actually explains. Comparing that image with a representation of the process corrects the novice's criterion: material can introduce why a subject matters without explaining how it works. Subsequent applications can stabilize this distinction through examples, criticism and accepted uses. The community need not have encoded a complete recognition procedure for the distinction to guide practice. Its stability also does not make it immune to dispute or revision.

An analogy contributes differently: it transfers a relationship from a familiar organization to a less familiar one. The transferred relationship and its limits must be distinguished. The human-compiler analogy below is useful only when we identify what it carries across, rather than inheriting every property of a software compiler.

### 2.4 Why current presentations reasonably rely on people

A textbook usually teaches people who already possess language, memory, perceptual recognition and practical understanding. A professional method addresses people trained to discriminate situations and adapt its application. An artistic account may cultivate a sensibility through examples rather than prescribe one uniquely correct continuation.

Such works can be successful on their own terms. Their purpose need not be to define a reusable operative environment. Writing every prerequisite into each book would be impractical, often unnecessary for its audience, and sometimes beyond the available conceptual or technical means.

Knowledge is also distributed. A model supplies calculations; an instrument supplies observations; a standard supplies conventions; a practitioner supplies interpretation; collaborators supply complementary expertise. These together can make work possible even where no single account explicitly constitutes their full connection.

The relevant change of demand is this: **when we want the domain's organization itself to support dependable, reusable operation, a necessary contribution cannot remain unlocated simply because competent people have previously supplied it.** We must discover whether it is already explicitly available, must be reconstructed, requires new inquiry, or must be deliberately constituted.

### 2.5 Formalization has a spectrum and several dimensions

There are substantial differences between a mathematical theory whose operations and consequences are explicitly specified and a practice communicated principally through examples, criticism and cultivated judgment. Those differences matter. They do not form one simple ladder on which every part of a field occupies the same position.

| Dimension | More explicitly constituted work can supply | A consequential responsibility that can remain elsewhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semantic articulation | Definitions, contrasts, examples and explanatory relationships | Identifying the intended referent or applying a distinction in a new situation |
| Ontological constitution | Types, identities, roles, processes and relationships | Determining what counts as one occurrence, one object or the same subject across contexts |
| Formal specification | Formation rules, axioms, constraints and justified consequences | Choosing and grounding the interpretation to which those laws are being applied |
| Empirical grounding | Procedures, observations, measurement models and evidence | Establishing whether a construct or causal interpretation is supported in this case |
| Inferential organization | Premises, consequence relations, qualifications and argument structures | Selecting warranted premises or retaining unresolved alternatives |
| Constructive organization | Operations, admissible compositions, transformations and result forms | Supplying a required primitive capacity or discovering a missing intermediate |
| Operative realization | Means that actually exercise the defined responsibilities | Resources, observation, correspondence and dependencies not supplied by the representation |

Mathematics provides especially strong examples of explicit structural determination. Yet mathematical exposition, discovery, modeling and interpretation are not identical to a finished formal theory. Psychology can combine exact statistical operations with substantial work over constructs and measurement interpretation. Engineering can combine exact local models with domain-dependent judgment about their application. Narrative and artistic practices can possess exact constraints, developed concepts and coherent methods without settling every interpretation or evaluation.

The question is not simply whether a field “has mathematics.” It is **which meanings and responsibilities are determined, how they are determined, and what the connected use of them still requires**.

## 3. What competent interpretation supplies

### 3.1 Learning develops an organization that descriptions can then presuppose

Learning a field includes acquiring ways to recognize its subjects, distinguish nearby cases, understand its explanations and use its methods. Remembered statements participate in this achievement, but do not exhaust it.

Here, *learned conceptual organization* names that functional achievement. It does not assert that a person contains one explicit, complete or consistent internal theory. Nor does it imply that all expertise is verbalizable.

A short instruction can activate a substantial organization of prior understanding. “Assess whether the explanation is relevant to the objective” presupposes that the practitioner can identify the objective, understand the explanation, recognize what the explanation actually establishes and relate that achievement to what the objective calls for. These are substantive contributions, not four names for the same mental act.

### 3.2 A traced example: what does an enjoyment answer concern?

Deci and Ryan explicitly distinguish their psychological account of intrinsic motivation from the operational indicators used to investigate it. One example asks whether a participant reporting enjoyment after a rewarded activity enjoyed the activity or the reward [^P4].

Follow what competent interpretation supplies:

1. **It identifies the question.** The inquiry concerns motivation for a particular activity, not undifferentiated pleasant experience.
2. **It distinguishes the events.** Performing the activity and receiving its reward can occur in one experimental episode without being the same occurrence.
3. **It reads the report as about something.** The meaning of “I enjoyed it” depends on what “it” concerns. The numerical strength of the answer does not supply its object.
4. **It places the distinction inside the theory.** In the account under examination, enjoyment of the activity bears differently on the construct from enjoyment of the reward.
5. **It determines what the present evidence permits.** If the referent is unresolved, the answer cannot simply be used as if activity-directed enjoyment had been established.
6. **It forms further work.** The interpreter may seek clarification, examine the procedure or retain alternatives. The result of that inquiry must remain associated with the original report and episode.

No step requires us to claim that a competent person consciously follows this sequence. It reconstructs the dependencies of the judgment. A questionnaire supplies wording, a participant supplies an answer, a procedure supplies context, and the investigator supplies or obtains the interpretation. The contribution is partly human and partly distributed.

The same recorded score can support different uses after its meaning is clarified. Therefore, the missing information is not another precision digit. It is a relationship between the report, its referent, the inquiry and the theoretical interpretation.

### 3.3 Recognition can constitute a subject of work

A method may assume that its input has already been identified: a relevant event, an explanatory relationship, a disputed requirement or an appropriate comparison.

Recognizing that subject can itself require construction. A designer may have to form an account of which requirements concern the same arrangement. An interpreter may distinguish an action from its narration, or a person's stated purpose from an observer's inference about it. The resulting subject was not necessarily present as a ready-made record.

This work can be constrained without having a single predetermined answer. Several interpretations may be defensible, and their differences may matter. A deeper account must preserve what distinguishes them and what would warrant using one, revising it or leaving the question open.

Formal domain definition consequently concerns both **operations on constituted objects** and **the meaningful formation of those objects in the first place**. Beginning with a database already containing “the relevant configuration” can conceal the latter responsibility.

### 3.4 Knowing, applying and performing

Anderson's treatment of declarative and procedural knowledge, skill acquisition and levels of explanation helps distinguish knowing a statement, understanding its use and possessing the capacity to perform that use. This is our functional synthesis, not a three-part taxonomy attributed verbatim to him [^P1].

Remembering a definition does not guarantee that a person recognizes its instances. Recognizing an instance does not establish that all premises of a method hold. Understanding the required result does not itself supply the skill to construct it.

The older *human compiler* analogy can illuminate how a trained person turns compressed material into situated understanding and action. What transfers is the relationship between an expressed organization and a capacity that develops it into something usable: bindings must be established, relationships preserved and results made available. What does not automatically transfer is the assumption that all consequential source meanings and permissible translations were already fixed. A practitioner may have to discover the intended subject, challenge an assumption or constitute a missing interpretation. Those acts can change what is understood, not merely encode an unchanged meaning differently.

The analogy consequently reveals a responsibility while also locating its limit. It is not a literal theory of the mind, and it does not establish that human judgment follows a deterministic translation procedure.

Psychology is useful here as a source about learned capacities. It has a second, distinct role in this document: its own constructs and methods are examples of domain knowledge requiring interpretation (§4). Neither role authorizes reducing all domain constitution to one psychological theory.

### 3.5 The unit of capability can include people, tools and practices

Hutchins's analysis of navigation motivates examining coordinated people, instruments and representations rather than assigning every contribution to an isolated mind [^P2].

An instrument can embody a transformation. A worksheet can ensure that a necessary question is asked. A professional convention can fix a unit or a notation. A collaborator can supply a distinct interpretation that another participant cannot produce.

A domain's capability can therefore exceed what its written exposition alone specifies. Conversely, the existence of a useful explanation does not establish that an operative arrangement has connected it to the situations in which it is needed.

The practical question is: **where is each necessary responsibility actually discharged, and what enables its reuse when the case changes?**

### 3.6 Intelligibility is not yet a reusable operative constitution

| Achievement | What it establishes | What does not follow automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding a statement | A reader can make sense of its intended claim | Its terms have been assigned correctly to this particular case |
| Identifying the subject | The relevant objects, occurrences or alternatives have been constituted | The claim applies or the evidence supports it |
| Establishing applicability | Stated premises and scope permit a particular use | The required result has been constructed |
| Producing a result | A method or capacity supplied an output | The output preserves the intended meaning or satisfies the purpose |
| Connecting a result to further work | The consumer receives the subjects and qualifications it needs | The entire possibility space has been covered |
| Reusing the capability | The responsibilities remain available over the claimed variation | Every admitted problem has a positive solution |

These are distinguishable achievements, not a compulsory linear workflow. Their separation explains why neither a readable book nor a working example automatically supplies the intended future operability.

An intelligent human or learned system can remain a legitimate realizing means. What must not happen is to name “interpretation” as a black box and then claim that all the required semantic constitution has been supplied. The contribution needs a defined subject, result, grounds, permissible discretion and relationship to subsequent work. Where those remain undeveloped, the operative claim remains correspondingly limited.

## 4. Comparative investigations: meaning, interpretation and missing constitution

The following cases are deliberately different. They show variation within domains as well as between them. They are not a survey establishing how formal every field is.

Each case asks what the source already determines, what competent use adds, what a different interpretation changes, and what a more explicit constitution would have to supply.

### 4.1 Mathematics: exact structure can already be constituted

A many-sorted algebra specifies sorts of objects and operations with particular input and output sorts. An interpretation assigns a carrier to each sort and a function to each operation. Equations state relationships that the operations must satisfy. *Algebraic Theories* develops these definitions precisely [^M5].

For example, a directed graph can be given by a vertex carrier, an edge carrier and two maps assigning each edge its source and target. This allows distinct edges with identical endpoints. It is a different constitution from treating edges merely as a subset of vertex pairs. The distinction is explicitly mathematical, not left to the reader's sense of what a graph ought to be.

The same source defines how finite terms are generated from variables and operations, how they are interpreted, and what a map must preserve to respect the operations. Within that account, substantial formation and consequence are already exact. A reader is not free to replace an operation's arity or discard one of its equations while claiming the same theory.

What remains in an application is different work. If an edge is to represent a person's participation in an event, we must establish whether two participation occurrences with the same endpoints remain distinct; what the vertices represent; and which further roles, times or qualifications matter. The mathematics supplies definite possibilities. It does not choose their adequate domain interpretation by itself.

This contrast is important: the formalization problem is not always an absent calculus. It may lie in connecting an exact calculus to the intended subjects, or in discovering that the selected calculus lacks a required distinction. Mathematical exposition and discovery can also depend on learned interpretation, but that does not erase the exactness already achieved by its formal definitions.

Section 14 develops one such algebra into an actual generative construction, rather than leaving its relevance as a suggestion.

### 4.2 Psychology: the construct is not identical to its operational indicator

Deci and Ryan's account of intrinsic motivation combines theoretical concepts, behavioral observations, reported experience and experimental procedures. It explicitly warns against identifying the psychological construct with any one imperfect operational measure [^P4].

Consider three distinct subjects:

- A person performing an activity in an episode.
- The procedure and observations through which that episode is investigated.
- The interpretation of those observations under a psychological account.

A duration measurement concerns an observed engagement. A questionnaire answer concerns what was asked and what the participant understood it to ask. An attribution of motivation concerns a theoretical interpretation of the episode. These are related, but they are not the same object.

The source's enjoyment example exposes a precise insufficiency. Two records may contain the same high rating after the same rewarded task. In one interpretation, the answer concerns enjoyment of doing the activity; in another, enjoyment of receiving the reward. Their numerical equality does not make them equivalent evidence for activity-directed interest.

Similarly, persistence without an apparent external reward is not, in the source's account, conclusive by itself. Pressure and self-esteem concerns can motivate further investigation. That does not establish that one sign reveals a unique hidden cause, or that every episode belongs to exactly one mutually exclusive motivational category.

**What a deeper constitution would supply.** A bounded domain of research interpretation could distinguish:

| Constituent | Its meaningful relationship | Consequence for permitted use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activity and episode | What the inquiry concerns and which occurrence is under consideration | Evidence about another activity or time cannot silently answer this question |
| Observation or report | What was recorded, by whom or what, under which procedure and wording | A record remains distinguishable from an inferred psychological state |
| Interpreted referent | What the report is understood to concern, and the grounds of that binding | Enjoyment of a reward cannot be silently recast as enjoyment of the activity |
| Construct and indicator | The theoretical question and how a procedure provides evidence concerning it | A measure does not become the construct merely by being computable |
| Interpretation and alternatives | What is supported, under which account, and what has not been excluded | Uncertainty and competing explanations remain usable information |
| Causal claim | What intervention or relationship is claimed to explain an outcome | Temporal succession or a construct label alone does not establish the cause |

The relationships must have operative consequences. An unresolved report referent can produce a clarification request or an explicitly unresolved interpretation; it cannot legitimately enter a rule requiring activity-directed enjoyment as though that premise were known. A missing reward entry does not prove the absence of all relevant contingencies. A clarified report can alter an inference while leaving the original recorded answer intact.

That is already more than a vocabulary. It constitutes admissible uses, qualifications, generated questions and revision dependencies. It does not yet provide a validated numerical model of motivation. Developing such a model would require substantive empirical work, not a convenient choice of field type or threshold.

The insufficiency is thus located: the source makes the distinctions intelligible and provides some methods, but a reusable operative arrangement must bind them to actual subjects, preserve their different evidential roles and supply the specified interpretive and investigative contributions.

### 4.3 Instructional design: joining meanings creates a constructive problem

Keller explains relevance through people's perceived relationship to outcomes, ideas and other subjects in light of their goals, motives and values. Immediate practical usefulness is not the only possible relationship. His examples also distinguish valuing something for itself from valuing its contribution to another outcome [^P3].

Later, his discussion of instructional material distinguishes personal interest and topic association from contribution to a learning objective. A dramatic depiction of hurricane destruction can concern hurricanes without explaining how hurricanes form. The source reports research about such interesting but instructionally peripheral additions; the present argument uses the semantic distinction, not a universal empirical rule that every dramatic introduction is harmful.

Suppose the adopted objective is to explain hurricane formation. A designer considering two illustrations must do at least three different kinds of work:

1. Understand what the learner values or wants to know.
2. Understand the formation relationships that the lesson is to explain.
3. Determine what each candidate actually depicts or enables, and how it could participate in that explanation.

A tag such as “hurricanes” does not settle any of those relationships. A learner interested in survival stories and a learner interested in weather mechanisms may select the same photograph for different reasons. A photograph of damage may introduce why the subject matters without itself representing the formation process.

**The constructive subject is the connection among purposes, content and activity.** It may have to be designed. A learner's concern about safety can lead into a question about storm development, which can lead into a specified explanatory representation. Each passage needs grounds; the fact that the topics are associated does not establish that the proposed passage teaches the intended relationship.

A bounded definition of accountable lesson plans could constitute:

- An adopted instructional objective: which understanding or performance the plan is intended to enable.
- Attributed learner-valued purposes: whose expressed or inferred purposes they are, in what context and on what grounds.
- Candidate content with an interpreted subject: what the material depicts, states, exemplifies or asks the learner to do.
- Roles within the plan: introducing a question, explaining a relationship, exercising a distinction or assessing a performance.
- Connecting constructions: how a component in one role supplies what a subsequent component needs.
- Assessment subjects: which intended achievement a response or performance is evidence about.

For the illustration, an explanatory component must identify the formation relationships it presents; an assessment must concern those relationships; an introductory photograph must not discharge an explanatory obligation solely because it shares a topic. A bridge from a learner-valued purpose to the objective needs an intelligible intermediate achievement and a justified connection.

These rules can generate many candidate plans from different content and purposes. They can exclude a particular semantic substitution—topic match standing in for explanation—without claiming that every admitted plan will succeed pedagogically.

The resulting capability is not a stored relevance score. It includes forming, inspecting, combining and revising reasoned arrangements. The realizing means must actually interpret content or obtain that interpretation through a bounded contribution, build the required connections, preserve the adopted purpose and evaluate the resulting use.

A later high enjoyment rating does not automatically validate the plan. It may concern the introductory spectacle rather than the explanatory work. Thus joining design with evaluation creates a further necessity: preserve the relationship between the purpose that justified a component, the work it actually offered and the subject of the later evidence. This is an example of a new domain obligation created by combination.

#### A complete small connection, rather than a placeholder for one

A separate, self-contained illustration makes the constructive content exact without requiring a complete empirical theory of teaching.

Suppose an expressed learner purpose is to predict the number of fixed-size cells needed when enlarging a rectangular pattern. Adopt the instructional objective of explaining why scaling both row and column counts by \(k\) multiplies the cell count by \(k^2\), whereas scaling only one multiplies it by \(k\). The subject is a represented grid, not every physical consequence of enlarging an artifact.

Define a grid with \(r\) rows and \(c\) columns as

\[
G_{r,c}=\{1,\ldots,r\}\times\{1,\ldots,c\},
\qquad |G_{r,c}|=rc,
\]

for positive integers \(r,c\), with \(k\) also a positive integer in the proposed scaling. Its cells retain row–column positions. A diagram-producing rule draws one equal-sized cell for each pair, arranged in those rows and columns. The presentation thus has a specified correspondence to the mathematical subject; we have not assumed that any attractive picture of a larger pattern expresses this structure.

A lesson fragment can now be constituted:

| Part | Actual content of the connection |
| --- | --- |
| Learner-valued problem | Predict cell requirements before changing the two dimensions |
| Adopted explanatory target | Distinguish one-dimensional from two-dimensional scaling |
| Constructed representation | Grids for \((r,c)\), \((kr,c)\) and \((kr,kc)\), with their row–column correspondence retained |
| Explanatory passage | Cells are jointly indexed by two independent choices; their counts are \(rc\), \(krc\) and \(k^2rc\) |
| Learner activity | State which counts change when one or both dimensions change, and justify the difference using the two indices |
| Assessment subject | The supplied response's treatment of the one-factor/two-factor distinction, not enjoyment of the picture |

The connecting argument is now present: the wanted prediction concerns cell count; cell count is determined by the two indices; the proposed transformation changes one or both index ranges; the resulting products establish the required distinction. The representation, explanation and question all concern that same relationship.

For \(r=2,c=3,k=2\), the three counts are \(6,12,24\). More importantly, the construction supplies the general relationship for admitted positive integers rather than only these three answers.

Changing the purpose exposes its boundary. If “enlargement” instead means making each existing cell physically larger while retaining two rows and three columns, the represented cell count remains \(6\). The old instructional passage would answer the wrong question if it silently substituted additional cells for enlarged cells. The constitution therefore needs to retain **what changes and what is held fixed**, not just the word “enlargement.”

A reusable realization with the defined grid, transformation and presentation operations can construct new instances of this fragment without inventing the connection again. It can check a formal response against the adopted mathematical relationship, or require a bounded interpretation of an informal explanation. It cannot infer that the learner understands the general concept merely from a correct count, nor guarantee that the presentation causes learning.

This distinction is the constructive point: the plan's semantic connection and generative structure can be constituted exactly, while the learner's interpretation and the educational result remain subjects of appropriate inquiry.

### 4.4 Narrative: even identifying an event can depend on an interpretive organization

Altman's *A Theory of Narrative* examines how a definition can privilege one pattern of narrative organization and then influence what an interpreter recognizes in a text [^N1].

His discussion of a limerick contrasts an analysis organized around a sequence such as smiling, riding and returning with one that retains the different characters and their changed relationships. We need not endorse every part of his alternative reading to identify the constitution problem: selecting an action label as the organizing subject can discard who performs it and what changes between the participants.

Two occurrences of smiling need not represent one subject persisting unchanged. If an interpretation concerns a reversal between participants, the bearer of each action is consequential. Collapsing both occurrences into the same action type loses what that interpretation needs.

Altman also asks why the sun's shining can be atmosphere in one narrative and a salient action in another. Physical occurrence alone does not determine its narrational role. The represented world, the manner of telling and the adopted analytical question participate in that role.

A deeper constitution would therefore distinguish, where required:

- The textual or other representational material.
- The subjects and occurrences represented through it.
- The order of presentation and the order attributed to events.
- Characters, their action occurrences and relationships.
- An analytical account under which particular features are salient.
- An interpretation's claims and the passages or structures offered as grounds.
- A compositional requirement adopted for making a new narrative.

These are not interchangeable layers. A rule for composing one kind of story is not automatically a definition of everything that counts as narrative. An observer's attribution of agency is not simply the presence of a verb. A represented character is not identical to the performer or medium through which that character is presented.

**What more exact work could achieve.** An interpretation could explicitly retain the action–participant bindings and the contrast it claims to explain. A transformation could then be required to preserve those bindings or state how it changes them. Two interpretations could be compared on their actual commitments rather than flattened into the same event-label sequence. A production task could adopt a specified pattern as a requirement without declaring excluded patterns meaningless.

The unresolved task of selecting or evaluating an interpretation remains substantive. Formalization can articulate plural interpretations and their grounds rather than force one into an unqualified truth field. It can also reveal that a proposed reduction is inadequate for the intended question. That is a positive capability, even where no universal judge of narrative quality is supplied.

This source is one developed theoretical position, not evidence that all artistic or narrative work lacks formal methods. Its value here is demonstrating that the ontology selected for analysis can determine which meanings remain visible.

### 4.5 Design and engineering: explicit models still owe connections to their uses

Alexander's discussion of form and context identifies fit as a relationship within an ensemble, rather than an isolated property of an object. He also shows how changing the form–context boundary changes the design problem: redesigning a kettle, its heating method or the surrounding arrangement are different undertakings [^N2].

We use that distinction, not his historical diagnosis as a claim about the present completeness of design theory. “Fits its context” is intelligible, but its concrete assessment can depend on geometry, use, perception, production, conventions or several together. Merely adding a field named *fit* does not supply those relationships.

A technically sharper case appears in *Tall Building Design*. Under a specified rigid-diaphragm idealization, the relevant analytical representation reports no axial force for certain beams; the source warns against unqualified downstream use in design postprocessing [^A1]. The absence of a reported result under one model is not automatically an adequate answer to another activity's physical question.

The missing passage can require the subject correspondence, assumptions, loading context and applicability grounds. It may require additional work, not a different file format. The source already explains the hazard: the task is to preserve that knowledge in the operative relationship, not invent a deficiency in engineering.

*Collaborative Design Management* supplies another contrast. Shaft dimensions, door-set dimensions or service positions can depend on selections not yet made. The required input does not always pre-exist somewhere waiting to be fetched [^A2].

Three different dependencies can therefore be present:

- **Acquisition:** obtain and connect something already established.
- **Inquiry:** investigate what is not yet known, possibly retaining uncertainty.
- **Constitution:** select, design or form an arrangement or intermediate that further work requires.

A fact learned through inquiry and an arrangement made through design have different grounds. The examples also describe existing coordination practices. *The Building Systems Integration Handbook* similarly organizes integration questions while leaving substantive answers to design work [^A3]. Existing domains already contain part of the connective organization; a deeper definition must discover, preserve and extend it.

### 4.6 What the comparison establishes

| Case | Already supplied | Consequential work not supplied by the local representation alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sorted algebra | Exact operations, formation, equations and interpretation laws | Selecting and justifying its domain interpretation |
| Motivation research | Constructs, indicators, procedures and explicit cautions | Binding observations to subjects and forming warranted, qualified interpretations |
| Instructional relevance | Conceptual distinctions, examples and design guidance | Constructing a meaningful connection between purposes, content, activity and evaluation |
| Narrative interpretation | Analytical concepts, structures and argued readings | Constituting represented subjects and maintaining the distinctions an interpretation needs |
| Design and engineering | Models, calculations, integration methods and professional practices | Connecting assumptions, selections, contexts and results across their actual uses |

The common problem is not that all these fields are secretly the same or equally informal. It is that **connected capability depends on meaningful relationships which must be identified at the level where they actually do work**.

Current accounts can be deeply explanatory while delegating substantial determination to trained participants. They can also already formalize particular responsibilities exceptionally well. The future work must distinguish those situations and develop what each intended capability actually lacks.

## 5. What formal domain definition constitutes

**Formal domain definition is the work of giving a domain's relevant meanings, subjects and constructive possibilities an explicit, coherently connected constitution: what its objects and distinctions are, how they acquire their interpretation, which relationships and operations are meaningful, and what premises and laws govern their use and consequences at a declared scope.**

The phrase also names the resulting account at a particular stage. It can reconstruct inherited organization, integrate dispersed knowledge, constitute new subjects or define a domain for a new capability.

It is neither transcription of a book into symbols nor replacement of its concepts by a list of data types. It develops the relationships through which the subject becomes intelligible and operable.

### 5.1 Explanation, ontology, formal structure and interpretation

These responsibilities cooperate:

| Responsibility | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Explanation | Makes the subject, distinctions and their significance intelligible |
| Ontological constitution | Distinguishes what exists or is represented as a subject: identities, roles, events, processes, claims, possibilities and composites |
| Formal structure | Gives relevant formation, relations, operations and laws exact expression |
| Interpretation and grounding | Relates that structure to its intended subject, including empirical grounds or constitutive decisions |
| Realization | Supplies means of forming, using and maintaining the defined relationships |

“Formal” requires more than an unusually careful description. Where exact consequence or operation is claimed, the determining rules, premises and meanings must be sufficiently specified for that claim. Not every explanatory sentence needs to be symbolic, but an undefined judgment cannot acquire exactness merely by being given a formal-looking name.

Conversely, a formal structure can be mathematically impeccable and have the wrong interpretation. The account must explain why its objects and laws are the right ones for the work, not only that they are consistent.

### 5.2 Ontological depth follows the distinctions a use needs

The psychological case shows why a report may require more than a person, a score and a date. Its meaning can depend on the question asked, the episode, the interpreted referent and the grounds for that interpretation.

Those are different kinds of subject. A report is an occurrence of expression; its content is what is asserted or answered; an interpretation relates that expression to a proposed meaning; an inference uses that interpretation under premises. A revision of the interpretation need not change the identity or historical content of the report.

Similarly, a role is not necessarily a permanent type of its bearer. It can depend on an arrangement and interval. A capacity differs from its exercise; a proposed object differs from an actual one; an adopted requirement differs from an observed property; a process differs from a summary of its result.

These distinctions can create further obligations. If several interpretations concern one report, the definition needs their common reference and their differences. If one is revised, it needs to identify which conclusions depend on it. If interpretations can coexist, it needs a mode of comparison that does not silently force exclusivity.

**The depth lies in these relationships and their consequences, not in the number of named entities.** Some distinctions require objects; others require a better relation, dependent type, inference rule or account of context. No universal ontology is imposed here.

### 5.3 Different relations have different logical and constructive natures

A relation called “supports” might mean physical support, evidential support, satisfaction of a prerequisite or contribution to a purpose. These do not share one automatic calculus.

Physical support may enter a mechanical model. Evidential support may be defeasible and depend on alternatives. A prerequisite can determine whether an operation is admitted. A purposeful contribution may need a connecting argument and still fail empirically.

Likewise, “same” can mean numerical equality, identity of an occurrence, common reference, equivalent interpretation for a use or isomorphism of a formal organization. Choosing one changes which substitutions are justified.

Formal definition must establish the relevant relation's operands, scope, properties and allowed consequences. It may need to discover or create the instruments for doing so. A labelled edge in a graph is a representation of that responsibility, not its automatic fulfillment.

### 5.4 Different origins require different grounds

| Contribution | What is being done | What warrants it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Description | Representing selected features of an existing subject | Adequacy to the subject and intended use |
| Reconstruction | Making an inherited but dispersed relationship explicit | Sources, practices or other evidence supporting the reconstruction |
| Inquiry | Establishing something not already known | Appropriate observation, argument, experiment or proof |
| Adopted formal construction | Interpreting established mathematics in the domain | Its laws and the suitability of the interpretation |
| Connective design | Forming a relationship or intermediate for joint work | Coherent constitution and justification of its intended role |
| Constitutive decision | Adopting a purpose, requirement, convention or new formal object | Explicit authority, meaning and consequences of the decision |
| Realization | Exercising the defined responsibility | Correspondence between the defined meaning and actual behavior |

The definition can create a criterion for an adopted task. It cannot create an empirical fact by assigning that fact a formal representation. It can make uncertainty exact enough to reason with without making the uncertainty disappear.

### 5.5 Description, requirement, admission and achievement

“This relation holds,” “this relation is desired,” “this relation is required for a valid construction,” and “we do not know whether it holds” are different commitments.

A domain investigating failure must be able to represent failing cases. It should not accidentally define them out of existence by treating every evaluated requirement as a formation invariant.

A well-formed assessment can also report an adverse or unresolved result. A correctly formed narrative interpretation can still be contested. A permitted proposal can fail its purpose. The constitution must preserve these differences.

For productive capability, the definition additionally connects meaningful circumstances and requirements to available means and usable results. Where a reusable environment realizes those defining forms, the definition can determine its operative arrangement (§15). The hard work of semantic constitution belongs inside that passage, not in an unspecified act of reinterpretation after it.

## 6. Purpose, boundary and the constitution of a selected domain

### 6.1 Participation is not taxonomic membership

A productive undertaking draws upon relevant parts of several fields without turning each contributing field into one of its subfields. What matters is the contribution: which subjects, methods and results are needed, at what depth, and in which relationship to the intended whole.

The organization of these contributions can be called a *productive domain ecology*. This names the connected arrangement, not a prescribed mathematical object. The older sketches also propose *Terrain* for a wider organization from which such ecologies can be developed. That wider concept may be useful, but an inventory of available fields does not yet specify their relevance or compatibility for a selected undertaking. It is not a prerequisite for the present definition.

### 6.2 Deliberate and arbitrary scope

A deliberately constituted domain selects a connected subject and forms of possible work. Its intended purpose helps reveal what must be distinguishable and achievable. Purpose does not uniquely determine the means or establish that the proposed capability is attainable.

*Arbitrarily specified* means that the selected scope need not come from a fixed catalogue of disciplines or prebuilt products. It may combine inherited regions in new ways and require new connective structures. It does not make facts arbitrary or waive the need to justify the resulting organization.

Two constitutions can differ while satisfying the same purpose. Conversely, two purposes expressed in almost the same words may require materially different constitutions.

### 6.3 A conjunction can create a new subject

Consider an illustrative capability concerned with replacing parts of a system. Describing permissible replacement parts and describing acceptable ordinary operation separately may be adequate for two local responsibilities.

Now require replacement **while specified operations continue**. The new obligation concerns intermediate configurations: which parts are temporarily absent, what supports or routes remain available, which operations are affected, and what sequence preserves the required continuity.

The missing subject is not another isolated part. It is the evolving configuration under a jointly stated requirement. Its constitution may require a sequence, temporary arrangements, affected regions and a relation between each stage and the continuing operation.

This in turn affects the inputs of evaluation, the structure of a proposed plan and the evidence a later user needs. A small change in purpose can therefore create a chain of definitional consequences without changing the names of the contributing fields.

The mapping sketch gives this kind of reasoning a precise direction [^D1]. In general, the requirements of a conjunction cannot be assumed to equal a collection of requirements considered separately. The example establishes the possibility of additional joint obligations; it does not establish one universal algorithm for deriving them.

### 6.4 Dependency without unlimited absorption

A deep definition need not reproduce every contributing field. It must supply what the claimed work depends upon at an adequate level.

| Treatment of a dependency | What is supplied | What makes the boundary defensible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal constitution | The required objects, relations and laws are defined within this account | Their internal organization is needed for the claimed inquiry or activity |
| Exact external interface | A contribution remains elsewhere, but its inputs, outputs, assumptions and use are specified | The intended work does not require unrepresented internal distinctions |
| Represented constraint | A limit or requirement is carried with its scope and meaning | Applying it does not pretend to explain its entire originating field |
| Bounded external participation | A person, instrument or service performs a stated responsibility | Its promised contribution and the dependence on it remain explicit |
| Unresolved context | A consequential matter is identified but not yet supplied | Claims depending on its resolution remain qualified or open |
| Exclusion | A region is outside the declared account | The claimed capability does not covertly rely on it |

An interface is not an adequate boundary merely because it exists. If the next activity needs an internal distinction the interface discards, the boundary must be deepened or the claim revised.

Dependencies can also be generated during development. The discovery of a new requirement need not mean that the purpose expanded; it may reveal what the original purpose already required. Equally, complications caused by an unnecessarily elaborate modeling choice should not be presented as unavoidable domain necessities.

## 7. Objects, generated constituents and their interfaces

### 7.1 Define what the work is about

The objects that need definition include more than physical nouns. A comparison subject, a candidate arrangement, a requirement package, an interpretation of a measurement, or a family of supporting configurations can be indispensable.

A *hidden constituent* is a necessary distinction or structure absent from the current account. “Hidden” names its relation to that account, not a special metaphysical kind. Some such constituents are reconstructed from practice. Others arise because a new combination of responsibilities makes them necessary.

Not every hidden relationship needs a separately named object. Sometimes an existing object's constitution or a relation's scope is what needs repair. The chosen structure should explain which distinction or use it enables.

### 7.2 A connected construction, not a list of fields

Constituting the meaningful subject comes first: the examples in §4 show why neither a qualifying occurrence nor its interpretation can be assumed to emerge from a label. Once that work is supplied, further exact structure is needed to preserve its constituents and uses.

Consider a deliberately simple finite example at one fixed interpretation and stage of work.

Let \(A\) be assembly identities, \(C\) component identities, and \(S\) qualifying configuration occurrences. Each occurrence has an assembly and a component:

\[
a:S\to A,\qquad c:S\to C.
\]

Suppose the selected unit for this responsibility is an **assembly–component pair**, rather than one global component or one supporting occurrence. Then define

\[
U=\{(a(s),c(s))\mid s\in S\}\subseteq A\times C,
\qquad q:S\to U,\quad q(s)=(a(s),c(s)).
\]

Each unit's support family is

\[
S_u=\{s\in S\mid q(s)=u\}.
\]

The connecting laws include

\[
S=\bigcup_{u\in U}S_u,\qquad
u\ne v\ \Longrightarrow\ S_u\cap S_v=\varnothing,
\]

and

\[
\pi_A\circ q=a,\qquad \pi_C\circ q=c.
\]

The first laws say that the supports are grouped exactly by their selected unit. The latter say that accessing an assembly or component through the unit agrees with accessing it through the original configuration.

These equations are elementary consequences of the definitions. Their explanatory importance is that the composite consists of **objects together with exact relationships**, not unrelated exports called “assemblies,” “components” and “supports.”

### 7.3 Different consumers require different access

A consumer counting qualifying configurations uses \(|S|\). One counting assembly-specific selected units uses \(|U|\). One counting globally distinct selected components uses \(|c(S)|\). Those answers can differ.

A component can occur in several assemblies. Consequently, a unique assembly accessor on the global component family is not justified by this construction. The assembly-qualified unit carries information the global identity alone does not.

A different consumer might need every support of a unit, the selected units belonging to an assembly, or a value justified by each support. These are different interfaces. Access can be single-valued, partial, relation-valued or family-valued according to the constituted object.

The selection of pairs in this illustration is itself a domain decision. If two association occurrences with the same endpoints have distinct meanings, the pair loses information and must not serve as their complete identity. An occurrence identity and its endpoint maps may then be required.

### 7.4 Internal access and external use

Interfaces exist between generated constituents inside one construction as well as between separate activities. A support family can determine a selected family; that family can support a measurement; the measurement can participate in a further comparison.

Each passage must preserve what its consumer needs. A count cannot replace a family when later work asks which member changed. A family of identities cannot replace a family of supporting occurrences when the distinction between several grounds matters.

Some information can legitimately be forgotten for one use. That does not make the reduced result an adequate replacement for the richer object in every use.

This is why interface definition belongs to the domain's mathematical and conceptual constitution before it belongs to a software API.

## 8. Contexts, worlds and correspondence

### 8.1 Context can belong to an object's meaning

A statement may concern one candidate, one purpose, one revision, one observation method or one set of assumptions. Removing that context can change its subject or licensed use.

For example, an assessment of candidate \(x\) under context \(k\) is not simply an unqualified property of \(x\). One possible representation is a family of result spaces \(R(x,k)\), whose admissible members depend on both. In the research case, the activity, episode, procedure and adopted theoretical interpretation contribute distinct parts of that context. In the instructional case, changing the objective can change the role of the same material. A context is adequate only when it retains the dependencies of the intended use; the notation is not a substitute for discovering them.

Type-theoretic accounts of contexts and dependent families provide established instruments for such relationships [^M2]. They do not prove that every piece of context should be encoded by the same mechanism.

### 8.2 Several formal organizations can concern one domain

A domain may require several *worlds*: formal organizations in which particular kinds of object, relationship and activity have their place. One can emphasize functional organization, another geometric configuration, and another the objects of an external system.

A field need not correspond to exactly one world, and one world can combine knowledge from several fields. A world is not assumed to be one database or one software application.

The same subject can participate in different worlds without having identical local relationships. Alternatively, several distinct formal objects can represent one external subject. These cases require different correspondence accounts.

A claim of shared identity or population requires laws explaining which identity is shared, which occurrences correspond and what happens under change. Reusing a name is not such a law.

### 8.3 Shared identity, equality and correspondence

Numerically equal measurements need not be the same measurement occurrence. Two representations can concern the same component without containing the same properties. Two candidates can be equivalent for one comparison while differing for another.

The definition must specify the kind of sameness being used. Treating all sameness as matching identifiers loses these distinctions; treating every presentation as unrelated prevents lawful joint use.

A correspondence can preserve only a declared part of meaning. Its adequacy depends on what the receiving activity requires. Complete equivalence is a stronger claim than a useful one-way interpretation.

Model theory makes different strengths of preservation explicit [^M1]. A map preserving every existing edge need not preserve the absence of an edge; it can map a source with no edge into a target that has one. An observation about absence therefore needs more than positive-edge preservation. Which distinctions a passage must preserve follows from the actual questions and constructions it carries.

### 8.4 Joint meaning cannot always be recovered from pairwise views

A small mathematical example makes the loss explicit. Let three roles each take a value in \(\{0,1\}\), and consider the two families

\[
E=\{000,011,101,110\},\qquad
O=\{001,010,100,111\}.
\]

Both have four members. For either family, projection onto any two roles yields all four possible pairs. Yet the triple \(000\) belongs to \(E\) and not to \(O\).

Thus equal counts and even complete pairwise projections do not determine the original joint family. A later activity concerned with the permitted triple needs the triple relationship.

This is an explanatory counterexample, not a claim that ordinary domains should use binary encodings. It establishes why joint configuration can carry meaning that its constituent summaries cannot recover.

Where local accounts are to form a whole, the definition must therefore supply the appropriate overlap, compatibility and assembly relationships. Sheaves and related constructions offer powerful instruments when their premises fit; they are not automatic names for every multi-world arrangement. The fact that compatible local data glue under a specified mathematical structure does not establish that a given domain has that structure.

### 8.5 When a representation is sufficient for a question

The report example in §4.2 permits a precise statement of information loss.

Let \(W\) be a stated family of interpreted cases, and let \(p:W\to X\) retain only the information present in a coarser representation. Let \(q:W\to Y\) give the answer to a particular, already defined question.

A procedure using only \(X\) can reproduce that answer for every represented case only if there is a function \(\bar q:p(W)\to Y\) such that

\[
q=\bar q\circ p.
\]

Here the composition regards \(p\) as a map onto its image \(p(W)\). Such a function exists **exactly when**

\[
p(w_1)=p(w_2)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q(w_1)=q(w_2).
\]

The necessity follows because a function gives the same answer on the same input. Conversely, if the implication holds, assigning \(\bar q(p(w))=q(w)\) is well-defined: any other case with that same representation gives the same answer.

This simple result locates a substantive limit. Suppose the coarse record retains the enjoyment score and episode but discards what the answer concerns. Two interpreted cases can have that same coarse record while differing on the question “does this report concern enjoyment of the activity?” No procedure receiving only that record can always recover the lost distinction. Increasing computational power does not change this impossibility under the stated information boundary.

A learned system may supply an inference from further context, learned regularities or a prior model. That is an additional contribution with its own grounds; it is not evidence that the original record already determined the answer.

The example's question concerns the report's referent, not a claim that identifying that referent settles the person's actual motivation. It therefore isolates one real dependency without pretending to solve the larger psychological inquiry.

For a family of intended questions \(\mathcal Q\), define

\[
w_1\sim_{\mathcal Q}w_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q(w_1)=q(w_2)\ \text{for every }q\in\mathcal Q.
\]

A representation is adequate for these questions only if it never merges cases that this family distinguishes. Enlarging \(\mathcal Q\) can make a previously sufficient representation insufficient. A domain concerned only with overall participation can need fewer distinctions than one concerned with what was enjoyed, learned or caused.

This explains why a seemingly small change in intended capability can require a deeper ontology. The extra structure is not demanded by a taste for complexity; it preserves differences on which the new work depends.

The construction assumes that the cases and questions have already received a determinate interpretation. Where a question legitimately returns alternatives, partial information or a qualified judgment, \(Y\) must represent that result structure appropriately. The theorem does not provide a universal method for discovering the right case space or questions. It does provide a criterion with which a proposed representation can be challenged.

### 8.6 Common context is a constituent of composition

Suppose two assessment families \(A\) and \(B\) both concern subjects in a context family \(K\), through maps \(s_A:A\to K\) and \(s_B:B\to K\). Their same-context pairs form

\[
A\times_K B
=\{(a,b)\in A\times B\mid s_A(a)=s_B(b)\}.
\]

The two projections recover the assessments, and their subject maps agree. Moreover, any other object supplying an \(A\)-assessment and a \(B\)-assessment with the same context maps uniquely into this paired object by taking the pair. This is the pullback's universal property in sets.

Its domain significance is concrete: jointly using two assessments requires a common subject, not merely two values with compatible data types. The context might include candidate revision, criterion interpretation and observation basis. Those distinctions must be selected because the joint use needs them.

The construction enforces agreement on the context that was actually defined. If \(K\) records only a candidate's name and omits its revision, the pullback will faithfully enforce an inadequate equality. Nor does common context make inconsistent judgments consistent or make either assessment true. Those are additional responsibilities.

This distinction—mathematically exact composition versus adequacy of what is being composed—will be developed through a complete generative example in §14.

## 9. Constituting possible work and its difficult middle

### 9.1 A task is more than its title

A responsibility such as “compare,” “integrate,” “verify” or “design” remains underdefined if its subject, criteria and achieved result cannot be distinguished.

An adequate account explains what is to be accomplished, over which admitted subjects and circumstances, with which consequential choices, preserving what, and yielding which kinds of result. It can leave several methods or outcomes possible where the responsibility permits them.

For an input organization \(i\), let \(O(i)\) be the possible result structures. A transformation specification may be a relation

\[
T\subseteq\{(i,o)\mid i\text{ is admitted and }o\in O(i)\}.
\]

Writing the relation does not define its substantive content. That work lies in explaining which pairs belong to it and why. Nor does the specification itself supply a method for constructing an appropriate \(o\).

### 9.2 Inputs, rules and outputs do not eliminate the work between them

Consider an illustrative responsibility to reconcile numerical requirements for the same finite family under the same interpretation.

Let \(L\) be a nonempty finite family of lower bounds and \(H\) a nonempty finite family of upper bounds. Set

\[
\ell=\max L,\qquad h=\min H.
\]

There is an integer \(n\) satisfying these interval requirements exactly when \(\ell\le h\), assuming the bounds themselves are nonnegative integers. If \(\ell>h\), the requirements are incompatible. If \(H\) is absent, its absence is not an upper bound of zero.

This calculation becomes domain work only after its subject is established. Do the bounds concern the same family? Do they apply simultaneously or to alternatives? Are they requirements or preferences? Has an ambiguous quantity been interpreted? These questions determine whether aggregation is meaningful.

Even interval consistency does not establish that the full intended arrangement is realizable. Additional structural or external constraints may rule it out.

### 9.3 Combining rules requires joint premises

Suppose two families \(X\) and \(Y\) have a justified inclusion \(X\subseteq Y\). A lower bound \(|X|\ge 5\) and upper bound \(|Y|\le 3\) conflict. Without the inclusion—or if the bounds concern different contexts—the two numbers alone do not establish that conclusion.

Similarly, lower bounds for overlapping families cannot simply be added as if the families were disjoint. A shared member, an alternative interpretation or a different unit of counting changes the inference.

The responsibility must therefore include the interpretation and joint applicability of the rules, not only each rule in isolation. The mapping sketch's requirement-reconciliation example supplies the direction for this illustration [^D1]; the present formulation is a general explanatory construction.

### 9.4 Results must express what was actually achieved

A result might be a consistent interval, a supported incompatibility, a set of alternatives, or a question whose unresolved interpretation prevents determination.

A justified incompatibility is a successfully formed domain result, even though it is adverse to the proposed arrangement. An execution error is different. So is a timeout during a search that has not established impossibility.

The incompatible result should preserve the decisive requirements, their common subject and the inclusion or other grounds that licensed the conclusion. A Boolean value alone cannot support a later responsibility that must decide which premise to reconsider.

Nor does detecting a conflict authorize the system to decide which requirement should be weakened. That is a different responsibility unless expressly included.

### 9.5 Means, discretion and composition

A defined responsibility can be realized through exact calculation, search, interpretation, design, a learned capacity, a human contribution or a combination. The account must specify what each contribution owes and what constrains its result. Naming a sophisticated capacity does not supply missing domain semantics.

Order can matter. Interpreting before aggregating, changing a configuration before evaluating it, and evaluating before selecting a revision can produce different meanings. Parallel execution is appropriate only where the relevant dependencies and effects permit it.

These are domain questions about lawful composition. They cannot be settled by drawing arrows between activity names alone.

## 10. From represented organization to input and usable product

### 10.1 Three related organizations

The organization of the represented domain, of an input for a particular responsibility, and of its result for later use need not have the same shape.

| Organization | What it must make available |
| --- | --- |
| Domain organization | The objects, contexts and relationships through which the subject is represented |
| Input organization | The particular bound configuration, evidence, alternatives and premises required for this work |
| Result organization | The achievement, its constituents and qualifications, and the relationships needed by later uses |

An input is not automatically “all relevant facts” retrieved as independent records. It may require retaining which evidence concerns which candidate and requirement, which objects share a context, and which combinations are alternatives rather than simultaneous commitments.

Likewise, a product need not repeat the input. It must express the achieved transformation in a form adequate to its receiving uses.

### 10.2 Constitute the question before checking its answer

If a responsibility must assess every required aspect of a proposal, it needs an account of the required aspect family. Defining that family as “whatever assessments have arrived” allows missing assessments to disappear from the apparent coverage.

Coverage, agreement and favorable conclusions are different. Every required question may have an answer while those answers conflict. A complete adverse account may be more usable than an apparently positive one that silently omits a necessary question.

This is one reason why task formation and sensing cannot be reduced to counting existing outputs.

### 10.3 The passage itself has content

Suppose activity \(P\) produces \(o\), and activity \(Q\) needs an input \(i\). Their passage may require an additional context \(k\) and can be expressed as

\[
H_{P,Q}\subseteq O_P\times K\times I_Q.
\]

The relation allows additional binding and multiple legitimate uses. A function or partial function is appropriate only where the passage actually has that character.

The definition must establish which result, context and input triples are legitimate. It may require a correspondence between subjects, compatible assumptions or additional material. Merely accepting the same data type is insufficient, as the analytical example in §4.5 illustrates.

A passage can legitimately reduce information when its consumer does not need it. If the consumer needs grounds that the producer discarded, adding a wrapper later cannot recover them. The deficient result constitution or passage must be repaired.

### 10.4 Entry, clarification and continuing use

A responsibility intended to form a criterion cannot require that completed criterion as its own initial input. Its entry conditions must distinguish what is required to begin from what it is supposed to produce.

A clarification request similarly needs a return relationship: which question was answered, for which subject, and how the answer alters the pending work. The mere existence of a reply does not settle those matters.

Products can become inputs to many later responsibilities, create new subjects, or change which work is appropriate. Their use need not be a fixed linear workflow. A reusable constitution must make those possibilities intelligible without requiring every future occurrence to be enumerated.

## 11. Situations, change and the continued meaning of results

### 11.1 Recognition has a defined subject

An operative domain must be able to distinguish the situations on which its claimed work depends. A relevant situation may concern a single property, a jointly bound configuration, the absence of a required contribution, a changed relationship or a structure formed by earlier work.

Recognition requires an account of the operands and their relationships. It is not enough to say that “a condition becomes true” if the account does not establish what is being observed and how that observation is related to the intended responsibility.

The repertoire of observations should follow the domain's needs and available means. A small set of compositional primitives may support many complex observations; a large list of named checks may still miss a necessary distinction. Neither number is a measure of general coverage.

### 11.2 False, empty and unavailable

An empty, well-defined selected family can legitimately have cardinality zero. An unavailable selection cannot be counted as zero merely because no result was supplied.

A false answer to a well-formed question also differs from an inability to form that question, or from contradictory information about it. Whether each distinction is necessary depends on its consequences for the particular work.

For example, a requirement covering every applicable member cannot silently omit a member whose assessment is unavailable. That would change the question into one concerning only the available assessments.

This does not prescribe one universal status vocabulary. It requires an explicit account of formation, availability and use wherever their differences alter the capability.

### 11.3 Equal summaries can conceal consequential change

Return to the constituent construction in §7. If one of several supports for a selected unit disappears, the unit can remain selected. A consumer concerned only with selection may have an unchanged result; a consumer concerned with its grounds may need to respond.

Conversely, the selected family can lose one member and gain another while its count stays constant. A count-based observation remains unchanged even though an identity-sensitive comparison does not.

A reference or interpretation can also change without any change to the represented object. A difference measured against a revised baseline may change, while a measurement of the current subject remains the same.

These are distinct dependencies. A single undifferentiated “changed” marker cannot by itself explain which consumers are affected.

### 11.4 Identity and applicability through change

A later state can preserve an object's identity, form a successor, or create a distinct object. The appropriate account depends on the subject and the work, not merely on whether a record identifier was reused.

Similarly, a result can remain historically true of the state from which it was formed while ceasing to apply to the present state. The account must distinguish historical grounds from current entitlement to use the result.

A useful dependency identifies the relevant relationship, not just chronology. Changing an unrelated description need not defeat a quantitative conclusion; changing a premise can defeat it even if the final reported value happens to remain numerically equal. Establishing non-impact is itself a claim requiring grounds where consequential.

### 11.5 Internal validity and faithful observation

A formal account can maintain its own represented states consistently while missing a relevant external event. Where external change matters, the realizing arrangement must provide an adequate acquisition or observation relationship.

The constitution must explain what changes are relevant, how they enter the represented domain, and what they affect. It must also identify any lag, uncertainty or missing observation that limits the claim. A coherent formal world is not, merely by being coherent, a complete account of its external subject.

## 12. Possibility, occurrence and reusable capability

### 12.1 Four different questions of size

A finite specification can admit infinitely many finite constructions. A domain with infinitely many possible configurations can currently contain only finitely many instances. A finite current population can permit several possible histories.

It is therefore necessary to distinguish:

1. The size of the defining account.
2. The family of admitted structures or circumstances.
3. The actual represented population or present case.
4. The possible transformations and histories.

A bounded subject is not automatically a finite set, and a finite set of object kinds does not imply finitely many possible combinations. Nor does an infinite possibility space establish the resources to realize all its members at once.

### 12.2 One result and a standing capacity

Defining or producing one successful arrangement differs from establishing a capability over an admitted class of arrangements.

The standing account must identify what can vary: requirements, populations, contexts, compositions, intermediate results and applicable courses of work. General formation and composition laws can account for cases that were not individually listed during design.

For example, a rule can identify an applicable family from the current population. The definition of that rule can remain fixed while the selected family and the work it makes appropriate change at each occurrence.

The invariant is the defined meaning and capability—not one repeated answer.

### 12.3 Admission, existence and achievement

Representing a requirement does not prove that it has a satisfying result. Establishing that some result exists does not by itself provide a way to find it. Providing a method does not establish that it succeeds over every admitted case.

A capability claim must therefore state the relation among admitted requirements, possible outputs and available means. Some admitted inputs may legitimately yield an incompatibility account or an unresolved question. That is adequate only where such an outcome satisfies the stated responsibility; it cannot silently replace a stronger promise to produce a positive result.

General reach needs grounds appropriate to its scope. Examples can expose failures and show particular successes. A universal assertion requires more than a collection of favorable cases.

## 13. Mapping as discovery, reconstruction and constitution

### 13.1 Mapping and the resulting definition

*Domain mapping*, in the methodological sense developed here, is the work of identifying, reconstructing, relating and, where necessary, developing the organization through which a domain receives an adequate account. *Formal domain definition* concerns giving that account the exact constitution required for its claims and uses.

They are connected activities, not impermeable phases. A map can contain exact definitions. Formal work can reveal that the initial mapping selected the wrong subjects or missed a decisive relationship.

Mapping an inherited subject and creating a domain for a new capability also have different responsibilities. The former must be faithful about what is already there; the latter must justify what it introduces and why its proposed organization supports the intended work.

### 13.2 Two directions of investigation

The first direction begins with actual practice or a source account. Reconstruct how its terms acquire their subjects, which contrasts make them intelligible, what a competent participant supplies, and how that contribution changes a judgment or construction. The cases in §4 demonstrate this route: a purpose, a report's referent or an analytical choice can be the decisive relationship, even before formal operations begin.

The second begins with a proposed explicit structure: ask what responsibility it performs and how that responsibility is handled today. It may already be formalized, distributed across sources, supplied by practice, newly needed for this undertaking, or unresolved.

These directions constrain one another. Existing practice prevents speculative mathematics from inventing universal deficiencies. Proposed deeper structures can reveal that an apparently simple activity relies on a substantial unarticulated connection.

A useful discipline is to hold the available description fixed and vary a plausible interpretation or context. If the warranted result changes, ask whether the proposed constitution preserves what made the difference. Section 8.5 makes one version of that test exact. Conversely, if a new object or relation changes no relevant interpretation, construction or preservation obligation, its necessity has not yet been shown.

### 13.3 A productive recursive step

A useful development step can:

- Introduce a missing subject or distinguish two previously conflated subjects.
- Repair an interpretation, interface or identity account.
- Establish a transformation and its premises.
- Discover an incompatibility in the proposed purpose.
- Locate an existing instrument that discharges a responsibility.
- Expose a limit in the realizing means.
- Revise the scope openly where the original claim cannot be sustained.

Development is not necessarily additive. An improved definition can remove an unnecessary object, replace a misleading relation or reject a proposed construction. It is also not circular justification: a conjectured constituent does not acquire warrant merely because other conjectures were built upon it.

The purpose can remain unchanged while its previously unseen implications require substantial new structure. Conversely, a deliberate change in purpose should be distinguished from discovering what the existing purpose required.

### 13.4 Sources and the discovery of new knowledge

Not all required knowledge will occur explicitly in a source. Work can preserve inherited knowledge, reconstruct implicit relationships, investigate a previously unanswered question, connect separate accounts, or create a new domain-specific structure. Discovering a property and constituting a design or representational object have different grounds (§§4.5 and 5.4).

Those contributions should remain distinguishable. Novelty relative to this account is not automatically historical novelty. A relationship may be new to the map while long familiar to practitioners.

Source selection must develop with the questions. A newly discovered dependency can make a previously peripheral passage central. Contrary evidence should be investigated when it challenges a consequential premise, not filtered out because it complicates the preferred account.

### 13.5 Scope discipline without premature closure

A bounded goal can induce a large development burden. It does not establish that the mapping will terminate after a fixed number of iterations or that one uniquely minimal adequate domain exists.

A proposed addition earns its place by explaining its relationship to the stated capability or a justified dependency. Where several constructions can discharge that responsibility, the account should preserve the distinction between the required achievement and the chosen means.

If a needed distinction cannot yet be defined adequately, identify what it prevents us from claiming. Calling it future work is informative only when its consequence is clear.

## 14. Mathematical constitution: from primitive meanings to generated capability

Mathematics can develop the missing structure, not merely encode an already completed vocabulary. It can define possible formation, establish what composition preserves, expose information loss and show that apparently compatible contributions cannot perform the intended work.

The following construction goes beyond listing instruments. It develops a small, exact possibility space and its interpretation. Its scope is deliberately limited so that the claims can be explained and justified.

### 14.1 The domain problem: a jointly meaningful assessment

Consider a responsibility to revise a represented proposal, assess two requirements and form a decision retaining its grounds.

The words do not determine what a valid composite is. Both assessments must concern the same proposal revision under the same adopted criteria and relevant assumptions. Their individual correctness is not enough.

For a concrete witness, let an original numerical proposal have mass \(8\) and space demand \(12\), with both corresponding limits \(10\). It passes the first requirement and fails the second. A revision has mass \(12\) and space demand \(8\); it fails the first and passes the second.

Combining the original mass assessment with the revised space assessment gives two passes, but **no one revision passes both**. The error is in the constituted relationship, not in either numerical comparison.

This is an illustrative domain of represented proposals, not engineering advice or proof that those numerical proposals can be physically constructed. It isolates a general responsibility also visible when an instructional plan's purpose, content and evaluation must remain correctly bound.

### 14.2 Constitute the subjects, operations and laws

Let \(Q\) be the sort of assessment requests. A request identifies the proposal revision, versioned criteria, relevant represented evidence and adopted interpretation. Equality of requests means equality of that complete subject for this bounded use, not merely matching proposal names.

Use additional sorts \(E\) for edits, \(A_1,A_2\) for the two assessment kinds, \(P\) for a common-subject package, \(D\) for a decision retaining that package, and \(V\) for verdicts.

For this construction, the represented inputs are immutable and the operations are total on their admitted carriers. These premises matter: a partial operation, live observation or consumable resource requires an appropriately different account.

| Operation | Type | Defined responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(\operatorname{revise}\) | \(Q\times E\to Q\) | Form the revised represented request |
| \(\operatorname{assess}_i\) | \(Q\to A_i\) | Perform the specified assessment for requirement \(i\) |
| \(\operatorname{subject}_i\) | \(A_i\to Q\) | Recover its complete subject |
| \(\operatorname{package}\) | \(Q\to P\) | Form both assessments as one coherent package |
| \(\operatorname{request}\) | \(P\to Q\) | Recover the package's subject |
| \(\operatorname{part}_i\) | \(P\to A_i\) | Access its assessment constituents |
| \(\operatorname{decide}\) | \(P\to D\) | Form a decision under the adopted reconciliation procedure |
| \(\operatorname{grounds}\) | \(D\to P\) | Recover the package supporting the decision |
| \(\operatorname{verdict}\) | \(D\to V\) | Access its judgment |

For \(i=1,2\), impose

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{subject}_i(\operatorname{assess}_i(q))&=q,\\
\operatorname{request}(\operatorname{package}(q))&=q,\\
\operatorname{part}_i(\operatorname{package}(q))&=\operatorname{assess}_i(q),\\
\operatorname{subject}_i(\operatorname{part}_i(p))&=\operatorname{request}(p),\\
\operatorname{grounds}(\operatorname{decide}(p))&=p,
\end{aligned}
\]

for every \(q:Q\) and \(p:P\) where those variables occur.

These laws give the interfaces content. The package's parts share its subject; a generated decision retains its grounds. An arbitrary pair of separately obtained assessments is not automatically a package. Their common subject must be established, as in §8.6, or a separate checked formation must supply that guarantee.

The laws are adopted commitments of this example. They do not follow from naming something an assessment, and they do not yet establish the substantive accuracy of its judgment.

### 14.3 A finite constitution can generate indefinitely many constructions

A many-sorted signature fixes the sorts and operation types. Its terms are generated inductively: a suitably typed input variable is a term; applying an operation to terms of its input sorts produces a term of its output sort [^M5].

For example,

\[
\operatorname{decide}\bigl(\operatorname{package}(
\operatorname{revise}(\operatorname{revise}(q,e_1),e_2))\bigr)
\]

is a constructed decision, not an item that had to be individually listed.

For any finite supplied sequence of edits, let

\[
q_0=q,\qquad q_{j+1}=\operatorname{revise}(q_j,e_{j+1}),
\qquad d_j=\operatorname{decide}(\operatorname{package}(q_j)).
\]

The laws give, for every such generated decision,

\[
\operatorname{request}(\operatorname{grounds}(d_j))=q_j,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{subject}_i(
\operatorname{part}_i(\operatorname{grounds}(d_j)))=q_j.
\]

Substituting the grounds law recovers the generated package; substituting its request or part laws recovers the request or its corresponding assessment. Thus the guarantee follows for every finite construction in this family.

The finite definition has supplied an unbounded family of possible constructions with a reusable compositional property. It has not enumerated all instances, promised an infinite execution or proved that every revised proposal satisfies its requirements.

Equations can also identify differently written constructions. Accessing the request of a package formed from \(q\) gives \(q\); inserting that access inside a larger expression cannot change the larger construction's meaning. This requires equality to be respected under substitution and surrounding operations, not only at the outermost expression.

### 14.4 The categorical structure has a definite job

The construction yields a category whose:

- Objects are finite lists of input or output sorts.
- Arrows are tuples of suitably typed terms, identified according to the imposed equations and their congruence consequences.
- Identity arrows are the corresponding variables.
- Composition substitutes the outputs of one tuple into the inputs of another.
- Products concatenate contexts and provide their projections.

Substitution supplies associative composition and identities. Respect for equations ensures that replacing a term by an equal representative does not alter the composite. This is the relevant algebraic-theory construction, not a category inferred from a diagram of vaguely connected activities [^M5].

An interpretation assigns carriers and operations satisfying the laws. Equivalently, in this setting it gives a product-preserving interpretation of the term category in sets. If \(F\) is a revision construction and \(G\) an assessment-and-decision construction, then

\[
M(G\circ F)=M(G)\circ M(F).
\]

The equation says exactly why lawful primitive realization can determine the meaning of their generated composites. It is one rigorous basis for deriving an operative arrangement from a definition.

The Cartesian account permits reuse of immutable information. It does not license duplicating a physical resource or treating an effectful observation as though it were a pure value. Where those differences matter, the domain's operation theory must express them.

### 14.5 Structural lawfulness does not supply the primitive meanings

An explicit model shows both what has been achieved and what remains substantive.

For the numerical illustration, let a request be

\[
q=(\rho,m,w,L_m,L_w),
\]

with revision identity \(\rho\), nonnegative rational represented quantities \(m,w\), and their corresponding limits, under fixed units and conventions. An edit assigns new represented quantities and records a successor revision; it does not promise a physical means of achieving them.

Define

\[
\operatorname{assess}_1(q)=(q,[m\le L_m]),\qquad
\operatorname{assess}_2(q)=(q,[w\le L_w]),
\]

where the brackets return pass or fail. Assessments retain \(q\). Packages contain assessments with that common subject. Generated packages use these two procedures. Decisions retain their package and are favorable exactly when both judgments pass.

All the displayed interface laws hold. The primitive interpretations additionally determine what the two comparisons actually mean.

Now keep the types, projections and subject binding unchanged, but interpret the first assessor as comparing \(w\) with \(L_w\), just like the second; generated packages use this newly interpreted assessor. **Every displayed interface law still holds.** Yet the revised proposal with mass \(12\), space demand \(8\) and limits \(10\) receives two passes, contrary to the intended mass assessment.

This is not a failure of names, types or connections. The wrong substantive operation was assigned to a correctly connected role.

Excluding that interpretation requires the domain constitution to determine the quantity, criterion, unit, subject and comparison that the role means. For this toy model the inequalities supply that content exactly. For a psychological or narrative judgment, the needed content and grounds are different and may require deeper conceptual or investigative work.

There is also a distinction between **a package generated by the specified assessors** and **an arbitrary imported package whose fields share a subject**. Shared subject does not make imported judgments accurate. Admission, validation or preserved derivation must establish whatever authenticity the receiving responsibility requires.

The example therefore supplies more than a warning: it exhibits a lawful but substantively wrong model and identifies the precise missing commitment.

### 14.6 What faithful passage preserves across every generated construction

Let \(M\) and \(N\) be two interpretations. Suppose there is a map \(h_s:M_s\to N_s\) for each sort and, for every primitive operation \(\sigma:s_1\cdots s_k\to s\),

\[
h_s(\sigma^M(x_1,\ldots,x_k))
=\sigma^N(h_{s_1}(x_1),\ldots,h_{s_k}(x_k)).
\]

Then the same preservation holds for every generated term. The proof is structural: it holds for variables; if it holds for all operand terms, the displayed primitive law gives it for their compound term. Therefore

\[
h_s(\llbracket t\rrbracket_M(\gamma))
=\llbracket t\rrbracket_N(h\gamma)
\]

for each well-typed term \(t\) and input assignment \(\gamma\).

This is a reusable result. We do not need to invent a new semantic bridge for each nesting depth.

However, operation preservation alone need not retain every distinction: a homomorphism may merge objects. If a use distinguishes revisions or verdicts, the correspondence must retain those distinctions as well. Section 8.5 gives the relevant question-sensitive test. A useful reduction is not automatically a faithful substitute for every use.

The result also presupposes that the primitive interpretations have the intended domain meaning. It propagates justified correspondence; it does not manufacture that justification from the syntax.

### 14.7 Context, transformation and local-to-global constitution

Other domain needs can require other structures.

Dependent types make the constitution of one object depend on another. Instead of a generic assessment plus an informal expectation that it concerns \(q\), a family \(A(q)\) can make that subject part of its formation. Contexts, substitution and their categorical treatment supply precise instruments for this organization [^M2]. The domain still determines what belongs in \(q\) and which changes permit transport.

Graph transformation supplies a different kind of precision: a transformation has a match, created and deleted parts, a retained interface and application conditions. These can determine the consequences of replacing something in a surrounding configuration [^M3]. That is more than treating an edit as an unqualified arrow.

A simple local-to-global construction makes another responsibility explicit. Let \(I\) be a family of features and \(D_i\) the admitted values of feature \(i\). For each subset \(U\subseteq I\), let

\[
F(U)=\prod_{i\in U}D_i.
\]

Restriction from \(U\) to a smaller \(V\) forgets the other coordinates. Restricting twice agrees with restricting directly. If subsets \(U_j\) cover \(U\), and assignments on them agree on overlaps, they determine a unique assignment on \(U\): take each coordinate from any local assignment containing it. Agreement makes the choice well-defined; coverage supplies every coordinate.

This is an actual gluing property, of the kind formalized by sheaf theory [^M6]. It can support the assembly of compatible local presentations.

But if a domain admits only assignments satisfying an additional joint constraint, gluing raw values does not prove that the assembled assignment satisfies it. The parity example in §8.4 exposes precisely that danger. The domain must determine which constraints are local to the chosen covering, what further global checking or construction is required, or whether a different notion of covering is appropriate.

The mathematics consequently identifies a decision that “connect the worlds” leaves hidden: what constitutes sufficient local coverage of a global responsibility?

Caramello's theory of bridges goes further in relating mathematical presentations through classifying structures under exact premises [^M4]. That provides a serious direction for relating different formal accounts. It is not a license to declare any conceptual translation an equivalence. The relevant theories, interpretations, invariants and correspondence must actually be developed.

### 14.8 Mathematical instruments expand what can be constituted

The preceding constructions have different achievements: detecting lost distinctions; constituting common-subject composites; generating lawful terms; propagating faithful interpretation; and assembling compatible local information.

They show how mathematics can help discover the required domain, not only encode it afterwards. A demand for a result can expose a missing context; a desired composition can reveal a necessary intermediate; a proposed representation can be shown incapable of answering a required question.

Other responsibilities may require richer logics, models of uncertainty, temporal or behavioral structures, geometry, resource-sensitive composition or new combinations of instruments. Their adoption must answer an actual domain problem.

The direction is not to fit all domains into this small algebra. It is to develop the appropriate formal instruments deeply enough that the domain's meanings, possible constructions and warranted uses become explicit rather than being deferred to suggestive names.


## 15. From definition to derived operability

### 15.1 The positive connection

A completed domain definition need not remain an inert description awaiting a new hand-written implementation for every domain.

**Where a realizing environment already implements the admitted defining forms faithfully, a sufficiently complete definition in those forms can determine the operative structures through which that domain is instantiated and exercised.**

The reusable environment supplies general powers. The particular definition supplies domain commitments: its objects, relationships, possible work, observations, interfaces and intended effects. Their combination can derive a domain-specific operative arrangement without inventing those meanings again downstream.

The underlying realization may involve interpretation, generation, installation or a combination. No particular programming language, database or folder arrangement is implied here.

The algebraic construction in §14 shows one exact form of this possibility. Once the request meaning, primitive assessments, package laws and verdict procedure are properly constituted, a faithful evaluator can realize a newly declared finite composite by applying those operations with the declared bindings. It need not invent a bespoke meaning for each revision depth. The structural induction explains why the primitive correspondence extends to every generated term.

The definition must therefore supply more than the operation name “assess.” In the numerical example it specifies the actual subject, quantities, criteria and comparisons. In a domain involving interpretive assessment, it must constitute the corresponding responsibility and its admitted results, including uncertainty or a need for further inquiry where appropriate. A reusable environment may itself need additional realizing powers before that defining form becomes supported.

The finite family construction in §7 illustrates the same relationship at another level. General means for maps, images and fibers can derive selected units and supporting families from the represented population. The domain definition still determines qualification and the intended unit of identity. Generic set operations cannot discover those meanings by themselves.

The instructional example in §4.3 reveals both the positive passage and its semantic boundary. When a diagram is generated from the defined grid by the stated cell correspondence, that particular relationship between presentation and subject is supplied by construction; it need not be guessed again from an unfamiliar picture. An environment might also be able to form plans, preserve objective–component–assessment relationships and detect missing obligations. That does not imply it can independently understand what an unfamiliar diagram explains. The latter contribution must be supplied, with an interpreted subject, grounds and limits that the other constructions can use. If it remains human-provided, the capability includes that dependence; if a reliable realizing capacity is developed, the same defined responsibility can gain a different realization without silently changing its meaning.

Direct operability thus means **a faithful passage from adequate definitions and available powers to their exercise**. It does not mean that a thin declaration automatically generates the understanding the declaration omitted.

### 15.2 What the connection must carry

A faithful realization must carry more than names or a static object inventory.

| Defined responsibility | What its operative realization must preserve or exercise |
| --- | --- |
| Objects and contexts | Their admitted formation, identity, scope and relevant relationships |
| Semantic and evidential roles | Which expressions concern which subjects, what is observed or inferred, and what each contribution licenses |
| Adopted and empirical commitments | Which requirements or decisions the definition constitutes and which factual claims still require grounds |
| Composite constitution | The generated constituents and their connecting laws |
| Interfaces | Access with the required binding, multiplicity, availability and qualifications |
| Transformations | Applicable inputs, meaningful effects, required preservation and supported outcomes |
| Recognition | Observations of the configurations and changes on which work depends |
| Result formation | Products that retain what their receiving uses require |
| Continuing operation | Dependencies, maintained meaning and handling of relevant change |
| External participation | The stated acquisition, contribution and return relationships |

A static export can be one part of this process. It does not establish the entire operative correspondence.

### 15.3 A simple formal coordinate for correspondence

Let \(D\) be a domain definition and \(E\) an established realizing environment. Write \(R_E(D)\) for the operative arrangement derived where \(D\) lies within the supported defining forms and the necessary realization premises hold.

Let \(s\) be a represented domain state and \(r\) an operative state. A relation \(\alpha(s,r)\) says that they correspond in the respects claimed. Starting from \(\alpha(s,r)\), if the operative arrangement performs a meaningful transition \(r\leadsto r'\), faithful behavior requires an appropriate domain transition or sequence \(s\leadsto_D s'\) with \(\alpha(s',r')\), allowing internal steps that do not change domain meaning.

This expresses one direction: operative behavior must respect the defined account. A claim that all admitted domain work can be realized also requires the other direction of capability: the required transitions or accomplishments must have supported operative means under the stated premises.

The notation identifies obligations, not an already established universal theorem. The relevant notions of state, correspondence, internal step, completion and external observation must be supplied by the particular realization account.

### 15.4 Locating what prevents operability

| Gap | What is missing | Appropriate response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incomplete domain definition | A subject, relationship, transformation or use needed by the intended work is not adequately constituted | Develop or repair the domain meaning |
| Missing realizing capacity | The meaning is sufficiently defined, but the environment cannot represent or exercise it faithfully | Develop the required general capacity or state the limitation |
| Faulty correspondence | The environment has relevant capacities but the passage loses or changes a required distinction | Repair the translation, binding or realizing behavior |
| Inadequate external grounds | The formal account is coherent but its empirical or other substantive premises are not established | Obtain or revise the appropriate grounds |
| Unavailable operating prerequisite | The realization is defined but lacks required resources, access, participation or observation | Supply the prerequisite or limit the operating claim explicitly |

These problems can coexist. Their separation matters because adding a database field cannot substitute for a missing domain law, and polishing a definition cannot make an absent operational mechanism exist.

Within an already supported realization environment, repeated bespoke reinterpretation of each new domain is not the intended finishing step. If substantial meaning has to be improvised there, the definition or the reusable realization contract is incomplete.

### 15.5 Direct derivation is not universal success by declaration

Directly deriving the apparatus does not make external facts true, guarantee that every proposed requirement is satisfiable, or eliminate resources and external dependencies.

The same coherent arrangement may correctly produce different outcomes as its population and circumstances change. It may maintain a family of candidates, form a supported negative result or identify a genuine unresolved question. The required outcome follows the responsibility, not a blanket expectation of a favorable verdict.

The strong possibility remains: when meaning, admitted forms and realizing powers are properly connected, definition can be an operative act rather than a handoff for another person to reconstruct the domain.

The defining effort can be substantial precisely because the resulting arrangement should not need that missing reconstruction. A domain's conceptual depth is not removed by direct derivation; it is constituted upstream and carried faithfully into its exercise.

## 16. Epistemic and technical capabilities developing together

### 16.1 Different frontiers can be reached

A domain can be well understood but poorly represented. It can be representable but lack a suitable operation. A technically powerful system can also be unable to act appropriately because the required meaning was never constituted.

The relevant development is therefore not one measure of “more formal” or “more automated.”

| Frontier | Typical unresolved question |
| --- | --- |
| Conceptual | Which distinction or subject does the intended work require? |
| Epistemic | What grounds, methods or knowledge would establish the relevant claim? |
| Formal | How can the relationship receive exact structure and laws? |
| Representational | Can the available medium retain those distinctions and connections? |
| Constructive | Are there means of producing the required result or transformation? |
| Operational | Can those means be maintained, coordinated and exercised under actual conditions? |

Progress can occur at different frontiers at different times. A problem may require coordinated advances across several of them.

### 16.2 Meaning directs development; realization supplies criticism

If a current representation loses a necessary joint relationship, the required meaning should not be silently reduced to what that representation can conveniently store.

However, not every failure of realization proves that the technology alone is at fault. The proposed meaning may be ambiguous, its laws inconsistent, its physical assumptions wrong or its desired capability unattainable. Operational difficulty can therefore return a question to the domain definition as well as to engineering.

The relationship is reciprocal without making success the definition of truth. A construction working once does not establish its full reach; a failed attempt does not by itself prove that no adequate construction is possible.

### 16.3 New epistemic instruments

Domain work may need to develop the instruments through which its own subjects become definable. A richer distinction of identity, a way of retaining context through translation, or an exact account of partial comparison can become necessary before a proposed activity is properly expressible.

Some instruments remain domain-specific. Others can generalize and support several domains. This is one route by which formal domain definition can contribute to wider conceptual and mathematical development.

The claimed novelty must remain proportionate to the investigation. Discovering that the current team lacks an instrument is not evidence that the instrument is unknown in mathematics or professional practice.

### 16.4 Present examples are not the future boundary

An early operational system may offer a finite repertoire of object forms, observations and transformations. Their combinations can already be rich, while still excluding consequential forms of context, interaction or judgment.

The general subject must not be reduced to that repertoire. A later domain may need richer identity, geometry, temporal organization, representations of uncertainty, qualitative structures or other instruments. Their necessity and adequacy must be established through the actual work, not listed as an inevitable architectural roadmap.

Nor should every domain be forced into numerical scoring merely because numbers are easy to process. Where plural judgments, partial comparisons or interpretive differences matter, the account must preserve what they mean and what use they support. The required formal and technical treatment may itself remain a substantive research question.

The direction is **co-development of what can be understood and defined with what can be faithfully realized**, without making either today's tools or imagined future tools the authority over the domain.

## 17. Adequacy, completeness and the grounds of a capability claim

### 17.1 Different claims require different evidence

| Claim | What must be established | What does not establish it alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intelligible definition | Consequential meanings can be understood without supplying missing commitments by guesswork | Terminology, length or a collection of diagrams |
| Formal formation | Expressions and constructions meet the rules of the selected formal system | Their intended external interpretation |
| Coherence of the defined account | Its relevant commitments have the consistency and compatibility properties claimed | Each component being well-formed separately |
| Domain adequacy | The account retains what the subject and declared work require | Elegance or correctness of the selected mathematics alone |
| Faithful realization | Operative representations and behavior preserve the claimed meaning | Producing files or accepting declarations |
| Successful execution | A stated activity was performed under the relevant conditions | Mere availability of the apparatus |
| Achievement of purpose | The result fulfills the intended responsibility at its scope | A technically successful execution with the wrong interpretation |
| General reach | Grounds support the claimed class of cases and compositions | A favorable example or a larger number of examples alone |

These claims can reinforce one another. They cannot be substituted for one another.

Formal verification proves conclusions under specified definitions and premises. It does not, without further grounds, certify the interpretation of an empirical domain. Empirical success can challenge or support an interpretation without proving an unrestricted mathematical claim.

### 17.2 Completeness relative to a responsibility

Completeness concerns the whole that an account undertakes to supply. An inherited-domain description, a cross-domain integration and an operative capability can owe different wholes.

A consequential gap exists when a claimed interpretation or activity requires a distinction, relationship, input, result or power that has not been adequately supplied. Separate completion of the contributing pieces does not close their undefined passage.

An adequate completion account should state:

- The subjects, purposes and variation it covers.
- The constitutions and connections supplied within that scope.
- The external contributions on which it relies.
- The preservation and change obligations required by continuing use.
- The grounds for the reach it claims.
- The unresolved matters and the claims they prevent or qualify.

This is not the demand to know everything about a contributing field. The coverage modes in §6.4 explain how boundedness and depth can coexist.

### 17.3 Completeness is not logical omniscience

Completeness of a domain-definition responsibility is not automatically completeness of a logical theory, decidability of every question, uniqueness of interpretation, or guaranteed termination of every process.

Those are distinct properties requiring their own definitions and arguments where claimed. One domain may legitimately retain open scientific questions while supplying an exact way to represent and investigate them.

Conversely, merely naming an unresolved matter does not complete a capability that depends on resolving it. A residual is informative when it identifies what is missing, why it matters, what can still be done and what cannot yet be claimed.

### 17.4 Revision without erasing prior meaning

A domain definition can be adequate at a stated scope and later require revision because the purpose changes, knowledge improves or an unanticipated dependency becomes consequential.

A revision should explain which commitments changed and which prior products or uses are affected. Earlier work may remain valid under its earlier premises without supporting every use under the successor account.

No universal procedure for proving adequacy across all domains is supplied here. This document defines the responsibilities such an assessment must distinguish; domain-specific methods and evidence remain necessary.

## 18. Why this work matters, and what its accessibility requires

Formal domain definition makes the organization through which knowledge becomes usable a subject of deliberate development. It can preserve connections that would otherwise be repeatedly reconstructed, expose disagreement at its actual premises, and make new combinations of work possible.

Its value is not confined to automation. An explicit constitution can improve explanation, teaching, criticism, collaboration and the ability to transfer results without silently changing their meaning.

Its stronger productive possibility is that the constitution can also determine an operative arrangement through suitable reusable means. Then developing a domain's explicit organization becomes part of developing its available capability.

For this work to become practicable across fields, people must be able to understand both the domain meaning and the role of the formal instruments used. Accessibility is not removal of necessary depth. It is the development of explanatory methods, representations and tools through which that depth can be inspected and used without demanding that every participant master every underlying specialization.

The present account defines one subject within a wider set of definitions. [The Formal Industrial Revolution](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/Episteme and Technebase/Proper Definitions of things/Episteme/Essays/01-THE-FORMAL-INDUSTRIAL-REVOLUTION-WORKING-DEFINITION.md>) addresses the broader proposed productive transformation. [Reclaiming the House of Philosophy](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/Episteme and Technebase/Proper Definitions of things/Episteme/Essays/02-RECLAIMING-THE-HOUSE-OF-PHILOSOPHY-WORKING-DEFINITION.md>) develops the constructive and critical responsibilities concerning concepts, knowledge and instruments of thought. The [Episteme Studio outline](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/Episteme and Technebase/Proper Definitions of things/Episteme/Outline/00-EPISTEME-STUDIO-WORKING-DEFINITION.md>) is a separate institutional account, not another stage of this explanation.

Those connections provide further context; they do not carry missing premises of this definition.

## 19. What this account establishes and what remains to be developed

The account now supplies more than an inventory of topics that formalization should address. It traces particular meanings into their use, exhibits competing interpretations that change the result, and develops structures whose laws determine what composition can preserve.

Its claims remain differentiated. A source shows how an author explains a concept or problem. Our reconstruction identifies the responsibilities of using it. A proposed constitution specifies a bounded arrangement. A mathematical argument establishes a result under its definitions. These are not interchangeable evidence levels.

| Subject | What has been developed here | What remains substantive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing semantic organization | Concepts have referential, contrastive, inferential, functional and other relationships; examples and practices make those usable without restating every prerequisite | The particular organization and degree of formalization of each further domain require investigation |
| Interpretive dependence | The enjoyment case traces the distinction between record, referent, theoretical use and further inquiry; the narrative case shows interpretation affecting which subjects remain visible | No complete theory of human cognition or universal substitute for expertise is supplied |
| Comparative diagnosis | Mathematics, psychological inquiry, instructional design, narrative and engineering exhibit different mixtures of exact structure and interpretive responsibility | The selected passages do not measure whole disciplines or establish one ranking of their maturity |
| Ontological constitution | Reports, interpretations, claims, episodes, roles and composite grounds are distinguished by consequences for use and revision | Actual domains must justify which distinctions they require and how their subjects are grounded |
| Representation adequacy | §8.5 proves when a question factors through a representation and exposes the impossibility of recovering a discarded distinction from that representation alone | Discovering the adequate case space and complete family of intended questions is not automatically solved |
| Constructive possibility | §14 defines sorted operations, composite laws, generated terms and interpretations; finite primitives generate arbitrarily deep finite constructions | Its pure, total, immutable scope does not cover every effect, partial operation, interaction or empirical contribution |
| Composition and correspondence | Common-subject formation, substitution and a structural preservation argument explain how justified primitive correspondence extends to generated composites | Correct primitive meaning, distinction preservation and grounds for external interpretation still have to be established |

Some open matters are deliberately outside this definition's responsibility: a complete psychology of motivation, an exhaustive theory of narrative or the implementation of a universal realizing environment. Their absence does not remove the explanatory achievements above.

Other residuals are conditional obligations. If a particular operative claim requires accurate motivation assessment, content interpretation or global compatibility, that work must be supplied before the stronger claim is made. Naming it as an interface or future task does not satisfy it.

The central conclusion is:

> Formal domain definition develops the organization by which a domain's subjects are meaningful, its relationships support justified use, and its possible constructions become explicitly available. Where adequate reusable means realize that constitution, defining the domain can determine its operative arrangement. Where they do not, the same account locates the semantic, epistemic, formal or technical work still required.


## 20. Source grounds and development record

### 20.1 How the sources are used

The sources support distinctions and investigations; they do not collectively supply a finished theory of formal domain definition. The mathematical illustrations are explained in the text, and extrapolations from particular practices are identified as such.

Psychology is examined both as a domain with meaningful constructs and as a source concerning learned capacities; these roles remain distinct. Instructional design and narrative supply contrasting semantic cases, not universal theories of their fields. Architecture supplies bounded integration tests, not technical design advice. Mathematical sources supply precise constructions and interpretation laws; our explanatory examples and elementary arguments are identified as such rather than attributed to the books.

The account does not establish how widespread any proposed deficiency is across all present fields. It distinguishes the kinds of investigation and development that the intended capability can require.
