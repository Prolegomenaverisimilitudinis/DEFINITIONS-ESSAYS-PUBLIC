# Authorial Development Methodology

## Purpose and relationship to the work

**Working methodology for developing Reclaiming the House of Philosophy — revised 12 October 2026.**

This methodology supports the collaborative creation of an original explanatory definition: its concepts, historical and causal interpretation, judgments, narrative, voice and implications. It is designed for the difficulty of this undertaking, in which conceptual creation, historical material and a forceful authorial perspective must become one intelligible account.

Its subject is **how to perform that authorial work**. The position we develop together in conversation determines what the account argues. The methodology helps us understand that position, discover what it requires, develop the necessary intellectual work, compose it adequately and carry revisions through the whole. Particular arguments, historical sequences and current priorities belong in the definition and its work context. They inform the method's requirements without becoming its prescribed conclusions.

**Our developing account determines what material it needs; the availability of material does not determine our account.** Sources and previous drafts bring explanatory substance to the structure being developed together: a mechanism that makes a connection intelligible, circumstances that establish its operation, a distinction that sharpens a judgment, or an example that gives a relationship its proper force. Their contribution must serve that same intellectual undertaking. Independent reasoning is essential to this development; a materially different undertaking is a proposal to discuss, not an unannounced replacement.

**The method governs development in conversation and development directly in the MD as parts of the same undertaking.** We may discover and refine an idea through discussion before integrating it, or develop it while writing cleanly into the document when that is the agreed task. In either mode, understanding intention, creating concepts, reasoning, source work, narrative judgment and authorial fidelity are active responsibilities now—not checks postponed until a final draft.

**The unit of development may be local; the unit of substantive integration is the entire current account.** Each new integration into the MD requires reconsidering that account from beginning to end in light of the best current understanding, then producing one coherent whole. The whole is the text we are presently developing, not everything that could be said about its subject. Whole-account judgment preserves adequate passages, carries new understanding through their relationships, and keeps genuinely undeveloped parts open. It does not require filling the eventual work's entire span during each update. The same principle applies when revising this methodology.

The definition is not yet the public essay. It needs sufficient depth for the author and collaborator to develop the subject properly, and sufficient narrative coherence for that depth to be understood. A later essay may select and compress this work. The present method should help produce the understanding from which such choices can responsibly be made. The new clean account will be developed in `FULL DEFINITIONS`; earlier definitions in `PREVIOUS DEFINITIONS PRE-AUTHORING` are available material, not its inherited draft or automatic scope. The working locations below record this separation.

**The governing purpose is to develop the author's intended meaning into its strongest adequately grounded and fully apprehensible expression, while contributing the discoveries needed to make that meaning more complete.** Fidelity and intellectual initiative cooperate: the collaborator understands what is being attempted, develops what is missing, and makes consequential proposed changes visible to the author.

## 1. First principles

### 1.1 Authoring creates understanding

The immediate material may be an intuition, a judgment, a remembered episode, an analogy, an objection or an unfinished connection. Development makes its relevant objects, relationships and consequences sufficiently definite to be examined and communicated. It may discover a concept the author could not yet name or reveal that an apparently simple assertion contains several claims.

Judge progress by what becomes understood or possible to understand. Expansion helps when it performs part of that development. Concision helps when it concentrates an intelligible relationship. Neither length nor brevity supplies the governing measure.

### 1.2 Meaning belongs to relationships, not merely to words

An author's point includes what it concerns, what relationship it proposes, why that matters, and what judgment or question it opens. Keeping the same vocabulary can conceal a changed point; changing the vocabulary can preserve and sharpen it.

Recover that relational meaning before revising. Preserve the originating perception as well as the proposition: the discrepancy noticed, the expectation challenged, the stakes perceived and the possibility opened. This serves a distinctive perspective rather than reducing it to a generally reasonable treatment of its topic.

### 1.3 Development, grounding and expression inform one another

Thinking does not always finish before writing begins. A sentence can expose uncertain agency; comparison can reveal a missing distinction; research can change the question; arrangement can disclose an unsupported transition. Use those discoveries to develop the work.

Conversation is itself a site of this intellectual creation. An answer can develop a candidate concept, expose a consequence, clarify the author's intention or open a better question. Writing into the MD can disclose further implications that return to discussion. These movements are reciprocal, not a one-way sequence in which chat supplies finished material and editing merely deposits it.

Distinguish what each achievement establishes. A compelling formulation makes something apprehensible; an inference establishes a consequence when its premises and reasoning warrant it; a source may support a historical claim; the author's approval settles authorial acceptance. These achievements cooperate without standing in for one another.

### 1.4 Understanding accumulates through an order

A reader encounters one passage after another. What a passage can accomplish depends on what has already been supplied. Definitions, reasons, examples and judgments therefore require relationships of preparation, development and consequence.

Discover the order from the work. Chronology, conceptual dependence, thematic development and rhetorical movement can cooperate, but no universal outline determines their arrangement.

### 1.5 Method answers to the work it enables

Use a procedure because it resolves a real difficulty. A clarification may need immediate writing; an original concept may need prolonged exploration; a disputed connection may need concentrated research. Depth is determined by the difficulty and its consequences.

The practices below follow from these principles. They are available intellectual operations, not a fixed production sequence. Move among discovery, discussion, research, composition and review as needed, and improve the method when it fails to serve the undertaking.

Full application means bringing the method's responsibilities to bear on the work at hand. A conversational clarification need not become a formal report, and every paragraph need not display every operation. At a substantive integration, however, the entire document is the field of reconsideration; the depth of investigation and extent of rewriting are selected through that whole reading.

## 2. Recover the authorial undertaking and its living state

Before a substantive intervention, recover what the author wants to develop, what has been accepted, what remains proposed, and what the current task authorizes. A previous draft is material to examine, not automatic evidence of agreement.

Keep the roles of working material distinguishable:

| Material | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Authorial direction and developments established together | The intended position, purpose, commitments and decisions to preserve or reconsider explicitly. Later clarifications govern the interpretation of earlier ones. |
| The current clean definition | The explanation actually admitted into the developing account. Its presence records the current text, not blanket authorial approval of every sentence. |
| Current work context | Questions, alternatives, proposals, source findings and decisions whose standing remains distinguishable. It supports work without becoming an instruction to include everything recorded. |
| Previous definitions and external sources | Material to consult for a particular explanatory or evidential need. Recover useful achievements, but assess their fit anew rather than carrying their outline, emphasis or conclusions into the clean account by default. |

The methodology guides work with these materials. It does not acquire authority to decide the subject by recording an earlier interpretation of it. Authorial authority and factual grounds are also distinct: a clearly intended proposition may still require investigation.

Understand the undertaking's intended reach, readers, stakes, genre and maturity. An explanatory definition, a public essay, a polemical intervention and a research note can share material while requiring different treatments. Establish what this document must accomplish before deciding how much explanation, citation, narrative preparation or compression it needs.

Recover intended functions at the appropriate scale. A passage may affirm, distinguish, indict, explain, expose, discover or propose. These functions can cooperate without every passage performing all of them. Preserve each contribution while examining its place in the whole.

When the author corrects the work, identify the correction's scope. It may repair a word, revise a concept, change a relationship or redirect the undertaking. Carry it as far as its consequences require while preserving unaffected work.

### 2.1 Keep conversation and document in one developing context

At each meaningful return, recover both what the MD currently says and what our subsequent discussion has changed, clarified or left open. The document may temporarily lag behind the developing understanding; conversation may explore possibilities that should not yet enter it. Keep that difference intelligible so neither becomes an accidental substitute for the other.

Distinguish an exploratory suggestion, an understood intention, an agreed definition, a proposed formulation and an authorized writing task. Agreement about an idea need not authorize a file edit during a discussion-only exchange. Conversely, an instruction to integrate or write directly authorizes the relevant editorial work without requiring repeated permission for each consequential adjustment within its scope.

Prepare to integrate when the development's meaning, role, grounds, unresolved choices and implications are sufficiently recoverable to compose responsibly. It need not be the last word on the subject. An explicitly provisional development can belong in the best current account if the author intends its inclusion and its standing is clear.

### 2.2 Grow the clean account from established understanding

A clean start preserves the authorial achievements of our discussion while releasing the new text from an earlier draft's organization. Recover the intended meanings, connections, examples and emphases; then compose what is presently ready in the order it needs. An older paragraph may prove useful, but its finished appearance does not establish that its argument belongs here.

For each meaningful addition, establish three things: **what we are trying to establish; what a reader must understand for that act to succeed; and what development supplies precisely that understanding.** These questions govern selection without prescribing a fixed outline. They allow extensive explanation where a relationship needs it and a short passage where the necessary work is already done.

Controlled growth concerns the admission of substance, not the number of words or the size of an editing pass. A single accepted clarification may require substantial rewriting throughout the current MD. A long source investigation may justify one sentence, a work note, or a proposal that does not yet enter the definition. The measure is what the developing account requires.

Keep a distinction between the intended eventual reach and the material currently developed enough to compose. A coherent beginning can be complete as the present integration while the larger definition remains unfinished. Use work context to retain future directions and unresolved choices, so the clean text can explain what we have actually developed without pretending to have reached its eventual destination.

## 3. Understand the contribution before deciding its expression

### 3.1 Recover the intellectual act

What is being noticed, connected, judged or brought into question? Why was this example chosen? What would disappear if the point became a more conventional claim?

The author's words may be compressed, colloquial, provocative or exploratory. Their register can reveal emphasis without determining the wording of the finished explanation. Develop the thought at the precision and force the document requires.

Preserve the original wording or its exact location for consequential contributions in work context. Later interpretations remain answerable to it; repeated paraphrase must not quietly become a substitute source of intention.

An analogy may identify a relationship rather than request a scene. A joke may concentrate a contrast rather than request a joke in the text. An emphatic judgment may state a thesis whose argument remains to be built. Determine and develop its function.

### 3.2 Preserve logical force

Track the formulation's commitments: subject, scope, time, agency, modality, comparison and consequence. Possibility, occurrence, tendency, necessity and obligation differ. Changes among them are changes of thought even when the wording looks modest.

Compare what the author intended the passage to establish with what the revision establishes. Examine relationships and conclusions, not just vocabulary.

Where an intended claim exceeds its present grounds, keep both visible in work context. Develop the missing grounds or propose a consequential revision explicitly. This preserves ambition without presenting unfinished work as an established result.

### 3.3 Contribute with intelligible authorship

Exercise substantial judgment within the understood undertaking: develop missing reasoning, discover a useful distinction, recover a causal connection, make an example precise, supply the preparation a reader needs and find a stronger expression. This is authorial development, not transcription. It does not require the author to prescribe each inference or approve each sentence before it can be written.

Judge the scope of an addition by its intellectual effect. Explaining the mechanism of an agreed connection can deepen the same argument. Adding another historical branch, assigning a new cause, changing the standard of judgment or promoting a supporting symptom into the central thesis can alter the argument's commitments. Where such a development is consequential and not already within the agreed task, present it in conversation with its reason and implications before incorporating it as our position. Broad permission to improve the writing calls for initiative within the undertaking, not silent enlargement of it.

Where an ambiguity leads to materially different definitions or conclusions, explain the alternatives and their consequences. Develop a proposal sufficiently for the author to assess it, rather than transferring all the thinking back through an abstract question. Seek a decision at the consequential fork while continuing useful work whose direction is clear.

The collaborator should be able to explain both what it understood and what it added. Source evidence may expose an error even in a clearly intended claim. Repair a straightforward factual detail narrowly; where the finding changes the interpretation itself, bring the exact difficulty and viable responses to the author. Preserve the difference between faithfulness to an intended argument and the grounds required to assert it.

### 3.4 Develop the thought in conversation rather than merely echoing it

Answer the intellectual question being asked. Show the understanding reached, then perform the next useful act: sharpen a distinction, develop a consequence, identify an unresolved relation, compare plausible meanings or propose a formulation. Give enough connected explanation for the author to assess the thought, without converting every exchange into a finished essay or a report on the method.

Preserve the accumulated discussion while attending to the newest contribution. A new remark may extend, qualify or replace an earlier one; determine which, and carry its implications into the working understanding. Reconsider an apparent agreement if a later clarification changes what it meant. Where a consequential ambiguity remains, make it available for joint resolution rather than letting fluent agreement conceal it.

An exploration can be valuable before it yields a stable definition. Keep the question alive at its actual level of development, explain what has become clearer and identify what would advance it. This makes conversation productive without forcing premature closure.

## 4. Generate concepts from explanatory need

### 4.1 Discover what the account cannot yet express

Start from the unresolved subject. What remains conflated? Which relationship is felt but not articulated? What cannot yet be explained with the available terms? Which apparently similar cases differ in a consequential respect?

Explore candidate distinctions through their objects, relations, conditions and consequences. A tentative name can assist thought, but its usefulness depends on what it enables us to understand. Develop enough substance to discover whether the concept is needed and what it should mean.

Conceptual creation can disclose a better question or a consequential alternative. Develop it enough to understand what it changes, and distinguish that proposal from an elaboration of the question we have already undertaken. Elegance and familiarity can help expression; they do not settle which conception best serves the inquiry or authorize its inclusion.

Where useful, vary a case or relationship in thought: change a condition, remove an assumed feature, alter the scale or follow a consequence further. Examine what changes and what persists. Compare candidate formulations by the differences they reveal, then investigate a newly exposed dependency, contradiction or unexplained case. These explorations can help an initially felt distinction acquire definite content. Select them according to the problem rather than perform a standard battery of exercises.

### 4.2 Determine the kind of distinction

When the author distinguishes two things, determine whether the difference concerns degree, kind, function, identity, relation, scope, means or result—or a combination requiring further articulation. The method supplies this inquiry rather than deciding its answer.

Improving a performance, changing what is performed and changing the standard by which it is judged are different developments. Identify the relevant distinction, explain its grounds and follow what it changes in subsequent judgments.

Existing classifications can themselves require revision. A new distinction may reorganize how familiar material is understood rather than distribute it among established categories. Make that contribution sufficiently explicit to examine.

### 4.3 Develop definitions through consequences and cases

A definition should explain the particular nature or relationship the account needs. Develop what belongs together, what can vary, what makes an instance count, and what follows from using the concept. Select the dimensions that matter; these are aids to discovery, not fields every definition must fill.

Use cases to expose implications. Straightforward cases, consequential contrasts and difficult boundaries can each reveal something different. Articulate the relationship that makes the resulting judgments coherent, so the definition extends beyond preferred examples.

Distinguish proposing a meaning, showing why it illuminates its subject, and establishing that a particular subject fits it. A consequential term can require all three.

Introduce concepts through the difficulty they resolve. Familiar vocabulary may need little preparation; an original use may need sustained development. Supply the explanation necessary for the actual use rather than a general lesson about the discipline. A concept can gain depth as the narrative proceeds, provided earlier uses have the meaning they require.

### 4.4 Develop analogy as discovery and explanation

Recover the relation an analogy makes available. Ask what it illuminates, which questions it suggests and how those questions can be investigated in the actual subject. Develop useful correspondences and let the subject determine their extent.

An organizing analogy can remain central throughout a work. Its returns should deepen recognition through new material. Preserve the relationship giving it identity while distinguishing its imaginative reach from the grounds of conclusions made through it.

Suggestiveness can initiate inquiry; the resulting explanation must establish what its conclusions depend on. An image earns its place through thought, not through elaborating every incidental feature of what it evokes.

### 4.5 Develop concepts in their necessary relationships

Some concepts become intelligible only through their relations to others. Determine whether an apparent definitional difficulty lies in the concept itself or in an undeveloped relationship. Develop enough of the connected structure to make each concept's role clear without attempting to explain the entire subject at once.

A provisional formulation can support further discovery when its provisional standing is clear. Return to it as the connected account matures. Distinguish mutual illumination from circular support: two concepts can explain one another's roles, but repeating each as the sole justification for the other leaves the basis of the account undeveloped.

Terminological consistency follows conceptual development. Preserve a name when its meaning remains stable, mark a consequential new use, and revise dependent passages when the concept changes. A consistent vocabulary is valuable because it helps the reader follow a coherent understanding, not because wording should remain fixed through discovery.

## 5. Develop the connection the argument requires

### 5.1 Locate the missing intellectual work

A topic identifies material. A developed question identifies what must be understood about it. Translate a consequential assertion into the question it answers and determine what would make that answer adequate.

At a difficult transition, ask: **what could an attentive reader accept in the preceding explanation while still not accepting the next conclusion?** The gap may concern a definition, factual premise, causal mechanism, standard, inference or significance. Develop that connection rather than collect more generally relevant material.

One sentence can compress several dependent claims. Identify which carries the unresolved burden. More support for an established premise does not repair an unestablished passage from it to another claim.

Distinguish incomplete thought from inadequate expression. The former may require conceptual development or research; the latter may require different ordering, a particular example or a more exact sentence. Composition can reveal which problem is present.

### 5.2 Match development to the relationship

An account may define, describe, explain, compare, evaluate and propose within a single movement. Determine what each accomplishes and how it supports the next. These functions need not become separate sections or follow a fixed order.

For causation, develop how the alleged cause could operate and what establishes its operation. For evaluation, develop the standard and its application. For a conceptual relation, establish the distinction and consequences. For a proposal, develop its means, dependencies and relation to the intended result.

A question can be raised before being answered. Make its role intelligible and return to it when the needed development becomes available. A purposeful open question differs from an unnoticed missing premise.

### 5.3 Give judgments their appropriate standards

Make clear what is assessed, in which respect and on what grounds. Develop why the standards govern this judgment and how they relate when they point in different directions.

Some criteria permit trade-offs; others may be necessary to the identity or purpose being examined. Establish the appropriate relationship rather than assume every evaluation is an aggregate score.

A position can be examined through its own commitments or through independently defended standards. Identify which basis carries the judgment. Inconsistency, inadequacy, falsehood, harmful consequence and culpability are different conclusions; develop the passage between them wherever it is claimed.

Strong evaluative language should acquire a definite object and basis. Severity can then concentrate a judgment instead of supplying missing reasoning by intensity.

### 5.4 Develop constructive conclusions on their own grounds

Identifying a problem can establish a requirement without identifying its unique solution. When the account proposes a response, distinguish the requirement from the means.

Explain why those means answer it, what they depend on, what consequences they introduce and how their adequacy can be assessed. Preserve genuine choices where the author's position remains open. A transition from diagnosis to construction requires intellectual work when the undertaking calls for it; the method does not make it every argument's obligatory destination.

## 6. Build historical-causal interpretation at its claimed scale

Historical and causal explanation are central needs of this project. The method should enable a coherent interpretation while letting the actual relationships determine its particulars.

Distinguish succession, influence, enabling conditions, reinforcement and direct causation. Establish the endpoints of a proposed connection and investigate what joins them. A plausible mechanism identifies something to examine; an account of its historical operation needs corresponding grounds.

### 6.1 Explain processes and their connections

Origin, persistence, reproduction and consequences can involve different processes. Discover which the argument needs. Identify the agents, commitments, practices, institutions, constraints or opportunities through which the proposed relation operates.

A position stated in a text, its circulation, its adoption and its effects are distinct matters. Join them where the argument depends on all of them. A quotation can establish a stated position without establishing its prevalence or consequences.

Investigate feedback, delayed effects and uneven development when relevant. Consequences can alter their own originating circumstances. Different capacities can follow different trajectories. Coherence comes from explaining these relations, not requiring every subject to display the same pattern.

### 6.2 Keep scale and comparison intelligible

Individual capacity, institutional practice, a field's output and a wider culture are related levels. Explain transitions between them when generalizing. A striking instance can reveal a broader question; its role in establishing an answer requires development.

For comparison, identify its dimensions and why they matter. Distinguish observed differences, their proposed explanations and counterfactual expectations about what could or should have happened. Their cooperation strengthens an argument when each performs its own work.

Where a charge depends on intention, omission or responsibility, investigate the corresponding standing, capacities and circumstances. Where it concerns observable consequences rather than private motives, develop those consequences. Preserve the intended judgment's precise reach.

### 6.3 Develop an owned interpretation

A definite historical perspective can have selective emphasis and a severe verdict. Its adequacy depends on developed relationships, not on resembling a balanced survey. Select material by the case being made and the burdens it actually carries.

Examine consequential contrary material where it affects a premise, mechanism, comparison or conclusion. A useful distinction may strengthen the case; a factual conflict may require repair; a major finding may warrant a substantive proposal to the author.

Present an interpretation with its actual standing. Perspective gives the undertaking its identity; inquiry develops and examines what it can responsibly claim.

## 7. Make source work answer the developing account

### 7.1 Read through consequential questions

Begin from a need in the work: a relationship we cannot yet explain, a historical premise requiring grounds, a chosen example needing development, or a compositional difficulty. State the question precisely enough to recognize what a useful finding would change. Select sources accordingly, then read enough surrounding material to understand their actual arguments. This permits deep reading without treating the whole source as a programme for our document.

Remain receptive to discoveries, including contrary findings. Distinguish an answer to the present question, a difficulty it exposes, and a new direction it suggests. Develop the first; investigate and report the second at its true significance; retain the third as a proposal when it would change our undertaking. Purposeful reading is open to correction and discovery while keeping the decision about the account's direction visible.

Alternate reading and development. Formulate the precise explanatory or evidential gain, assess whether it belongs in the current account, and compose the contribution in our own connected explanation when it does. Return to sources when this work reveals another consequential question. Pause the investigation when the current need has been adequately served; further interesting material can remain available without becoming another section to write.

### 7.2 Distinguish the source from our contribution

Record exact locations for relied-upon material. Distinguish what the source reports or argues, its grounds, what this account draws from it, and what remains a further inference. The source's authority concerns the support it can actually provide; its prominence, eloquence or affinity with our outlook does not give it authority over our explanatory sequence.

Read polemical sources for both insight and the standing of their claims. Read rhetorical sources for the intellectual or compositional practices they illuminate. Transfer useful practices after examining their assumptions; doing so need not import their philosophical positions.

Write the custom explanation the document requires. Giving the structural bones explanatory flesh means showing how and why their relationships hold, under what relevant circumstances, with what consequences and through which particulars. It can require considerable original reasoning. It means neither decorating an assertion with learned references nor developing the neighbouring subjects that happen to interest the source.

Choose attribution according to its function. Name or quote a thinker in the exposition when their identity, exact formulation or historical action is part of what the reader needs to understand. When the source supplies grounds for our explanation, a precise reference or note can preserve attribution without making the source's author a new protagonist. A quotation should accomplish something the passage actually needs—such as establishing what was said or exposing a decisive commitment. Our own inference remains ours to explain and justify; attribution must not make it appear to be the source's finding.

### 7.3 Select particulars by function

| Use of a particular | Development required |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | Its relevance and the inference it supports. |
| Example bearing on a broader claim | Why the instance bears on that claim and how far it supports generalization. |
| Illustration | Concrete features that make an already developed relationship more apprehensible. |
| Model | What can be learned or continued, and under which relevant conditions. |
| Distinctive episode | Its particular role, whether or not it is representative. |
| Analogy | The revealing relationship and grounds for applying it here. |

A particular can perform several functions, provided the passage does the work each requires. A memorable image can concentrate an argument without supplying all its grounds.

Develop the author's chosen example for its intended contribution. If a replacement seems necessary, explain the reason and what it changes rather than silently substituting a different point.

An example's role governs its development. If it illustrates a symptom, develop what makes that symptom revealing; if it carries evidence for a causal claim, establish that connection. The vividness or abundance of available material does not promote a supporting example into a different thesis. Its treatment can become deeper while its place in the argument remains proportionate.

### 7.4 Use challenges to strengthen positive development

Investigate the strongest relevant difficulty at a consequential point: another explanation, contrary evidence, disputed characterization or missing connection. Determine what answering it establishes.

Investigation and inclusion are different decisions. The final text needs what makes its case intelligible, not a transcript of every objection considered. Place qualifications where they convey a real change of meaning, grounds or scope; formulate them so the intended claim remains exact.

Showing that one explanation fails does not establish another. Develop the positive account's own grounds. Preserve unresolved difficulties with their actual consequences in work context.

### 7.5 Admit material by its contribution to the whole

Before bringing a substantial source-derived development or earlier draft passage into the clean text, judge its fit in several related respects:

| Question | What an adequate answer establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Which intended point does this serve? | The addition belongs to the account we are developing, rather than merely to its broad topic. |
| What becomes understandable that was missing? | Its intellectual contribution: grounds, mechanism, distinction, circumstances, consequence or a necessary particular. |
| What commitments does it add or change? | Whether it develops the agreed direction or introduces a consequential proposal that should first return to discussion. |
| Where and in what form does it belong? | The preparation, placement and expression through which it contributes to the reader's understanding. |
| How much attention does its role warrant? | Depth and prominence proportionate to its contribution, including the option of a source note rather than a new exposition. |

Apply these as authorial judgments, not as a form to complete for every sentence. General relevance answers only whether material is worth considering. Inclusion requires a particular contribution. Material can be excellent and remain in work context because its proper question has not yet arisen.

Retain useful findings in a recoverable form: the question investigated, the passage or evidence found, the exact gain it offers, its limits and its present standing. This preserves the value of exploration without making research accumulation dictate the text. The same discipline applies to material generated by the assistant or reviewers: its origin confers no automatic right of entry.

## 8. Discover the explanatory and narrative order

### 8.1 Coordinate different orders

The order of discovery, conceptual or argumentative dependence, the subject's temporal order and the order of presentation can differ. Coordinate them through the understanding the reader needs.

An essential distinction may emerge while writing an ending, requiring earlier preparation. Chronology may need a conceptual passage before an event becomes significant. A thematic return can connect distant material while preserving its historical relationships.

Understand why the author's intended sequence matters. Develop its connections, and propose substantial rearrangements by explaining what becomes more intelligible and which intended relationships remain preserved.

The order of our discussion records how understanding was reached; it need not be the order through which the document should explain it. At integration, place each developed idea where it prepares, establishes or deepens the account. A late discovery may belong near the beginning; several exchanges may become one explanation. Preserve the intellectual achievements while composing their proper sequence anew.

### 8.2 Develop the reader's changing understanding

For a substantial passage, consider what a reader can understand on entering it, what the passage enables, and why that changes the significance of what follows. These questions guide composition without imposing a repeated form.

Understanding a proposition, recognizing its significance, explaining its cause and judging its consequences are different advances. Discover which the passage needs to accomplish.

Transitions should carry those relationships. Announcing a new topic is insufficient when the connection remains unexplained. Arrangement can sometimes make the relation clear; state it when otherwise the reader would need to invent it.

### 8.3 Prepare, reveal and return

Supply essential orientation before relying on it. Then use anticipation, recognition before naming, delayed judgment and meaningful return where they deepen participation.

Productive inference uses relationships the account has supplied. Repairing an omission requires the reader to do intellectual work that still belongs to the author. Concentrate explicitness at original distinctions, disputed premises, consequential transitions and changes of scope.

Recurring concepts and images should retain identity while gaining determination. Each substantial return can add a consequence, perspective or newly intelligible relationship. Explain changes of scale or use where they might otherwise become silent changes of meaning.

The introduction can mature after the body reveals what it must prepare. The ending should perform the completion this undertaking requires, whether a definition, judgment, newly formulated problem or constructive direction. During controlled growth, let a developed part reach its appropriate stopping point; reserve the eventual introduction or conclusion where its content still depends on work we have not done.

### 8.4 Allocate depth and emphasis by consequence

Expand where a relationship remains undeveloped. Compress where its substance can be concentrated. Repetition may renew attention or accumulate force; distinguish that role from new support.

Review the whole's proportions. Easily available material should not consume the space required for harder and more consequential development. A promised destination needs enough treatment to be reached intellectually, not merely named at the end.

Historical narrative, conceptual explanation and polemical force can cooperate. Their arrangement should grow from the authorial purpose and actual dependencies rather than a standard dramatic pattern.

### 8.5 Preserve a governing line through substantial depth

Identify the main inquiry or explanatory development that makes the parts one work. A supporting exploration earns its place by what it enables that development to accomplish. Introduce its relevance, give it the depth its contribution requires, and return with an identifiable gain. This permits serious complexity without asking the reader to assemble an unconnected collection of essays.

Several lines can develop together, separate temporarily and meet again. Make the relationships that justify those movements intelligible. Where sequence suggests causation, hierarchy or inevitability, check that the account establishes the suggested relationship. A satisfying order can reveal reasoning; its fluency should remain faithful to what has actually been developed.

## 9. Compose so that the thought acquires its proper force

### 9.1 Treat expression as intellectual work

Composition can expose an unidentified agent, an uncertain relation, an equivocal comparison or an unintended conclusion. Use what the sentence reveals. When alternative formulations imply different responsibilities, scopes or outcomes, compare their meanings rather than choosing by elegance alone.

Attend to subjects, verbs, modality, grouping and endings. A list can imply equivalence among different roles. A passive construction can conceal agency that matters. A final qualification can become the apparent verdict of an otherwise affirmative passage. Arrange the actual relationships so the reader encounters them in their intended proportions.

Read both a precise paraphrase and the prose itself. Paraphrase tests the assertion; the prose carries emphasis, tempo, recognition and irony that paraphrase may lose. Adequate writing needs both intellectual identity and effective realization.

### 9.2 Make rhetorical construction serve a discernible relationship

Begin with what needs to be made apprehensible: parallelism, divergence, dependence, accumulation, reversal, continuity through change, or another relation discovered in the work. Choose a construction that lets the reader perceive it.

Parallel syntax can clarify genuinely parallel roles. A break in a pattern can mark a consequential difference. Expansion can unfold a complex dependence; contraction can concentrate its result. Delaying completion can give intervening material a common destination; direct statement can provide needed orientation. These are possibilities whose usefulness depends on occasion, not devices to distribute through the text.

Study substantial examples to learn that judgment. Ask what preceding material prepares a figure, what it does at its appearance, and what follows from it. A striking sentence detached from those relationships teaches less about how to use its construction well.

Give attention to sound and pace. Read substantial passages with an ear for stress, pause, syntactic completion, repetition and changes of tempo. Where the rhythm directs attention differently from the intended thought, revise their relationship. Musicality is useful here because prose unfolds in time and can clarify or obscure how ideas belong together.

### 9.3 Develop force through recognition

A powerful passage can arrange for the reader to recognize a discrepancy before the author names it. It can let a position's stated commitment meet its consequence, bring an initially remote implication close, or return to a familiar image with newly developed significance.

Identify the grounds that make the recognition possible. Allow the reader to draw a prepared consequence where that is more forceful than announcing it repeatedly. State the judgment directly where explicitness is the stronger act.

Nietzsche is useful to this project as a source of such movements of thought: probing a self-description, exposing its commitments, shifting perspective, varying tempo and concentrating a judgment. Learn the operation that gives a passage its force, then determine whether our material warrants it. Its adoption need not carry his doctrine, conclusions or mannerisms.

The author's other stylistic directions likewise identify qualities to understand and develop. Incisiveness, elegance, irony, severity and a public summons can have different realizations. Develop an original voice through this account's intellectual conduct rather than assemble recognisable imitations.

### 9.4 Sustain a range of appropriate tones

Affirmation, patient explanation, admiration, dry irony, grief, urgency and indictment perform different work. Let the tone express what is being understood or judged. A sustained maximum of intensity can erase differences that the argument needs the reader to feel.

Develop significance so feeling has an intelligible object. The worth of something can make its loss matter; an exposed discrepancy can make irony apt; a developed consequence can make urgency reasonable. Emotion and grounds cooperate through understanding rather than one replacing the other.

Preserve the local function of a passage. If it affirms an achievement, its composition should accomplish that affirmation. A necessary limitation should have the place and proportion required for accuracy and the larger argument. Placement is substantive because the last or most prominent thought may determine what the reader takes the passage to have said.

Actual reader response remains contingent. Assess whether the prose supplies the means of recognition and appropriate force, not whether a rhetorical device guarantees persuasion.

## 10. Develop the relationship between author and reader

An account addresses someone from a position. Determine the knowledge, questions and expectations its intended readers can reasonably bring, and what the text must develop for them. Treat those assumptions as revisable, particularly where the work introduces original concepts.

During our conversation, the author is the collaborator with whom the position is being developed, not the eventual public audience to be persuaded. Use the shared context to think together at the required depth. During integration, supply the preparation an attentive reader of the MD needs without relying on access to our exchanges. The same intellectual standards apply in both settings; their explanatory needs and forms of address differ.

Consider both understanding and significance. A reader may follow a definition yet not see why it matters; accept a criticism yet not understand its reach; recognise a connection yet not know what it changes. Supply the relevant advance rather than repeating the original assertion more emphatically.

Presence is selective attention made concrete. Naming, examples, detail, recurrence and placement can bring an aspect of the subject into view. Choose what deserves that attention by its role in the account. Examine whether vivid but secondary material has become more prominent than the relationship it was intended to serve.

Authorial authority is exercised through intellectual conduct: making distinctions, providing grounds, owning judgments and developing consequences. A commanding register may express that authority; the underlying conduct makes it intelligible.

Give collective terms and forms of address a determinate role. A shared experience, shared responsibility, proposed alliance and accusation are different relationships. Develop whichever the passage invokes. The reader may be invited to examine, judge, recognise, reconsider or participate; that invitation should arise from the account rather than from an assumed response.

A forceful perspective does not require an encyclopedic distribution of praise and blame. It requires selection adequate to its purpose, honest representation of relevant material and sufficient development of the claims actually made. This allows the work to speak decisively in its own voice while remaining answerable to its grounds.

## 11. Keep intention, development and evidence distinguishable

Two questions must remain independently answerable:

**Is this what the author intends to develop?**

**What currently supports, explains or challenges it?**

A claim can be clearly intended and still require investigation. A source-supported finding can be irrelevant to the author's question. A fruitful new interpretation can remain a proposal. A polished draft can remain unaccepted.

For a consequential open matter, keep enough work context to recover the intended proposition, what has been developed, the exact unresolved question, what finding would materially change it and the next useful investigation.

Use that distinction constructively. If the intellectual gap is a missing concept, develop it. If the gap concerns a source, investigate it. If it concerns an authorial decision, prepare a precise proposal. The status record should point to work, not become an additional ceremony surrounding it.

The main text should convey its actual standing in readable prose. Attribution, conditional formulation or an explicit statement of an interpretation's status may be appropriate. Choose the form that preserves both exactness and the passage's intended work.

When grounds require a substantive change, explain what it corrects, what it preserves and what else would change. Ordinary factual repairs can be recorded briefly. Replacing the governing interpretation, narrowing its ambition or deciding an open definition requires an intelligible authorial decision.

## 12. Reconstitute one coherent account at every substantive integration

### 12.1 Reconsider the whole from the present understanding

Before integrating a new development, personally read the entire current MD and recover the relevant discussion since its last integration. Establish what is now understood, which earlier formulations have been refined or superseded, what remains open, and what the account must presently achieve.

For the first entry into a clean file, begin with the relevant authorial discussion and the agreed scope of that entry; there is no existing clean text to inherit. Consult previous definitions for particular needs, with the same judgment of fit applied to any source. Once the clean MD exists, it is the current account to read in full. Earlier drafts remain references, not additional documents whose content must all be reconciled into it.

Examine the whole anew: its conceptual foundations, claims, dependencies, examples, sequence, emphasis, voice and destination. A local addition can change how an otherwise unchanged passage is understood. Whole reconsideration therefore goes beyond repairing references in the new material's immediate neighbourhood.

Existing prose has value as developed work, not immunity from reassessment. Preserve it where it still performs its role. Rebuild, relocate, combine, deepen or remove material where the new understanding requires it. The scope of consideration is always the whole; the extent of rewriting follows what that consideration reveals.

### 12.2 Compose the development into its proper relationships

Write the best current explanation of the material ready and authorized for this integration, whether it arose in conversation or through direct composition. Introduce ideas where they belong in the conceptual and narrative sequence. Explain their relationships and consequences in the body, rather than relying on a late addition to correct what earlier passages still imply.

Carry the development into earlier preparation, later inferences, examples, terminology, transitions, emphasis and conclusions. If a concept changes, revise the uses that depend on its former meaning. If it gains a new aspect, make the relationship to its earlier presentation intelligible. If an example changes role, reconsider the argument and expectations it now supports.

Translate conversational achievements into self-contained prose. Preserve their meaning, force and necessary explanation while giving them the form the document needs. The final MD should explain the subject, not require the reader to reconstruct our sequence of corrections or consult chat to understand a new paragraph. Discussion history and reasons for revisions remain available in work context.

### 12.3 Examine cooperation, not just consistency

Two accurate passages can compete for attention, redirect the apparent judgment or repeat the same contribution. Examine what they do together. A local climax can displace the document's destination; a qualification can become its apparent thesis; a source passage can redirect the account toward the source's interests.

Determine what the accumulation supplies. Some passages add independent grounds; others explain mechanisms, extend consequences or give presence to established relations. Repeated formulations from one source or assumption are not independent corroboration. Differences between materials may reveal changes of scale, conditions or meaning that require explanation.

Review proportions and tone across the account. A new explanatory demand may require preparation elsewhere or change what deserves emphasis. Consolidate redundant explanations while preserving distinct contributions. An unchanged passage can remain the right choice after this reassessment.

### 12.4 Complete the integration as a whole reading

After composing and revising, personally reread the entire resulting MD from beginning to end. Apply the intellectual, narrative and compositional attention in §14 to what it now says. Resolve stale assumptions, unexplained transitions, duplicate developments and incompatible formulations before presenting the integration as complete.

The integration is the completed editorial undertaking, which may involve several writing passes—not each keystroke or intermediate save. It is complete when the whole account embodies the development coherently at its current maturity. Local checks support that work but do not replace the concluding whole reading.

Coherence does not require pretending the subject is finished. Place genuinely intended open questions in the account where their role is intelligible; keep unadopted alternatives and future branches in work context or conversation. If a newly exposed choice requires the author's decision, develop the choice and report the remaining work. A completed integration may therefore leave the larger subject unfinished while faithfully embodying everything presently admitted into the account.

## 13. Work recursively at the level of the actual difficulty

### 13.1 Choose the next meaningful advance

Recover the live task and identify the most consequential unresolved relationship. Consider both its importance and what work can presently advance it. A central ambiguity may deserve discussion before extensive prose; a settled idea may benefit most from sustained composition.

Choose a manageable intellectual unit: enough material to complete a meaningful development and assess its consequences. This might be a distinction, a causal connection, a section or a whole-document reconstruction.

For a material decision, briefly establish what advance is sought and how its adequacy could be recognized. This makes the work purposeful without requiring a full plan for discoveries not yet made.

Determine whether the present task is exploration in conversation, clean writing directly in the MD, or integration of accumulated development. These are cooperating modes, not compulsory stages. In conversation, pursue the relevant insight and its implications. In an authorized MD update, perform the whole-account integration in §12 even when the initiating insight is small.

### 13.2 Alternate operations according to what emerges

A productive cycle can move through understanding, exploration, reading, formulation, composition, integration and rereading. Their order is responsive. Writing may expose the question that determines the next source reading; reading may make an existing outline inadequate; discussing an analogy may reveal the concept needed to proceed.

Develop alternatives when they expose consequential differences. At a hard conceptual or compositional choice, a small set of genuinely distinct candidates is more useful than many cosmetic variants. Compare what each enables, commits the account to and changes downstream.

Record consequential discoveries and distinguish what is ready to integrate from what needs further development. When writing is authorized, integrate them through §12 rather than transferring chat passages mechanically. When the task remains conversational, continue the intellectual work without treating every provisional result as a document change. Useful prose may be retained verbatim when it genuinely fits; placement and adequacy are judged afresh.

During direct MD development, writing itself may reveal a new concept or dependency. First determine whether it supplies needed explanation within the established undertaking or changes its commitments. Carry the former through the same integration; develop the latter as a proposal for discussion while continuing compatible work. Record honestly if the integration depends on a choice still unresolved. The distinction concerns the discovery's intellectual effect, not whether it was found by reading, conversation or writing.

### 13.3 Diagnose the failure at its responsible level

When a passage disappoints, locate the cause. Is the intended point misunderstood, the thought underdeveloped, the evidence insufficient, the order wrong, the expression imprecise or the tone misaligned?

Repair the level that carries the problem. A failed definition may require conceptual work rather than another synonym. A weak transition may require a premise rather than a linking phrase. A flattened passage may require restoration of its intended judgment rather than louder language.

Sometimes the responsible problem is earlier than the visible defect. Follow that dependency and revise far enough to resolve it. In conversation, examine how the repair changes the developing understanding. In an MD integration, reconsider and reread the entire account under §12 to see what it actually accomplished.

### 13.4 Make recurrence productive

Return to the work with a changed question or a distinct mode of attention. One pass can examine meaning; another how the reader experiences it; another the sources supporting a consequential relationship. Each return should have something to discover or improve.

Continue where further work can complete a relationship, resolve uncertainty or materially improve expression. Move elsewhere when local refinement contributes less than addressing a consequential gap. A time allocation supports sustained work; it does not require adding text merely to show activity.

An actual improvement may expand, compress, rearrange, distinguish or preserve. Recognizing that a passage already performs its purpose can be a useful outcome of review.

## 14. Review through complementary readings

### 14.1 Read for authorial identity

State what the passage now asserts, explains or discovers. Compare it with the originating intention and accepted developments. Examine its subject, scope, force, function and destination.

A lucid passage making a different point is a substantive issue, not a successful stylistic revision. Clarify whether the difference is an intended development, a proposal needing decision or an error to repair.

At whole-account scale, trace the major developments to their place in the authorial undertaking. Check where a source, memorable example or easily expanded theme has acquired a role beyond that assigned to it. Restore the intended hierarchy of claims and supporting material. If the new role seems genuinely better, explain it as a proposal rather than treating its polished execution as consent.

### 14.2 Read for intellectual adequacy

Follow definitions, premises, mechanisms, standards and inferences. Examine the specific support each consequential relationship needs. Give proposals and definitions the same seriousness as criticism.

Locate weaknesses precisely enough to act on them. “Needs more depth” is useful only when followed by what remains undeveloped and why it matters. A review should expose the next intellectual task rather than merely grade the prose.

### 14.3 Read in the reader's order

Follow the text from its beginning as written. Notice what becomes intelligible, where significance emerges, where attention is misdirected and where the reader must invent a missing connection.

Examine rhythm, emphasis, recurrence, tonal movement and closure. A reasoning paraphrase can miss these effects. A compositional reading can miss unsupported implications. Use both and resolve consequential discrepancies.

### 14.4 Read the cooperation of the whole

Check the balance of development among major commitments, the preparation of later claims, the roles of particulars and the cumulative destination. Ask whether the account achieves what it promises and whether its parts help that achievement.

Keep the depth of investigation, amount of rewriting and reporting proportionate. Focused checks are useful within a writing cycle. Every integration of new understanding into the MD still requires the whole-account reading before and after revision in §12; a local check alone cannot establish its completion. These modes of attention cooperate in that reading rather than require compulsory separate reports.

Reviewer agreement provides feedback within the review's scope. Authorial acceptance, factual grounds and actual reader responses retain their separate standing.

## 16. Recognize development and completion

Progress is a change in the intellectual and explanatory state: an intention understood more exactly, a concept developed, a missing connection supplied, a source question resolved, an example made effective, or a revision integrated coherently.

Distinguish readiness levels:

- **A development ready for discussion:** its meaning, contribution and open choices are sufficiently clear for the author to judge.
- **A development ready for integration:** it belongs to the established authorial direction or an expressly accepted extension; its intended meaning, role, grounds, outstanding choices and consequences can be recovered well enough to compose responsibly in the authorized update. Its provisional status can be preserved where intended. Readiness does not require completion of the entire subject, and literary polish or source support alone does not establish it.
- **A developed passage:** it performs its intended function, has the grounds its current claims require, and cooperates with its surroundings.
- **A completed integration:** the new development is embodied in one coherent current account after whole-document reconsideration, revision and rereading. The rest of the subject may still require substantial work.
- **A completed phase:** the author-agreed purpose of that phase has actually been achieved, with dependencies and remaining work explicit.
- **A completed definition:** the agreed subject is adequately developed end to end, its promised connections are explained, the whole is coherent, and essential unresolved choices have been settled or explicitly left open by authorial decision.

Completion criteria come from the undertaking; the methodology helps interpret and assess them. Reclassifying an essential unanswered question does not complete it. Conversely, a conceptual definition need not claim to have executed every programme it defines.

Every substantive integration revisits the introduction, foundations and destination together. Whole completion adds a final assessment that the undertaking itself has been achieved: earlier promises, later consequences, scope and standing agree, and the explanatory work those promises require has actually been done.

Between work sessions, report:

1. **What I worked on.**
2. **What is now complete and at which evidence level.**
3. **What remains or was newly discovered.**
4. **What comes next.**

Use those headings to describe actual intellectual and editorial progress, not to repeat the methodology or an active goal.

## 17. Keep the methodology generative and proportionate

The work can reveal a need this method has not anticipated. Develop an appropriate operation and judge it by its contribution. Expand the methodology when a lesson is reusable; keep a particular argument's solution in the subject's own work context.

Learn from both modes of work. A misunderstanding in conversation may reveal an inadequate way of recovering intention; a difficult integration may reveal a missing way of tracing conceptual consequences. Identify the general authoring problem, what helped resolve it and what remains uncertain. When enough has been learned—or a consequential failure makes correction necessary—revisit the methodology and integrate the improvement throughout it.

The development of the subject and development of the method inform each other without needing to proceed in lockstep. Use the current method while discussing and writing; revise it when experience warrants a reusable improvement. Apply the same whole-account discipline to that revision, reconciling related provisions instead of accumulating addenda. This makes the method learn from the work while leaving substantive definitions and historical conclusions to the account itself.

A useful methodological improvement explains the difficulty it addresses, the operation that can help, the judgment needed to apply it and the sign of a useful result. It leaves the subject's answer open.

For example, distinguishing degree from kind is a methodological inquiry; deciding that a particular historical change belongs to one is substantive work. Investigating a causal mechanism is methodological; prescribing that mechanism is an argument. Preparing a reader for a judgment is methodological; selecting the judgment belongs to the account.

The method is properly tailored when it equips us for this project's conceptual, historical, narrative and polemical demands. It overreaches when it fixes the substantive definitions, conclusions or causal sequence that the work itself must develop. It becomes too generic when it offers virtues such as “clarity” or “rigour” without enabling the intellectual operations through which they can be achieved.

**Understand the intended act; develop the relationships it requires; select material by the work those relationships need; compose their meaning and significance; examine the result; carry admitted developments through the whole. Bring consequential new directions into our conversation. Let the actual work determine how these operations cooperate.**
