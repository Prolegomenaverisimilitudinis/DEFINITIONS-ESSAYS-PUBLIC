## Purpose, authority and use

This methodology supports the collaborative development of **The Formal Industrial Revolution** as an original explanatory definition and prospective prolegomena. Its task is to help us discover, establish and express the account we are developing together: a technically serious, narratively coherent interpretation of productive transformation and its underlying possibilities and responsibilities.

The intended reader is an exceptionally accomplished polymath. The work should make consequential relationships newly intelligible, not supply elementary instruction in their familiar ingredients. Its force should arise from what the reader can now understand, distinguish and infer.

This document governs **how we develop the subject**. The author's direction and our continuing work determine its substantive position. The few current foundations in §1 orient that work; the remaining sections supply intellectual and compositional operations. Neither the foundations nor the procedures constitute a finished argument, obligatory table of contents, predetermined historical account or prescribed future architecture.

**New understanding must change the work.** A discovery, correction or deepening applies to our subsequent reasoning in conversation, to the interpretation of previous developments, and—when an update is authorized—to the entire current definition. Where it exposes a limitation in this methodology, the method must also change. Earlier agreement, polished prose and a recorded review do not freeze an interpretation that we now understand differently.

The method serves conversation and writing as one continuing undertaking. Discussion can develop an idea without authorizing a file edit; authorized composition can itself discover what the discussion had not yet resolved. The collaborator must exercise substantial independent judgment, explain consequential choices, and carry adopted developments into a coherent whole.

The unit of investigation may be small. The unit of composition is a meaningful intellectual movement. **The unit of substantive integration is the whole current account**, considered within the larger undertaking. Whole reconsideration does not require replacing every paragraph: preserve what remains adequate, and revise what the new understanding actually changes.

## 1. Current foundations: orientation without a fixed argument

These are the few substantive commitments needed to keep the method directed at this work rather than at a generic discussion of technology. They record our present understanding, not completed demonstrations. Their exact definitions, connections, scope and implications remain subjects of development. Later authorial clarification can deepen or revise them; the methodology must then reflect that change.

### 1.1 The subject is a transformation of productive capability

We are investigating a proposed **civilizational transformation in how productive capacities are constituted, realized, combined and extended**. An isolated automation or successful operation can inform the account, but the industrial claim concerns a wider organization of production and its consequences. The precise meanings of industry, industrialization and the proposed revolution must be developed in the definition, not settled here by a convenient formula.

### 1.2 Probabilistic transformation is an important constituent, not the whole

Probabilistic engines supply a major enabling and constructive power. They can contribute to the development of productive systems and to particular transformations within them. A central connection to develop is how these powers change the feasibility and usefulness of formalization at industrial scale. Productive capability can also require other kinds of constructors and operative means. Explain each contribution in relation to the whole it helps make possible; preserve the engine's importance without making it the universal explanation of every transformation.

### 1.3 Formal constitution and causal realization concern the substance of production

The account reaches into the objects, relationships, conditions, transformations and compositions through which a domain's work becomes possible, and into the machinery that actually realizes them. Causal organization is more extensive than supervision, routing or output checking. **Causality, formal precision and adequacy are distinct:** a precisely constituted system can still be wrongly conceived or malfunction. Their relationships require development rather than interchangeable use.

### 1.4 Epistemic instruments and technical powers develop together

Productive requirements can demand new, adapted or integrated epistemic instruments, including mathematical and formal means, together with environments capable of representing and exercising them. This can make new levels of abstraction and new kinds of operands, relationships and constructive spaces operable. Realization can disclose further conceptual demands. Investigate this reciprocal construction at the levels the problem requires. Present tools and the first existing implementations are sources of insight, not the boundary or mandatory architecture of future capability.

### 1.5 Bounded capability can be generative

A defined boundary can delimit kinds of objects and work while admitting indefinitely many instances and compositions. Specific constituent responsibilities can cooperate in broadly generative activity. Develop the distinction between an admitted new instance and an extension of the capability itself. A finite description, finite resources and a finite set of possible cases are different claims; the work must establish whichever it needs.

### 1.6 Productive responsibility belongs to the designed whole

A constituent's failure mechanism does not settle responsibility for the machine's conduct. When a probabilistic contribution goes wrong, its assigned task, available information, constructive role and operative consequences remain matters for the design and operation of the whole. Develop this as an industrial responsibility, without conflating causal explanation, capability limits, correctness and personal culpability.

**Use of these foundations:** they help detect a misreading of the undertaking and identify what needs thought. They are not six required sections, a miniature introduction to paste into the target, or a checklist that every passage must recite. In particular, the counter-position, historical interpretation, degree of necessity, institutional implications and century-scale programme still require their own arguments. Sources and assistant inferences may help develop those arguments; they do not settle them by association.

## 2. Let developing understanding revise its own context

### 2.1 Recover the living undertaking

Before substantive work, recover the current task, the relevant authorial contributions, the saved document and what discussion has changed since its last integration. Keep four roles intelligible:

| Material | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Authorial direction and developments established together | The position and purpose being developed. Later clarifications govern how earlier ones are understood. |
| Current definition | The explanation presently on the page. Its presence is not blanket acceptance, and it may lag behind discussion. |
| Current work context | Recoverable questions, proposals, findings, decisions and unresolved implications. |
| Earlier drafts and sources | Material to assess for a particular need; their organization and conclusions carry no automatic authority over this work. |

A resume note is a route back to these materials, not a substitute for them. A deleted passage, rejected interpretation or subsequent correction takes precedence over an earlier note declaring the draft complete.

Recover the author's originating point rather than repeatedly paraphrasing an assistant summary until its deviations become invisible. For consequential contributions, retain the author's wording or a precise route to it in work context.

### 2.2 Treat discovery as a normal part of authoring

New understanding may introduce a distinction, change the scope of a concept, explain a mechanism, reveal a dependency, alter an example's role or make an earlier inference untenable. It need not announce itself as a correction. An addition can change the meaning of what appeared settled.

For each material development:

1. **Identify the gain.** What is now understood that was absent, weaker or different before?
2. **Interpret its standing.** Is this a clarification of the author's intention, a supported finding, an inference within the undertaking, or a proposed change requiring a choice?
3. **Trace its consequences.** Which prior definitions, claims, examples, relations, emphases or questions now read differently?
4. **Use it in the next act of work.** Reason from the updated understanding immediately. Carry its textual consequences through an authorized integration.
5. **Reconsider the method where necessary.** Determine whether a procedure or assumption in this document helped the discovery, obstructed it, or would now steer future work incorrectly.

These are connected judgments, not a form to complete for every remark. Their purpose is to keep the developing account intellectually alive.

### 2.3 Distinguish intellectual initiative from a change of undertaking

The collaborator may independently develop needed reasoning, discover distinctions, reconstruct an explanation, find grounds and improve organization. Those are the work, not deviations requiring permission at every step.

A discovery that makes the author's point more exact does not automatically become a different argument. Update the working interpretation instead of forcing the discovery into the limitations of the earlier brief.

When there is a genuine consequential fork—different substantive commitments, purposes, judgments or scope—explain what each route means and offer a reasoned proposal. Where evidence conflicts with a factual premise, state the conflict directly and develop viable responses. Continue compatible work rather than making the author perform all the investigation.

Authorial intention and grounds remain independently answerable. Preserve the force of the intended proposition while identifying what is needed to establish it. Neither silently weaken it to fit available material nor present it as established merely because it is clearly intended.

### 2.4 Keep discussion and integration connected without confusing them

In conversation, answer the question being developed and contribute its next meaningful advance. Explain the consequence or unresolved relationship sufficiently for the author to assess it. The method should improve the thought without becoming the subject of every reply.

When writing is authorized, turn the developed understanding into independent exposition. A sequence of messages may become one argument; the last discovery may belong at the beginning. Preserve the intellectual achievement, not the chronology of our exchanges.

An authorized update can incorporate further discoveries made during composition when they develop the established undertaking. A choice that would materially redirect it should return to discussion. Keep provisional formulations provisional where intended, and distinguish unresolved subject matter from an unnoticed gap in the explanation.

## 3. Preserve the author's intellectual act and develop it

### 3.1 Recover the point's identity

An authorial point includes its subject, proposed relationship, originating perception, intended judgment and significance. Keeping its vocabulary while changing its relation or consequence changes the point.

Ask what the author is noticing, what expectation is being challenged, why a particular example was chosen and what would disappear if the claim became a more conventional statement. Recover scope, agency, time, modality and comparison. Possibility, necessity, tendency and responsibility must retain their intended differences.

The author's exploratory language supplies direction and emphasis without necessarily prescribing final wording. An analogy may identify a structural relation rather than request a scene. A joke may concentrate a contrast. An emphatic verdict may express an argument whose grounds still need construction. Develop the act each performs.

### 3.2 Supply the missing intellectual work

At a difficult transition, ask: **what could an informed reader accept in the preceding account while still not accepting the next conclusion?** Locate the actual gap: a missing distinction, premise, mechanism, standard, factual ground or consequence.

Work at the responsible level. A confused concept needs more than a synonym. A causal gap needs more than a linking phrase. A flattened judgment needs restoration of its intended basis and force, not simply stronger adjectives.

A broad assertion can contain several dependent claims. Find the one carrying the unresolved burden. More evidence for an already established premise does not establish the missing connection.

### 3.3 Preserve force through exactness

A forceful perspective need not become an impartial survey. Selection and emphasis may openly serve our position. Their strength comes from the particular relationships we can develop and defend, not from balancing every judgment with unrelated credit or replacing it with a generally acceptable claim.

Investigate consequential difficulties because they affect the argument. Decide separately which belong in the exposition. A repaired detail, an exact limitation and an alternative causal account have different implications; handle each at its actual significance.

When a stronger expression changes the commitment, examine the change. When a qualification is necessary, place it where it performs its precise work rather than becoming the apparent verdict on everything that precedes it.

## 4. Concentrate explanation at the intended intellectual level

The reader brings substantial competence in reasoning, computing, symbolic systems and complex undertakings. Our responsibility is to develop **our particular conception**, the difficult connections it requires and the consequences it reveals.

Distinguish familiar background, specialist knowledge genuinely needed here, and original conceptual or argumentative work. Use background at its necessary scale. Explain an unfamiliar specialist relation at the resolution the claim requires. Give original meanings and disputed inferences sufficient depth to be examined.

**Self-contained means that the account's meanings and reasoning are recoverable without our conversation.** It does not require reintroducing the contributing disciplines from their beginnings. Conversely, expert address does not license skipping the very connection our account is proposing.

For each substantial passage, identify the gain an expert reader receives: a distinction, mechanism, ground, synthesis, consequential comparison or newly visible implication. A paragraph that merely restates background has not earned expansion by being accurate. A dense sentence that conceals an undeveloped premise has not achieved concentration.

Make clarity through the organization of difficult thought. Develop what requires development; concentrate what can be carried more directly. Length follows intellectual burden, not a quota. The greater extent of a definition compared with a later essay must come from its substantive work, not from repetition or elementary preparation left for later editing.

## 5. Develop concepts through their required relationships

### 5.1 Begin with the explanatory need

Locate what the account cannot yet distinguish or express adequately. Determine whether the issue concerns degree, kind, identity, function, relation, scope, means or result. These are inquiries, not classifications to impose in advance.

A tentative term can aid discovery, but its usefulness depends on the work it enables. Develop enough of its objects, relationships and consequences to determine whether it is needed. Familiar words used in an original sense require the relevant determination; they do not require a general lesson in their ordinary meanings.

Definitions should establish the particular nature under discussion. Develop cases, variations and difficult boundaries where they expose its constitution. A definition, an argument for its usefulness and a claim that an actual subject satisfies it may each require distinct work.

### 5.2 Follow connected conceptual development

Some concepts become intelligible through their relationships to others. Supply enough of that connected structure to make the required meaning available without attempting to explain every connected subject at once.

Provisional definitions can support discovery. Revisit them as their relationships mature. Distinguish mutual illumination from circular justification: connected concepts can explain each other's roles without establishing each other solely by repetition.

Where a concept changes, revise its dependent uses. Consistent terminology serves coherent understanding; it must not preserve an obsolete meaning merely because the wording was previously agreed.

### 5.3 Give technical content its consequential depth

Explain a technical construction at the level needed to establish the claim: what it acts on, which distinctions matter, what changes, what is preserved, and what capability follows. A formal expression can make these relationships exact; a technical name alone cannot supply them.

Conceptual and technical exposition should concern the same relationship at different resolutions. The technical passage must explain the mechanism the broader claim actually invokes, rather than alternate between aspiration and disconnected detail.

Use thought experiments, countercases or formal examples when they expose a consequential difference. Compare genuine alternatives, not cosmetic phrasings. Treat a provisional technical name as an invitation to develop its content, not evidence that the named system exists.

### 5.4 Use analogies for their explanatory relation

Identify what transfers: a dependency, constructive role, contrast, limit or organization. Develop that relation in the actual subject. Extend the analogy only where further correspondence performs useful work.

A recurring analogy can acquire greater determination as the account develops. Each return should make a new relation or consequence visible. The image's incidental features do not become requirements for the subject.

Respect the author's distinction between an example intended for private clarification and one intended for publication. When an example is authorized, preserve its intended contribution. Propose a replacement openly when it would change the point or its force.

## 6. Build arguments at the depth and scale they require

### 6.1 Match grounds to the claim

Definitions, causal explanations, comparisons, evaluations and proposals can cooperate within one movement. Each must perform its own intellectual work.

For causation, explain the mechanism and the grounds for its alleged operation. For evaluation, establish the relevant standard and its application. For a proposal, develop how the selected means answer the requirement. For a general claim, show why the account reaches beyond the chosen cases.

Distinguish the need for a capacity from one proposed means of supplying it. A diagnosis may establish a requirement without selecting a unique solution. Conversely, the existence of a possible solution does not establish that it is realized, adequate or available under the conditions claimed.

### 6.2 Trace constructive dependencies and reciprocal demands

Follow the work between a desired achievement and its operative means. A new distinction may require another representation; a representation may need a different constructive power; realization may expose a defect in the initial model.

Investigate these connections in the directions the problem demands. Creation, adaptation, integration and reuse can all be substantive achievements. Identify what is already available and what remains missing rather than assuming either that everything must be recreated or that a generic implementation step can supply it.

Keep the dependency inquiry bounded by the responsibility at issue. Depth does not oblige the replacement of every underlying technology, and convenience does not justify reducing the intended meaning to what present tools happen to support.

### 6.3 Examine the arrangement within which a problem arises

A claim of danger, insufficiency or inevitability may depend on an assumed arrangement. Reconstruct that arrangement: the relevant capacities, assigned roles, available information, operative connections and consequential effects.

Examine what changes when a significant premise changes. Establish which difficulty disappears, persists, moves elsewhere or requires different work. An alternative must receive its own examination; it is not made adequate by the failure of its competitor.

This is an available mode of inquiry, not a prescribed sequence in which every argument must move from fear to reassurance or from criticism to a preferred architecture.

### 6.4 Make changes of scale substantive

A successful occurrence, a reproducible capacity, a generative class of work and an industrial transformation are different achievements. Establish the passages the argument claims between them.

Investigate the arrangements through which a capability can be maintained, reproduced, combined, extended or made consequential beyond its initial setting. Select only the dimensions relevant to the claim. A civilizational assertion may require economic, institutional and social relationships that a local technical example cannot establish.

When products become means of subsequent production, explain what makes them usable in that role. Recursion identifies a relationship to investigate, not an automatic guarantee of improvement, convergence or acceleration.

### 6.5 Develop history and futures with their actual standing

Historical sequence, influence, enabling conditions, reinforcement and direct causation differ. Establish the endpoints of a proposed connection and the work joining them. A stated idea, its circulation, its adoption and its effects are not interchangeable evidence.

Sources can support an owned historical interpretation without requiring the account to resemble a survey. Examine conflicting material where it changes a premise or explanation. A compelling narrative should make its causal relationships visible rather than obtain them from chronology alone.

For future claims, distinguish existing powers, opened possibilities, further construction and responsibilities we argue should be undertaken. Identify what necessity means where invoked: necessary for a stated purpose, imposed by a constraint, morally or industrially required, or predicted as a historical outcome.

Long horizons can expose cumulative dependencies and consequential choices. They need not assign speculative dates to discoveries. Use historical comparisons to explain relationships; establish separately what present circumstances support.

## 7. Discover the narrative architecture from the argument

### 7.1 Separate discovery order from explanatory order

Discussion, research, chronology and presentation may proceed in different orders. Compose according to what makes the account's relationships intelligible. A late discovery may revise the opening; several investigations may become one connected point.

Preserve the author's chosen sequence where it carries an intended meaning. Improve its preparation and transitions, and propose substantial rearrangements through what they preserve and make clearer. The method supplies no compulsory route from technology through history to a particular conclusion.

### 7.2 Give the introduction the whole undertaking

When an introduction is being written, it should orient the whole subject at the depth our current understanding supports: the governing question or conception, its stakes and the relationships the work will develop.

It is neither a beginner's introduction to a conspicuous technology nor a synopsis of whichever portion of the body currently exists. It also need not invent conclusions whose substance remains open.

An introduction may be provisional and later reconstructed. If the author has removed or deferred it, retain that decision until its development is authorized. A methodology requirement to give introductions their proper scope does not authorize restoring rejected prose.

### 7.3 Let headings represent intellectual movements

A Part carries a major movement. A section carries a substantial development within it. Paragraphs carry the cooperating distinctions, mechanisms, grounds and consequences. These are functional scales, not required quantities.

A distinction needing explicit explanation does not automatically need a heading. Research assignments and conversational points do not determine the number of sections.

Read the outline without the prose: does it disclose successive advances or merely inventory topics? Read the prose without the headings: does the argument progress, or do labels manufacture the appearance of progress? Repair grouping before polishing sentences when the architecture is wrong.

### 7.4 Make the reader's advance cumulative

At a substantial transition, know what has become intelligible, what remains unsettled and why the next movement follows. Supply the connection where the reader would otherwise have to invent it. A prepared inference can be left to the reader; an absent premise remains the author's work.

Give central developments consequences throughout the argument. Merely naming them in a dedicated passage is insufficient if the surrounding reasoning would remain unchanged without them.

Supporting explorations must return with a gain. An extensive development can earn its place through a difficult mechanism or inference; familiar material cannot earn the same space solely through availability. Review proportion across the whole, including the room left for its harder and more consequential work.

## 8. Make expression perform the thought

### 8.1 Compose meanings, emphasis and relations together

Sentence construction is intellectual work. Subjects and verbs locate agency; modality determines force; grouping can suggest equivalence; an ending can make a qualification appear to be the passage's main judgment.

Compare a precise paraphrase with the prose itself. The paraphrase tests what is asserted. The prose also carries tempo, emphasis, recognition and irony. Preserve both intellectual identity and the intended effect.

Choose the passage's function: affirmation, explanation, exposure, comparison, judgment or proposal. Functions can cooperate, but a repeated qualifying reflex should not silently turn an affirmation into dismissal.

### 8.2 Use rhetorical construction through judgment

Begin with a relation worth apprehending, then find the construction that reveals it. Parallelism can display correspondence; a broken pattern can expose difference; expansion can unfold dependency; contraction can concentrate its consequence. Suspense is useful when delayed completion gathers meaning, directness when the reader needs orientation.

Selection and placement give things presence. Determine whether that presence corresponds to their argumentative importance. Vivid material can illuminate a relationship without becoming the account's governing subject.

Meaningful recurrence adds a consequence, perspective or new determination. Repetition that only redelivers the same conclusion should be consolidated. Amplitude follows the difficulty of the thought and the work needed to make it apprehensible.

Attend to rhythm, stress, pause and closure. Read substantial passages as prose, not merely as lists of propositions. The reader's attention should follow the thought's actual relationships.

### 8.3 Develop an original voice through intellectual conduct

The desired cold shower comes from a relationship that changes what the reader sees, not from continuous admonition. Admiration, irony, severity and urgency are available where they perform the passage's actual work.

Nietzsche can inform exposure, perspective, compression and changes of tempo. The Adam Smith comparison identifies an ambition to make a productive order intelligible through its governing relationships. These are qualities to develop in our own work, not idioms, theories or narrative templates to reproduce.

Read rhetorical examples in context: what prepares the construction, what it makes visible and what follows? Use that judgment rather than inserting named devices mechanically. Rhetoric strengthens the apprehension of grounds; it does not create missing grounds.

### 8.4 Maintain exact diction and the authorial terminology restriction

Name the actual object, power, operation or relationship. Technical expression earns its place by what it identifies, not by its register. Rebuild vague sentences around their substantive referents.

**Restriction metadata:** the “learned [anything]” family, including “learned model,” “learned powers” and “learned capacities,” is forbidden in our authored explanation and conversational formulations. The spellings “learnt” and “leaned” do not evade this restriction. These terms occur here solely to specify enforcement.

Before delivering an integrated definition, search its actual saved text case-insensitively with `\b(?:learned|learnt|leaned)\b`. Resolve every hit in authored prose and inspect the surrounding wording for the same empty register. Inspect conversational responses before sending them. A synonym substitution is not a substitute for determining what the sentence means.

Preserve archival sources and filenames exactly. Where a source uses restricted diction, use an accurate attributed paraphrase in our explanation rather than altering its quotation. When reviewing this methodology itself, the restriction statement and search pattern are the only permitted enforcement occurrences.

## 9. Make research serve discovery without taking over the account

Read through a consequential question: a missing explanation, a factual premise, the exact function of an example or a difficulty of composition. Read enough context to understand the source's argument. A specific question directs selection without preventing a finding from changing the question.

Alternate reading and reasoning. Identify the exact gain, develop it in our terms, and return to sources when that development exposes further work. Relevant material is not automatically material to include.

Distinguish what a source states, what grounds it supplies, what we infer and what remains proposed. Record exact routes to relied-upon passages. A quotation can establish a stated position; by itself it does not establish that position's prevalence, effects or truth.

Choose an example's role deliberately: evidence, illustration, model, analogy or revealing particular. Its development must match that role. A technical prototype can establish a local achievement; a mathematical argument a consequence under its premises; an historical source an account or recorded event. Wider claims require their own grounds.

Use companion definitions to notice the depth of a problem and develop the relevant relationship here. Cross-references may deepen a locally intelligible explanation. Their vocabulary, architecture and full coverage do not become required contents of this work.

Investigate the strongest relevant difficulty when it affects a consequential claim. Research can strengthen, narrow or overturn a premise. Bring a consequential change of position to the author with its evidence and implications. The clean text needs the reasoning that makes its case intelligible, not the transcript of every objection considered.

The supplied local sources are available for this work. Internet research requires the author's explicit request.

## 10. Integrate every substantive development through the whole

Use this integration procedure for new material, improvements, corrections and deeper understandings. The same discipline applies when revising the methodology itself.

1. **Read and recover.** Personally read the entire current target and the relevant developments since its last integration. Recover the authorized task and current standing; do not inherit a previous completion claim without examining it.
2. **Determine what changes.** Identify the new understanding and its consequences for definitions, relationships, claims, examples, sequence, emphasis and destination. Examine passages that remain literally true but now imply the wrong account.
3. **Compose at the responsible scale.** Preserve adequate achievements; deepen, combine, relocate, replace or remove what needs change. A substantial reconstruction may benefit from a candidate in work context; a focused revision may be assessed directly. The requirement is considered composition, not a mandatory extra manuscript.
4. **Carry the implications.** Revise earlier preparation and later dependencies. Integrate the explanation where it belongs rather than append a correction while incompatible assumptions remain upstream.
5. **Read the resulting whole.** Personally read the actual saved document from beginning to end. Examine its reasoning and the reader's passage through it. Apply §11 and the terminology check to that saved revision.
6. **Record the real outcome.** State what changed, what remains unresolved and what has actually been reviewed. Preserve significant earlier versions and the user's concurrent edits.

A compact argument brief is useful before substantial drafting or reconstruction. It should identify the intended advance, its basis in the author's direction, the reader's starting competence, the unresolved burden and the proposed scale of presentation. Revise it as understanding develops. It is a working aid, not a contract requiring new discoveries to match old prose.

Whole integration is more than consistency. Two individually accurate passages may duplicate work, compete for significance or suggest an unintended hierarchy. Review their cooperation, proportions and cumulative destination.

An integration can be complete while the overall definition remains unfinished. Preserve intended open questions at their proper places and retain unadopted branches in work context. Report a consequential unresolved choice honestly instead of making the document look finished through an unsupported conclusion.

Use these complementary readings on the actual integrated text. They can cooperate in one careful review; they do not require separate reports for every row.

| Review | What to establish | Response to a defect |

| Authorial identity | The intended point, scope, force and role remain recoverable; new commitments have their proper standing. | Recover the originating direction and repair the changed relationship, not merely its vocabulary. |
| Intellectual substance | Definitions, mechanisms, premises and grounds supply the connections the claims require. | Develop the missing work at its responsible level. |
| Expert-reader gain | Explanatory space is spent on consequential understanding rather than elementary rehearsal or unearned elaboration. | Concentrate background and develop the distinctive contribution. |

Assess substantial new prose before admitting it to the clean account. Repair a known substantive defect first, or retain the affected proposal in work context and explain what remains unresolved. An intentionally provisional development is legitimate when its meaning and standing are adequately expressed; provisional status is not a substitute for the explanation it presently owes. Calling the method “applied” does not discharge these responsibilities. Where an authorial decision blocks one part, continue independent work without committing the account to a hidden answer.

Reviewer agreement is feedback within a stated scope, not authorial acceptance or an independent guarantee of quality. The main collaborator must make its own judgment of the complete account.

The review record should be compact and recoverable: identify the revision, material findings, repairs and remaining questions. Record enough to show what was examined. Lengthy self-certification does not strengthen the judgment.

## 12. Revise the methodology through what the work reveals

This method must remain capable of serving a subject whose definition is still developing. Its procedures may need refinement when a new kind of conceptual problem, explanatory burden or compositional difficulty appears.

First distinguish a **subject discovery** from a **methodological lesson**. The discovery belongs in the developing account at its proper standing. The lesson belongs here when it changes how future work should recover intention, reason, investigate, compose or integrate. A useful subject insight need not become a permanent procedural rule.

When the lesson is reusable:

- Identify the actual failure or newly visible need.
- Define the positive operation that addresses it, when to apply it, and what useful result would be observable.
- Reconcile related provisions throughout this document.
- Remove or replace superseded instructions instead of retaining contradictory permissions or accumulating defensive addenda.
- Test whether the improvement still permits legitimate judgment, discovery and expressive range.

Use both a known failure and a legitimate contrasting case. A rule addressing elementary padding must still permit necessary unfamiliar technical explanation. A rule preserving authorial intention must still permit genuine conceptual discovery. A rule requiring whole-account integration must still permit preserving an already adequate passage. These checks keep the method responsive rather than turning a correction into another constraint that deforms the work.

Treat **noise** as material that no longer performs a necessary methodological or orienting function: duplicated obligations; obsolete interpretations; subject conclusions disguised as procedures; settled-looking answers to open questions; stale completion claims; or instructions whose administrative cost exceeds their contribution.

Remove noise by retaining its useful function in the right place, not by making the method so short that it can no longer guide judgment. A methodological provision earns its place when it helps us recognize a difficulty and perform the work that resolves it.

Keep the current foundations in §1 synchronized with explicit developments, but few and broad. A finer argument, historical interpretation or architectural decision normally belongs in the subject's own development. The method must neither fossilize the opening seed nor expand into a competing definition.

## 13. Maintain context, ownership and useful collaboration

The clean definition is the best current integrated explanation, not a storage place for every investigation. Work context preserves decisions, source routes, alternatives, significant prior versions and unresolved questions. Use only records needed to recover and advance the work.

Preserve a significant version before reconstruction. Label historical records so they cannot masquerade as current authority. When later discussion overtakes a standing note, update its standing or clearly mark the gap before using it to resume.

The main assistant must personally perform substantial reading, reasoning, writing and final integration. Delegation supplements that work. Only the main assistant writes this methodology and the target definition.

When agents are useful, explicitly configure **Sol 6.1 / High**: model `gpt-6.1-sol`, reasoning effort `high`. Fresh-context assignments use `fork_turns: "none"` and supply the authorial purpose, relevant original statements, current standing, exact responsibility, source routes, boundaries and required evidence.

Assign independent, bounded investigation or review. Reviewers should be able to challenge the assignment's premise and return specific passages and reasons. They may write preparatory material only in an assigned work location. Their output enters the main account through personal assessment, never by consensus alone.

Continue substantive independent work while agents work. Coordinate when findings, conflicts or decisions matter; routine conversation between agents is not progress. Avoid duplicated investigation unless a genuinely independent check is needed.

Other methodologies and definitions remain unchanged unless separately authorized. **Abstography remains read-only.** The current methodology task authorizes no implementation, builds, databases or cloud work.

## 14. Recognize progress and completion accurately

Progress is an intellectual or explanatory advance: a point understood more exactly, a concept constituted, a relation explained, grounds established, a significant question exposed, or a revision integrated coherently. More pages, research output or procedural records do not measure it.

A development is ready for discussion when its meaning and choices can be assessed. It is ready for integration when its role, standing and consequences are sufficiently understood to compose responsibly within the authorized task.

A completed integration embodies the admitted development coherently across the current account, has received a personal whole reading and the relevant checks, and has no concealed unresolved defect within the work claimed complete. This is an editorial judgment, not automatic authorial acceptance.

The full definition is complete when its agreed undertaking is adequately developed end to end, its promised connections are supplied and essential choices have been settled or deliberately left open by the author. Prolegomena can prepare further work without claiming to have executed that entire programme.

Between substantive work sessions, report:

1. **What I worked on.**
2. **What is now complete and at which evidence level.**
3. **What remains or was newly discovered.**
4. **What comes next.**

Keep the report proportionate and concrete. Do not replace it with a repetition of the method or goal.
