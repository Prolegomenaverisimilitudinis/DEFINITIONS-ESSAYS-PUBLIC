# Opening review — 9 October 2026

Reviewed the complete active goal, working state/evidence notes, and candidate opening plus §§1–5 only. Later sections were being written concurrently and are outside this review. No definition or candidate was edited. Source checks used the actual readable local books below.

The opening meets its main obligation: it starts with existing domains, recognizes their formal achievements, examines functional human contributions, and includes distributed arrangements. The examples do not support a universal deficiency, and the candidate correctly avoids claiming one. I found no need for a new cognitive theory, blanket automation, or a specialized industrial definition at the beginning.

## Consequential local corrections

### 1. Separate discovering an answer from constituting an arrangement — §4.3, with consequences for §§3.2 and 5.2

The acquisition/constitution distinction is useful but should explicitly remain non-exhaustive. A required empirical answer may not yet be known: inspection, measurement, experiment or inference must establish it. It is neither existing information awaiting retrieval nor a fact made true by a design decision. Including “interpretation” inside constitutive dependency makes that boundary especially easy to miss.

Grounds: A2 establishes decisions needed to settle project-specific arrangements; it does not establish that all non-retrievable inputs are created. The same book's preceding “Design changes” discussion, printed pp. 88–89, identifies unforeseen ground conditions and discoveries when opening existing buildings. Those subjects exist independently of the newly obtained information. The distinction is a reasoned implication of those contrasting cases, not a taxonomy claimed by the source.

Proposed correction: add that inquiry may have to establish what is the case, whereas selection/design constitutes what arrangement will be adopted. A dependency can combine these contributions; recognizing it does not guarantee that inquiry will yield a determinate answer. Preserve the two useful names without presenting a universal binary inventory.

### 2. Constitution also occurs in inherited subjects — §1.5

The empirical-properties/formal-construction contrast can leave readers thinking that constitutive rules occur only in deliberately constructed formal domains. Existing domains also concern agreements, classifications, institutional roles and other objects whose identity depends on established conventions or decisions. A formal account may describe those prior rules; writing the account need not enact them.

Grounds: this is a conceptual counterexample to the opening's apparent division, not a new empirical claim. A2's existing procurement and design-management arrangements also illustrate subjects already partly constituted through decisions and procedures. The goal requires understanding inherited organization before reconstructing it.

Proposed correction: add one sentence distinguishing an account that represents existing physical or conventionally constituted subjects from defining a new object/rule within the proposed domain. This strengthens the already useful separation of subject, knowledge and account; it need not expand into social ontology.

### 3. Make the threshold for “formal” concrete — §5.1

“Sufficiently exact accounts for the claims made through them” gives the right use-relative direction, but alone can describe any careful prose account. The next paragraph mentions carriers, relations, operations and laws without yet saying what their explicit constitution enables a reader or realizing arrangement to distinguish.

Proposed correction: specify that where such claims are made, the account must distinguish legitimate formation and interpretation, warranted consequences, and unresolved choices or external contributions. The point is accountable structure, rather than mandatory symbolic notation or a universal machine grammar. §5.3 already supplies a good example: assessable requirements and guaranteed invariants have different consequences. This is a proposed sharpening of the definition, not a source theorem, and later sections may already provide its development.

### 4. Attribute the knowing/using/performing triad as synthesis — §3.3 / P1

Anderson supports the declarative/procedural distinction in the introduction (converted PDF pp. 18–19, source lines 391–400). Chapter 1 also discusses competence/performance (converted pp. 27–28) and reviews Pylyshyn's algorithm/functional-architecture distinction (converted pp. 29–31). These are related distinctions with different purposes; they do not directly establish one three-part classification of knowing a statement, knowing its use, and possessing the capacity to perform it.

Proposed correction: say Anderson's distinctions help motivate this account's separation, while the three responsibilities and their application to domain definition are our synthesis. The existing qualification about not endorsing a cognitive architecture should stay. No withdrawal of P1 is needed.

## Source checks and smaller corrections

- **A1:** *Tall Building Design*, §3.15.2.6, printed p. 103, supports the model/output/downstream-use issue. It specifically concerns VLLRS beams whose two end joints are connected to a rigid diaphragm. The source says no axial force is reported; it does not show an exported numerical zero. Tighten the example to that condition and distinguish “none reported under this idealization” from the explanatory zero. The draft's general interface inference is well marked and defensible.
- **A2:** *Collaborative Design Management*, case study 5B, printed pp. 90–93, supports all three delayed-selection examples and the designed/piloted management protocol, orientation meetings and collaborative planning workshops. The interpretation about constitutive dependency is our reasoned generalization. The interventions addressed communication/collaboration; the source does not demonstrate that every selection dependency was subsequently resolved.
- **A3:** *The Building Systems Integration Handbook*, printed pp. 18–20, especially source lines 793–824, supports the method for system distinctions/relations and questions supplied by the book with answers supplied by architects. Its printed p. 5 (lines 318–324) explicitly limits its account to recognition, description and a context for integration. The candidate does not overstate it as a complete generative system.
- **P2:** Hutchins's introduction, converted PDF pp. 16–20 (especially lines 301–305 and 355–385), directly supports the extended unit of analysis and tools carrying structured means of computation. Chapter 3, converted pp. 275–277 (lines 5505–5582), also distinguishes observable system organization from unobserved individual internal processes. The candidate's distributed contribution and cognitive-mechanism limit are appropriate. No missing-readable-text problem exists.
