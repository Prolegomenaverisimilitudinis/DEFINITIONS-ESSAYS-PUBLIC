# The Formal Industrial Revolution — Working Definition

**Status:** Provisional foundations and possible introductory material — 10 October 2026. The central direction has been clarified; the complete definition, explanatory sequence and eventual title remain under development.

**Purpose:** To define the Formal Industrial Revolution as a proposed transformation in our means of knowing and producing, and as a responsibility to develop that transformation. This is a conceptual working document, not a finished essay or a claim that the transformation has already occurred.

The opening material acknowledges a major technological opportunity. The foundations that follow establish the wider subject it may introduce. The detailed account of insufficiency, the argument about epistemological abdication and the final bridge between them remain to be developed.

## I. Provisional introductory material — The programmable probabilistic engine

### 1. The achievement to acknowledge

The development of transformer-based models, together with the training and technical developments around them, has made an extraordinary capability available: **programmable probabilistic engines through which we can configure transformations over our symbolic representations, including newly defined objects, rules and tasks.**

The importance of this achievement is not exhausted by the range of subjects a model has encountered. The engine brings a learned capacity to interpret our language and semantic relationships. Through that capacity, we can introduce an organization of objects and operations that was not individually implemented in advance, explain what those objects mean, specify their relationships and rules, and program work over them.

The objects need not already belong to a prepared application. We can create our own operands and define what transformations should be performed upon them. Their intelligibility comes from the definitions and relationships through which we introduce them, drawing upon the engine's learned organization of our symbolic world. This allows novelty in both **what is operated upon** and **what operation is configured**.

Making new subject matter computationally usable can require developing dedicated representations and operations for its interpretation and manipulation. A substantial part of that task-specific work can now be supplied through definitions and instructions interpreted by the engine. We can specify new meanings, rules and tasks in the language it already handles, rather than separately implementing each corresponding interpretation and transformation.

This is the sense in which we can avoid constructing a task-specific semantic and operational model from the ground up on every occasion: the engine's learned interpretive capacities allow newly defined arrangements to become usable through the program supplied to it.

That flexibility also creates an opportunity to develop new epistemic instruments. Interpreting a supplied definition and developing the concepts, relations or methods required by a new problem are distinct achievements. The engine can contribute to both. Reducing the burden of task-specific implementation can enlarge our capacity to engineer new intellectual means; it does not make that engineering unnecessary.

### 2. A generative space of transformations

A program can specify a stable responsibility while admitting different input compositions and different resulting work. The input arrangement can be defined while its particular contents vary. The required output arrangement can be defined while the particular product must be constructed. Different inputs can therefore call for different intermediate transformations under the same program.

**The freedom is generative rather than enumerative:** we can configure new combinations of operands, relationships and transformations instead of remaining confined to individually anticipated commands. The space of such configurations is open-ended, although the engine's actual competence and the conditions of its operation remain bounded. Expressing a program and establishing faithful performance are distinct achievements.

### 3. Its potential place in productive life

The analogy with electricity concerns the place such a capability could occupy: a foundational enabling power, incorporated into many different arrangements and serving many different purposes. Its significance need not depend on treating it as a single autonomous intelligence responsible for establishing all the powers of those arrangements.

The achievement opens an immense field of further construction. It gives us a flexible means of working upon inherited symbolic material, introducing new organizations within it, and producing transformations that would otherwise require substantial task-specific implementation.

Its place in this account is therefore that of a major enabling opportunity. The Formal Industrial Revolution concerns the wider development of our means of knowing and producing, including technologies whose importance does not depend on their relationship to language models.

### 4. The broader question this opening may introduce

The question is not confined to what must be added around a probabilistic engine to make it more useful or dependable. The wider question is:

> **How can we develop and integrate our epistemic and operative technologies into formal organizations through which existing and newly created productive capabilities can be understood, designed, composed and controlled?**

The engine is one powerful means through which that undertaking can be pursued. Its arrival enlarges what we can attempt; it does not already constitute the whole undertaking.

Whether this opening should lead directly to the foundations below or first to an account of epistemological abdication remains an explanatory decision. The introduction should follow the argument we establish.

## II. Foundations of the proposed transformation

### 1. Technology includes epistemic means

**Technology is understood here as deliberately developed means for exercising a capability:** organized knowledge and techniques embodied in tools, processes or systems through which particular transformations can be performed.

Epistemic means belong within that definition. Formal languages, methods of reasoning, representational systems and techniques of proof are developed means through which we can distinguish, explain, infer, construct and evaluate knowledge. Their technological character does not begin when they are implemented in software or machinery. Their operative form can already be a symbolic system, a method or an organized intellectual procedure.

Their embodiment in computational, material or other productive arrangements is further technological development. The distinction is between kinds and forms of technology, not between intellectual means that are supposedly outside technology and the artifacts that finally make them technological.

This matters because the proposed revolution concerns the entire connected undertaking: the invention of epistemic instruments, their combination and adaptation, the systems that embody them, and the arrangements through which their capabilities become practically available. These are distinguishable responsibilities, but none can be understood adequately by ignoring its consequential relationships with the others.

### 2. The central direction of the Formal Industrial Revolution

**The Formal Industrial Revolution names, provisionally, the deliberate development and integration of inherited and newly created epistemic and operative technologies within formal systems through which our means of knowing and producing can be understood, designed, composed and exercised with greater justified control.**

It is proposed as a responsibility of the twenty-first century: to bring existing intellectual and technical resources into such relationships, and to create the additional means required where those resources are insufficient. Its direction is toward enlarging productive capability, accelerating knowledge creation, increasing the means of abundance and strengthening our ability to shape our future deliberately.

The scope is not limited to making current activities faster. It includes developing the instruments through which new activities, products and classes of capability can become intelligible, definable and realizable.

Nor is this merely the retrospective formal description of what already works. The formal organization can participate in determining what can be constructed next, which means are needed, and how their combination could make a previously unavailable accomplishment possible.

“Formal” identifies the substantive constitution of those objects, relationships and possibilities. “Industrial” concerns their place in sustained productive capability. “Revolution” names the proposed depth and reach of the transformation; the conditions that would warrant that designation must be developed rather than inferred from the name.

### 3. What formal integration must accomplish

The aim is to make the consequential organization of knowledge and technologies itself available for reasoning, construction and operative use.

For a capability, this can require establishing what it accomplishes, what it depends upon, under which circumstances it applies, what its results mean to another activity, and what must remain true when it is combined with other means. A change to one constituent may alter the possibilities or validity of another. A desired accomplishment may expose a missing instrument or a missing relationship between instruments that already exist.

A catalogue can tell us that two technologies are available. A sufficiently developed formal relationship should additionally let us establish how their capabilities can participate in a common construction, which premises that participation requires, and what follows from it. Availability, compatibility and successful joint operation are different achievements.

Existing technologies consequently become subjects of formal understanding and design in their own right. New technologies can be developed within that organization and extend what it makes possible. Formal integration is productive when its relationships can guide or constitute actual work, not merely accompany it as descriptions.

The ambition to bring our knowledge and technologies into this organization is comprehensive in direction. It does not require treating every subject as identical, imposing one notation upon all knowledge, or pretending that every relevant relationship is already known. Different representations and methods may need precise correspondences and distinct grounds. Their exact organization remains a matter for development.

### 4. Development begins with a need or a potentiality

A need exposes something that cannot yet be adequately understood, represented, connected, designed, controlled or produced. A potentiality identifies a way of doing such things that could enlarge our capabilities even where no established practice has yet demanded it.

Either can require more than selecting an existing instrument. The response may involve creating a new epistemic tool, combining existing tools, adapting their relations or developing a specialized instrument for the problem. Some tools may be widely reusable; others may be appropriate to a particular class of circumstances. Generality and specialization are different forms of usefulness, not an automatic ranking of intellectual value.

This work extends through its operative consequences. A new conceptual distinction can require a new representational form. A new formal construction can require a language or computational capability that existing systems do not supply. A new system can enable work that reveals a further epistemic deficiency. Conversely, attempts to embody an instrument can expose an inadequacy in its original definition.

**Epistemic invention, method development, technical embodiment and productive operation must therefore be able to inform and transform one another.** They do not form a one-way sequence in which thought is completed first and implementation merely follows.

The practical limits of a current realization must be known. Those limits can also become the reasons for developing successor technologies rather than being silently accepted as permanent limits on what can be understood or accomplished.

### 5. Formal means are constructive as well as justificatory

Formal methods are not introduced principally as a corrective wrapper around a probabilistic engine. Their role can begin with the creation of the very distinctions and relationships through which a new class of work becomes possible.

They can constitute admissible constructions, make dependencies explicit, establish composition laws, define what a transformation preserves, and allow consequences to be derived across more than one individually anticipated case. Checking a completed candidate is one use. Defining a generative capability is another.

This is why epistemic technologies are themselves part of the productive means to be engineered. Their development can change what we can design, how several capacities can be combined, and which questions can be stated or answered adequately.

The intended control concerns those relationships as well as individual outputs. It includes establishing what follows from a construction, which effects or properties are justified under stated premises, where a result may be used, and what a proposed change would affect.

Formal consequence, empirical adequacy and faithful realization require their appropriate grounds. The organization must preserve their connection without treating them as interchangeable. Greater control means a stronger ability to understand, intervene in and revise our productive arrangements; it does not mean that naming a formal system makes the external world wholly predictable.

### 6. Industrial capability and knowledge creation

The industrial concern is the constitution of means that can support continuing productive work across their intended range of circumstances. A particular successful result is one occurrence. The capability concerns the organization through which further results can be formed, related and used, and through which failures or changed requirements can be addressed.

Its products can be material, computational or intellectual. Its constructive work can include the creation of further productive means. An epistemic instrument can make a new technical capability possible; that capability can support inquiry or construction that was previously impractical.

Knowledge creation therefore belongs within the proposed transformation, rather than appearing only as an external source of instructions for industry. More powerful means of forming explanations, constructing models, examining alternatives, organizing experiments and establishing consequences can enlarge what we are able to discover.

Generating a candidate and establishing its standing remain different responsibilities. The productive ambition is to develop the means through which both can be performed more adequately, and through which established contributions remain available for further criticism and construction.

### 7. The civilizational direction and the Enlightenment analogy

The proposed responsibility is to develop these means toward greater human capability: the ability to address harder problems, create more powerful productive arrangements, extend access to their benefits and deliberately improve the conditions of life.

Abundance and the shaping of our future express the purposes of the undertaking. They are not automatic consequences of formalization. Their realization depends on what capabilities are created, how they are embodied and used, and the purposes and arrangements through which their benefits become available.

The Enlightenment analogy is being used here to indicate a constructive ambition: developing intelligible models and methods through which new powers of action become possible. It is not yet a historical explanation of industrial development.

The proposed additional layer makes our own means of knowing and producing subjects of deeper formal organization. We continue to investigate the world, while also developing the means through which knowledge, instruments and technologies can be deliberately integrated into further capability. The relevant historical comparisons and the limits of the analogy remain to be examined.

## III. The prospective account of epistemological abdication

The wider responsibility above gives the possible critique a more substantial subject.

The risk is not exhausted by trusting an unsupported answer or allowing an error to propagate through a long chain of inference. It also concerns abandoning the work of developing the epistemic and operative means required for an advance, because we expect an unspecified artificial intelligence to make that work unnecessary.

On this proposed account, acquiring a powerful instrument increases the possibilities and responsibilities of construction. Abdication would occur where that acquisition is treated as discharging those responsibilities already.

The engine can contribute to explanations, methods, proofs, designs and corrective arrangements. Its participation is not the problem. The question is how those contributions acquire appropriate grounds and become constituents of a dependable capability.

This identifies a direction of inquiry, not a completed diagnosis of contemporary society. We still need to define the relevant forms of abdication, examine what would establish their occurrence, and explain the relation between epistemic responsibility and legitimate delegation. If the title uses “continued abdication,” the earlier tendency that is supposedly continued must also be established.

## IV. What remains to be defined

The foundations now identify a broader subject than the improvement or control of one class of engine. The following substantive work remains:

- Develop the meaning of industrial capability and the transformation that would warrant the term “revolution.”
- Explain the relevant forms of formal integration, including what they must make constructible and controllable.
- Define the insufficiencies of the programmable probabilistic engine relative to particular intended uses, without making those insufficiencies the definition of the entire undertaking.
- Develop the account of epistemological abdication, its grounds and its possible continuity with earlier tendencies.
- Clarify the relationship between the proposed civilizational purposes, technical achievements and evidence of practical improvement.
- Decide the explanatory sequence and whether the acknowledgment of the Artificial is the eventual introduction or another part of the account.

These are development responsibilities, not settled answers supplied by the outline. The present document establishes the direction and the connections already clarified.
