# Second-cycle review — Popper fidelity and argumentative scope

10 October 2026. Bounded review of the revised working definition, §§1, 4–6, 11, 14 and 18, against the previously investigated *Myth of the Framework* conversion, with fresh rereading of its decisive passages. No internet, no main-document edits. Main line numbers refer to the second source-grounded development as read for this review; source line numbers refer to `sources/Popper-Myth-of-the-Framework-reading.md`.

## Verdict

No blocking Popper source-fidelity defect found. The revision now has a connected explanatory movement, preserves the status of that movement as the author's proposed history, and attributes Popper's polemics without making them a completed census. It respects the user's exclusion of nineteenth-century scientific achievement from the indictment. The present application to available knowledge, hardware, formalization and productive means is introduced in §11 after the historical diagnosis. There are two small concrete repairs and one optional fidelity refinement below.

## Concrete repairs

### 1. The first paragraph of the abandoned-house section sounds more completed than its argument

Main §6.10, line 323: “The gathering has dispersed. The vows of omnipotence have exhausted themselves. Even the confidence that the tavern could continue forever has gone.” These are vivid within the analogy, but their unqualified placement after the attributed historical passages can briefly suggest an accomplished universal intellectual transition. Line 325 then correctly defines the half-century claim as proposed, limited in scope and historically unestablished; line 327 correctly allows old errors to continue circulating. The tension is local, not a failure of the whole section.

Minimum repair: begin the paragraph's second sentence with a scope marker, for example, “In the proposed trajectory of the house, the gathering has dispersed.” Keep the metaphor's force and the remainder of the section. No additional disclaimer paragraph or retreat from the user's abandoned-house thesis is needed.

Popper's text supports the possibility of unlearning constraints and the obligation to work, but does not establish that all ideological gatherings had dispersed: chapter 2, §XII, 830–838; §XVI, 890–908; Author's Note, 154–158. The main's later qualification is correct; putting the narrative scope at the first assertion avoids momentary misattribution.

### 2. The register's addendum locator lands before the addendum

Main §18.2, line 659, M3: the link labelled “the addendum” points to source line 1136. That line is note 11 from the preceding paper. The addendum heading is source line 1142, “ADDENDUM 1974: THE FRANKFURT SCHOOL.”

Minimum repair: change only this target from `:1136` to `:1142`. The register's chapter and addendum description is accurate.

## Optional fidelity refinement

Main §6.8, line 303 says Popper distinguishes attitudes that “can help discussion” from agreement on basic assumptions. This is compatible with his text, but slightly softer than source chapter 2, §III, line 622, which says such attitudes “may indeed be preconditions.” A useful precise formulation would be “attitudes that may be practical conditions of discussion—willingness to understand and learn—from prior agreement on basic assumptions.”

This is optional, not a defect requiring a new argument. The existing passage correctly rejects a universal doctrinal gate. The essential nuance is that Popper does not promise productive discussion in every encounter: source §IV, 640–646, says agreement is not assured and the gulf can usually, not invariably, be bridged. The main's allowance for unproductive encounters and redirected effort preserves that nuance. Do not replace the passage with a universal claim that a shared framework of rational values must first be accepted by both parties.

## Checks passed and why

**Creation and criticism.** Main §4.1, 114–120 correctly separates Popper's conjectural account of Thales from recorded instruction, and makes explanatory creation part of the inheritance. Source chapter 2, §§V–VI, 652–738, especially 702–708 and 730–732, supports this. The European summons does not import Popper's sweeping claim of unique European critical tradition from chapter 9, 2538.

**Industrial roots without worship of power.** Main §4.2, 136–138 separates Bacon's influential vision from the adequacy of his method and distinguishes expanding capability from deification. Source chapter 9, §§V–X, 2580–2596 and 2622–2658, supports this. The main does not claim that philosophical ideas alone caused industrialization or that knowledge has worth only as power.

**Obscurity and transmission.** Main §6.3, 220–228 accurately combines learning versus winning with the intergenerational reproduction of impressive opacity. Source chapter 2, §VII, 742–746, and chapter 3, §§IV–VI, 1030–1052, support the mechanism. The revision explicitly allows sincere participants and distinguishes necessary technical difficulty. This avoids turning Popper's criticism into a claim that every obscure author consciously perpetrates fraud.

**Disillusionment and the wider trajectory.** Main §6.4, 232–242 correctly attributes disappointed expectations of decisive victory to chapter 2, §VIII, 752. It attributes the addendum's despair/improvement criticism as a situated polemic. The proposed obscurity → failed promise → despair → abstention sequence is a synthesis by this document, not an assertion that Popper narrated precisely that complete universal sequence. Main §6.1, 198, and §18.3, 671–675 make this status sufficiently explicit.

One distinction to preserve in later polishing: source addendum line 1156 relates Adorno's despair to Hitler's rise and the loss of a Marxist promise of salvation; source 1172 again says history failed that school. Popper does not establish that obscure prose by itself caused Adorno's despair. The main currently does not say that. Do not make the proposed sequence a single causal biography through further compression.

**Clearing without abolition.** Main §§6.8–6.10, 290–339 correctly defines clearing as making obstructions criticizable, not ending all disputes or eliminating every opponent. The theory of fallible inquiry is positive construction. Tarski is neither a universal empirical oracle nor evidence that nobody besides Popper did serious work. The last-fifty-years abandonment remains the user's proposed historical diagnosis; it is explicitly not established by the metaphor or by a census of publications.

**Science and chronology.** Main §5.4 explicitly exempts nineteenth-century scientists; §6.7 places the relevant warning within twentieth-century attitudes. Source chapter 3, 1040–1048 is a contemporary warning, including conditional predictions of catastrophe, not proof that all specialists had already ended science. Main line 280 correctly represents it as a warning. Source note 8 at 1130 even marks Popper's disputed claim that scientific routine became normal after 1939. There is no need to import that sweeping date into the main.

**Present reality and constructive scope.** Main §11, 459–496 properly names existing domain knowledge, exact means, hardware, software and organizations as the present situation builders must investigate. It does not announce a new programme of natural science, confuse formal derivation with empirical confirmation, or make present infrastructure the final limit of possible construction. Earlier general discussion of machines and science prepares the responsibility; the specific current application remains in the constructive ending rather than rewriting the historical diagnosis.

**Positive summons and dated sources.** Main §14, 550–554 faithfully adapts source Introduction, 206–212: future openness is joined to responsibility, not guaranteed improvement. It makes the source's challenge apply back to the document's own possible sterile denunciation. Main §18, 642 and 675, preserves editorial distinctions and explicitly forbids treating old texts as observations of 2026. Chapter 3's 1974 addendum is dated at its attribution in main 242; Nietzsche's warning is explicitly nineteenth century at 238; no analogous present-tense source slippage remains in the reviewed passages.

The minimum completion is therefore the two local repairs above. Wider historical substantiation remains an acknowledged research burden, not a reason to interrupt this source cycle or weaken the constructive summons.
