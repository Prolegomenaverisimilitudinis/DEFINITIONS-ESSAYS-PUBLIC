## 1. §7.4 “A semantic construction can reopen a philosophical possibility” — the obstacle Tarski helped Popper overcome is misstated

**Actual defect:** The first paragraph says an exact account of truth helped Popper articulate a distinction he had previously found difficult: truth versus belief, acceptance or recognition. In the cited P5 passage, Popper says he already accepted objective correspondence truth, but found the relation between a statement and a fact difficult to explain clearly and consequently avoided the topic. Later in the same passage he explicitly describes the objective position as ancient. Tarski's contribution therefore cannot be adequately characterized as first making truth distinct from belief. The subsequent distinction is a consequence of the account, rather than the original difficulty Popper reports.

This matters to the essay's central constructive claim. As written, the example presents a familiar distinction and calls the underlying work exact, but does not explain what had to be constructed to make correspondence intellectually defensible. The last paragraph properly denies that one truth-condition sentence would amount to Tarski's technical construction, yet that caution also leaves the constructive mechanism out of view.

**Concrete minimal correction:** Replace the account of Popper's prior difficulty with his difficulty explaining correspondence between statements and facts. Add two or three sentences identifying the distinction between a language's assertions and the language in which their truth conditions are stated: the latter can name an assertion and state the condition under which it is true. Explain that controlling these relationships, including the danger of unrestricted self-reference, is substantive semantic work; a single instance only illustrates the relationship and does not supply the whole construction. Then retain truth versus acceptance as a philosophical consequence. P5 itself supplies the statement/fact difficulty, object-language/metalanguage distinction, and reference to the liar paradox, so this correction does not require a new source programme.

**Not a required change:** A technical tutorial on Tarski, or a claim that the complete construction is presented here.

## 2. §7.3 “A limitation of representation can become a task for new exactness” — show the successor, not only the impossibility

**Actual explanatory omission:** The unit-square example clearly shows what rational-number representation cannot do. Its constructive answer remains “Further mathematical means can accommodate the relation.” Consequently the reader sees an exact diagnosis but is asked to take the actual enlargement of the means on trust. This is a material gap because §2.1 explicitly promises that §7 makes the creative power concrete, and because the core argument concerns creation of further objects, relations and methods rather than willingness to continue after failure. The calculus paragraph repeats the need for development without filling that gap.

**Concrete minimal correction:** Expand the successor paragraph by identifying one exact enlargement: introduce a positive number whose square is two into an enlarged number domain, retain the familiar rational arithmetic, and give rules for expressions involving the new number. State that this makes the diagonal exactly expressible without turning it into a rational number or substituting a decimal approximation. This demonstrates new admitted objects and usable relations while preserving the original impossibility result. It can remain a conceptual illustration rather than a historical claim about the sequence in which mathematics developed.

**Not a required change:** A proof of irrationality, a construction of all real numbers, or a longer intellectual history. The existing diagnosis is mathematically sound; the missing element is its constructive counterpart.
