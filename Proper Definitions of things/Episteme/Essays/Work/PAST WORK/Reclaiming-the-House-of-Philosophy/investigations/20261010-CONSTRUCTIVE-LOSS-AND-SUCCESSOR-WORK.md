# Constructive loss and successor work

10 October 2026. Bounded investigation for the rewrite of §§2, 3, 7 and 10. This is a source finding and argument-development note, not replacement essay prose or an established history of philosophical decline.

Source: Karl R. Popper, *Conjectures and Refutations*, the supplied local Markdown reading copy. All line locators below refer to that copy. Tarski is encountered through Popper's philosophical exposition, not through a direct technical examination of Tarski's definitions and proofs.

## Consequential finding

The capacity at issue is **the creation of further means of intelligibility**: the ability to develop objects, distinctions, relations and methods through which a problem can become thinkable and answerable in ways the inherited means did not permit. This includes changing what a representation can express, separating questions it previously conflated, and making consequences available to examination. Criticism can disclose the inadequacy that calls for this work; it cannot supply the successor construction merely by declaring the inadequacy. Cooperation can support the work; it cannot replace the intellectual invention required.

The three cases below specify that capacity more deeply than the main document's example of distinguishing technical approval from permission. They concern the development of ways of thinking, not simply clearer administrative vocabulary or better transfer of already completed understanding. They also constrain a sweeping abandonment claim: the source itself records consequential constructive achievements. Any contemporary charge must identify where this capacity is now declined or underdeveloped, rather than treating its continued presence as an inconvenient exception.

## 1. Calculus: a deficient foundation can be a problem to develop

**Popper's account.** In chapter 2, §II, Popper uses the early differential and integral calculus against a general prohibition on philosophical formulations regarded as nonsense. Its early formulations were seriously problematic, yet sustained mathematical work made them more adequately founded. His point is especially strong because he says Berkeley's criticism was fundamentally right: acknowledging a real criticism does not require strangling the development it criticizes. He adds that the foundations continued to invite clarification at the time of writing. [Chapter 2, §II, lines 1448–1464](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1448>); the specific computability illustration is [note 10, line 1452](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1452>).

**What capacity changes.** The relevant achievement is developing a mathematical means of treating change and accumulation, together with the grounds and distinctions that make its operations defensible. Those mathematical descriptions of calculus are explanatory gloss here; Popper's cited passage establishes the trajectory from genuine foundational difficulty to constructive clarification, rather than presenting a technical derivation of derivatives or integrals. The case makes visible two losses: prohibition can prevent an emerging instrument from being adequately constituted; indiscriminate celebration can preserve operations while neglecting what makes them valid. A successor must be able both to invent and to improve foundations.

**Limits.** Popper does not show that every unclear proposal conceals a future calculus, that successful calculation excuses contradiction, or that all criticism should be postponed. His counterfactual about a Wittgenstein suppressing calculus is an argumentative illustration, not an independently documented historical event. The force is that a present defect does not, without further argument, establish a permanent impossibility of adequate construction. Nor should his dated discussion of Kreisel be converted into a technical claim that a computable bounded monotone sequence lacks a classical real limit: he specifically distinguishes the computability of terms from that of a limit.

## 2. The diagonal: an inherited means can fail to admit what must be understood

**Popper's account.** In chapter 2, §§VII–VIII, the diagonal of a unit square defeats an account under which every magnitude is represented by the ratio of two natural numbers. Popper carefully distinguishes failure of that arithmetic representation from geometrical incomparability: the diagonal has not become an unintelligible nonentity. The proof of irrationality uses the inherited arithmetic itself to expose its restriction. A method admitting incommensurable magnitudes can then preserve exact comparison and construction without insisting that every length have the excluded form. [Chapter 2, §VII, lines 1770–1774](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1770>), [the proof, lines 1866–1874](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1866>), and [§VIII, lines 1886–1900](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1886>).

**What capacity changes.** The intellectual invention is not a new label pasted onto the diagonal. It is a reconstruction of the means by which magnitudes are related and investigated, so that a legitimate subject need no longer be forced into an inadequate representational form. What the old method cannot express exactly becomes accessible to another exact treatment. This is a precise model of constructive loss: we can continue repeating correct operations within inherited forms while losing the ambition to develop forms adequate to the subjects that defeat them.

Popper goes further than the claim that specialists should coordinate. He identifies a philosophical achievement in consciously constructing an approach to knowledge of the world. He distinguishes acting within an inherited theoretical approach from reflecting upon, rejecting, constructing or explicitly formulating that approach. [Chapter 2, §VIII, lines 1926–1946](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1926>). His own argument therefore supplies an affirmative account of philosophical creativity, rather than only a permission to criticize.

**Limits.** The irrationality proof and the distinction between ratio representation and geometrical comparison are firmer elements of this passage than Popper's historical reconstruction of Plato's motives, Euclid's intended cosmological purpose, or the extent of their influence. Present the latter as Popper's account. Do not infer that constituting a geometric construction constitutes the physical world. A geometrical instrument can make a cosmological conjecture formulable without making it true. Popper's own surrounding account of Democritus also warns against triumphal reconstruction: he notes an invalid inference in the argument for the void. [Chapter 2, §VI, note 22, line 1722](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:1722>).

## 3. Tarski through Popper: articulating truth without guaranteeing recognition

**Popper's account.** In chapter 10, §§VII–VIII, Popper reports that he once avoided discussing correspondence truth because he lacked a satisfactory way of making the relation clear. His exposition of Tarski separates an assertion being discussed from the language in which its correspondence condition is stated. This makes the difference between an assertion and the condition under which it is true explicit. It also supports distinctions between truth, justified acceptance, conviction, consistency and usefulness. [Chapter 10, §VII, lines 4390–4416](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:4390>) and [§VIII, lines 4420–4456](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:4420>).

**What capacity changes.** The construction lets Popper articulate what his earlier approach had left difficult: an account can be true despite being unbelieved or unjustified, and accepted for good reasons while being false. That is an epistemic capacity, because one can now distinguish the success sought from the grounds or recognition currently available. Exact semantic work can remove an apparent obstacle to truth-directed inquiry. Merely announcing realism, praising rigor or coordinating disciplines would not supply that distinction's developed account.

**Limits.** Popper's intuitive exposition is not the technical construction of a truth definition, and individual correspondence biconditionals should not be presented as that entire construction. He makes expansive claims about applicability to natural languages, qualified by consistency and precautions against inconsistency; report those as his claims, not as a technical result independently established here. The safe essay use is the philosophical achievement he describes and its expressly limited consequence. In particular, stating a truth condition does not establish that the condition obtains, and Popper explicitly denies a general criterion by which empirical truth can be recognized. [Chapter 10, §VIII, lines 4430–4456](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:4430>). Do not attach Popper's subsequent formal theory of verisimilitude to this case as an automatically vindicated measure of nearness to truth.

## Distinctions the rewrite should carry through

**Constituting an intellectual object or instrument** establishes an account's definitions, permitted relations, operations and consequences. A new choice of formal means changes what can be expressed or derived. Its creator cannot simply choose the consequences independently of that constitution, and its adequacy to the motivating problem remains open to assessment.

**Interpreting an empirical subject** proposes how those intellectual means concern something in the world. Mathematical permission to construct an object does not establish a corresponding physical object, causal relation or process. Conversely, a failure of one representation does not abolish the subject it failed to represent.

**Establishing or assessing truth** concerns whether the claim is right about its subject and what grounds bear upon that judgment. A truth definition is not evidence for every instance; a formal deduction is not empirical confirmation of its premises. This is why creative reason can enlarge our means while reality remains independent of our preferences.

The cases support a demanding account of successor work: undertake the problem exposed by an inherited limitation and create the means needed to address it. They do not establish that present philosophy universally refuses that work, or that exactness alone constitutes the entire philosophical vocation. Popper also cautions against formal elaboration detached from the problem and its criticism: deductive systems make otherwise unobvious consequences available for testing, but their elaboration is not an end that independently settles empirical adequacy. [Chapter 10, §V, lines 4346–4364](<C:/Users/Graphics/Desktop/General Downloads/Conjectures and Refutations - Karl R. Popper.md:4346>).

For §§2 and 10, the central clarification is thus a capability: **developing the means in which successors can pose and pursue further questions**. For §3, the boundary is the distinction between constituting those means and commanding the truth of what they say about reality. For §7, the diagonal supplies a substantial illustration of semantic and ontological underdevelopment: inherited exactness may be inadequate because its permitted forms exclude a relation the subject requires. The historical accusation becomes meaningful only where refusal or abdication of such work is independently shown; the cases establish what would be lost, not the universal fact of its loss.
