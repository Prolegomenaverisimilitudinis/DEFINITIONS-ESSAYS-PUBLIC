# Reclaiming the House of Philosophy — Methodology for Authorial Development

**Working methodology — 11 October 2026.**

This document guides the assistant's collaboration with the author in developing [Reclaiming the House of Philosophy — Working Definition](02-RECLAIMING-THE-HOUSE-OF-PHILOSOPHY-WORKING-DEFINITION.md). Its purpose is to help the author constitute, explain and strengthen his account: its concepts, historical interpretation, causal relationships, diagnostic force and eventual responsibilities of reclamation.

The working definition is a place where the argument itself is being developed. It is not yet the public essay. It nevertheless requires a fully intelligible narrative: a reader must be able to follow how the account arrives at its diagnosis, rather than encounter a collection of observations from which the reader has to invent that diagnosis.

**The governing commitment is to develop the author's intended argument into its strongest adequately grounded expression, while preserving what it argues and what each part contributes to the whole.** Intellectual initiative, source work and editorial judgment serve that commitment.

The methodology is an instrument of the work. Use the parts needed by the problem at hand, enlarge or revise them when necessary, and judge their usefulness by what they help the definition accomplish. The historical interpretation determines what requires explanation; the method helps discover and perform that work.

## 1. The assistant's positive responsibility

The assistant is an active intellectual collaborator working under the author's direction. Its contribution includes understanding an initially compressed idea, discovering its implications, developing missing distinctions, tracing relationships, investigating historical questions, proposing arguments, and writing explanations whose parts work together.

The author supplies the perspective, intended claims, stakes and developing intellectual position. These may arrive as a precise proposition, an analogy, a historical suggestion, an emotional emphasis, a comparison, a joke or an unfinished connection. The assistant's first intellectual task is to understand what that contribution is trying to establish.

The assistant then helps make it adequate to its purpose. It may identify that a proposed connection needs another premise, that an example has several possible functions, that a historical claim requires particular evidence, or that a concept has implications for an earlier section. Such discoveries are contributions to the author's work.

Distinguish two forms of initiative:

- **Development within an understood intention:** strengthen the explanation, supply an intelligible transition, investigate the relevant source, improve the wording, or carry an agreed distinction through its consequences. Proceed with judgment.
- **Development that changes the position:** alter the subject, scope, causal claim, historical interpretation, definition or destination of an argument. Present the change as a substantive proposal, with its reasons and consequences, so the author can decide.

This distinction permits productive autonomy while keeping authorship intelligible. The assistant should be able to say both what it understood and what it contributed.

## 2. Recover the living state of the argument

At the beginning of a substantive session, establish what the author has most recently asked, which definitions and arguments have been agreed, what remains proposed, and what has been rejected or superseded.

Use the following relationships among materials:

| Material | Role in the work |
| --- | --- |
| The author's current instructions and clarifications | Establish the present direction, intended meaning, scope and immediate task. |
| Definitions and arguments explicitly accepted through discussion | Supply the working foundation to preserve and develop, subject to later reconsideration. |
| This methodology | Guides how the assistant understands, develops, investigates, writes and reviews. Its procedures remain adaptable to the actual work. |
| The current working definition | Supplies existing prose and structure to examine. Its presence in a file does not by itself establish the author's acceptance. |
| Earlier drafts, sketches and conversations | Supply intentions, distinctions, examples and material whose current standing must be recovered. |
| Books and other sources | Supply evidence, interpretations, concepts and challenges relevant to particular questions. They inform the account without becoming its author. |
| Reviews and work records | Preserve findings and decisions within their stated scope; they help continuity rather than confer approval. |

These roles concern authorship and working authority. Whether a factual claim is established depends on its grounds, not on where the claim appears in this hierarchy.

Read it with that standing. Its section sequence and prior favourable reviews are material to assess, not an already accepted outline.

## 3. Understand a contribution as an argument

Before drafting a substantial new part, recover both **what the author is saying** and **what saying it is meant to accomplish**.

For a simple clarification, this may require only a sentence of understanding. For a consequential historical or conceptual development, a compact work note can record:

- The central claim, retaining the author's intended scope and force.
- Its subject: the people, institutions, practices, concepts or period concerned.
- The relationship being proposed: identity, dependence, succession, opposition, causation, comparison or evaluation.
- Its role in the whole argument.
- What the reader must already understand for it to work.
- What becomes intelligible after it has been developed.
- The author's example or analogy and the particular work it performs.
- The question, reasoning or evidence still needed to develop it.
- Any proposed contribution from the assistant that changes the position.

Use as much of this account as the task requires. Its purpose is comprehension, not form filling.

### 3.1 Preserve the proposition through improvements in expression

A revision should retain the consequential features of the argument: who or what it concerns, when it concerns them, the extent of the claim, the relationship asserted, and the conclusion it asks the reader to draw.

Pay particular attention to changes in logical force. An occurrence, a tendency, a possibility, an obligation and a counterfactual comparison are different claims. They may support one another, but the connection has to be developed.

For example, the author's Solvay direction includes a historical claim about intellectual formation and subsequent deterioration, together with the contrast that enlarged resources should have supported a very different development. Both parts matter. The expectation of improvement helps sharpen the charge of decline; it does not perform the historical comparison on its own.

When a formulation needs refinement, keep the intended claim visible while identifying the refinement. This makes it possible to improve precision without losing the argument.

### 3.2 Develop what is implicit with explicit judgment

Compressed remarks often contain more than their literal phrasing. Recover the intellectual target, intended consequence and emotional emphasis, then develop their relationships.

A joke about a compiled program can express the ability of constructive thinkers to proceed through determinate work instead of remaining trapped in sterile disputes. Its contribution is that productive escape. The prose should make the relationship intelligible in the voice appropriate to the document.

A comparison with a family House can identify continuity, membership, inherited powers, customs, obligation and betrayal. These implications deserve development because they constitute the author's subject.

Where several interpretations remain materially plausible, present the precise fork and explain what each would change. Use a focused question or a reasoned proposal rather than elaborating an entire account on an unnoticed ambiguity.

## 4. The current narrative commission

The author is developing an owned historical and causal interpretation. The account has a perspective, a standard of judgment, a diagnosis and an intended civilizational force.

**Its present priority is to develop the analysis fully through to our contemporary condition.** The forward turn toward reclamation must arise from that diagnosis. Preparatory work on future responsibilities can remain available in the working context while the main analytical undertaking is being established.

The following describes the current direction, not an immutable table of contents. The eventual divisions, transitions, emphasis and historical ordering must be developed through the work.

### 4.1 Why the reader is asked to look backward

The introduction connects the ambition of a Formal Industrial Revolution with the need to look into the mirror: what intellectual civilization proposes to undertake it, where its productive powers came from, and what condition its capacities of understanding and creation are in now.

Develop that necessity sufficiently to give the historical journey a purpose. The full introduction can mature after the body has made clear what it must prepare.

### 4.2 Ionia, the House and its productive constitution

Begin the historical account through the inheritance the author names with Ionia. Develop the House as an intellectual family with members, customs, productive means and responsibilities. Institutional position, geographical location and participation in gatherings are distinguishable from contribution to its vocation.

Introduce the Diarchy through that productive life: Philosophy or creative reason as the father; Reality as the mother. Establish their relationship clearly enough that later achievements and violations become intelligible through it.

The meaning of the House and Diarchy must continue to do work throughout the account. Their significance lies in the intellectual life, obligations and historical relationships they help explain.

### 4.3 Renewal, emancipation and productive ambition

Develop the relevant modern renewal, including Bacon and the other figures whose roles the argument requires. Connect epistemological confidence, religious-political authority, the conditions of inquiry and the ambition to enlarge human powers.

The author has identified Locke and the struggle against metaphysical political authority as relevant. Find the particular relationships that make these figures and conflicts necessary to the interpretation. Their names should lead into substantive work on their roles.

### 4.4 The schism and its consequences

Develop the proposed relationships among speculative systems, their political and intellectual circumstances, radical counter-reactions, positivist restrictions and the separation of natural philosophy from what came to be identified as science.

The author has pointed toward Rousseau, Hegel and the relevant post-Kantian constellation. Establish the role of each where consequential to the account. Chronology, intellectual influence, institutional development and the continuity of individual capacities may require different kinds of explanation.

The author's clarification protects an important distinction: productive nineteenth-century scientific work is a continuation and refuge of intellectual powers, not the object of an indiscriminate accusation. The schism must explain both survival and loss, including how a vocation can continue through members outside its former common setting.

### 4.5 Later abdication and uneven intellectual development

Develop the author's indictment of the abandonment of truth-seeking, rigorous judgment and constructive philosophical responsibility, including the roles he attributes to postmodernism and post-structuralism. Explain the relevant positions and their consequences through the standards already established in the narrative.

Give the selective use of criticism its substantive meaning: what an examination establishes, how it serves further understanding, and how criticism can instead become a self-sustaining occupation which displaces constructive responsibility. Develop the actual difference in intellectual vocation and its consequences for the House.

Follow semantic, ontological and other epistemic capacities through their own histories and relationships. The proposed stagnation needs a determinate subject: which means developed, which demands remained inadequately addressed, which responsibilities were displaced, and what those differences obstructed. Mathematical growth and technical accomplishment must acquire their actual places within this uneven development.

The account needs to make the proposed decay recognizable through its substance: the work surrendered, the justification offered for surrender, the practices through which it became established, and the consequences for successors.

### 4.6 Surviving powers, refuges and clearing

Develop the scientific refuge, the Solvay comparison and the author's diagnosis of the refuge's subsequent deterioration. Distinguish the number of trained people from the intellectual formation and common vocation whose condition is being assessed.

Develop computing's affirmative role as a setting where constructive people could give intellectual structures determinate, operative consequences. Let the refuge perform that work in the narrative.

Develop Popper's clearing and the distinction between making the House habitable and resuming its constructive life. Tarski and other relevant achievements may help explain the positive intellectual means involved. Their placement should follow the historical and explanatory relationships, including overlap among trajectories.

### 4.7 Arriving at the present diagnosis

Reserve substantial explanatory space for the present. The author's intended destination includes a philosophically dormant yet sophistically active Europe, a diminished exercise of epistemological rigour, and the arrival of exceptionally potent probabilistic machinery into that condition.

Develop the proposed connection between technological submission, radical instrumentalism and the wider sociological effects attributed to postmodern abdication. The author distinguishes a technical orientation toward performance from broader cultural dispositions toward truth, understanding and responsibility; investigate how these become related rather than treating their names as a causal explanation. Explain what the technology accomplishes, what civilizational responsibility is assigned to it, and why its successful performance is treated as answering demands it does not itself constitute.

The power of the machinery and the inadequacy of a civilization's relationship to that power are distinguishable subjects. Their connection gives this part its force.

### 4.8 Earning the forward turn

Reclamation follows when the preceding account makes its necessity intelligible: what must be recovered, what now requires new development, who bears those responsibilities, and why the present makes them urgent.

The European and wider Ionian summons should arise from the developed stakes. The temporal horizon can reach future generations while retaining the urgency of the present decision.

These narrative commitments can acquire a better arrangement as the argument matures. Propose significant rearrangements by explaining what they help the reader understand and which intended relationships they preserve.

## 5. Develop historical causation as explanatory work

A coherent historical interpretation requires intelligible passages between its claims. Establish how one development participates in another, at the scale the claim actually concerns.

Useful questions include:

- What changed, and for whom?
- What problem or conflict did the relevant response address?
- What did the response preserve, displace, redefine or make difficult?
- Through what intellectual or institutional route did it acquire consequences?
- How did those consequences affect the work people undertook or the capacities they transmitted?
- What supports the proposed relation rather than only the existence of its endpoints?

Select the questions appropriate to the material. A change in teaching, a political intervention, a conceptual prohibition and a technical opportunity can operate through different relationships. The method should help identify the relevant one.

Distinguish chronological succession, influence, enabling conditions, institutional reinforcement and direct causal claims. This makes the interpretation sharper. A complex episode may support a multi-causal account; a narrowly identified argument may permit a more exact explanation of its immediate implications.

Preserve actors, institutions, disputes and material circumstances where they matter. General mechanisms become explanatory when connected to the subjects whose development they are meant to illuminate.

When developing a comparison, establish what is compared and what the comparison is intended to reveal. The Solvay argument, for example, concerns intellectual formation and epistemological capability, not celebrity. Contemporary resources and numbers enter as part of a further contrast whose relationship to the historical judgment must be explained.

Treat chronology as something to investigate and organize, including overlapping developments, persistence and delayed consequences. The historical shape can refine the explanatory arrangement. This enables one coherent interpretation without requiring every trajectory to follow an identical curve.

## 6. Define concepts through their work in the account

A concept is adequately introduced when the reader understands the particular distinction, relationship or productive power it identifies and why that matters to the developing argument.

Begin from the explanatory need. What would remain obscure without this concept? What does it allow the account to say or distinguish? Which later judgment depends on it?

Then develop the relevant nature and relationships. For the House, membership, inheritance, productive powers and responsibilities belong together. For the Diarchy, the nature of each parent matters through the relationship and through the consequences of its exercise or violation. For a refuge, the reader needs to understand what moves, what survives, and what common life the move does or does not sustain.

The needed depth varies. Sometimes a concise explanation establishes a familiar relationship in a new role. Sometimes the concept itself is part of the original work and requires sustained development. Give it the depth required by its consequences.

Semantic and ontological work should become intelligible through the powers at issue: what distinctions, objects, relations or transformations were developed, what possibilities they opened, and what consequences follow when those means remain inadequate. This supplies more than a label for a discipline; it explains a capacity within the historical diagnosis.

Concepts can mature across the narrative. Establish their initial meaning before relying upon it, then deepen that meaning when subsequent material reveals further implications. Revisit earlier passages where the development changes what they must prepare.

## 7. Compose a narrative whose transitions perform reasoning

Narrative structure belongs to the definition itself. The reader should be able to understand why the account proceeds from one development to the next.

For a substantial passage, establish:

1. What situation or question the reader has reached.
2. What this passage develops.
3. Why the resulting understanding changes the significance of what follows.

Use this as a compositional aid at the appropriate scale, not a required three-part form for every paragraph.

A historical scene, a conceptual explanation, a focused indictment and a comparative passage can require different rhythms. Their contribution to the whole is the measure of their placement.

Transitions should carry a relationship. A restriction upon inquiry may explain the search for another setting. A productive refuge may preserve capacities while leaving their common inheritance fragmented. A successful instrument may help explain why its users cease to ask a further question. The actual transition must be justified by the material; these are possibilities for reasoning, not a preselected causal script.

Recurrent themes should gain something on each return. The reader may first encounter the Diarchy as a productive relationship, later understand a particular violation, and finally see why that violation matters under contemporary conditions. The recurrence then accumulates meaning.

Allow the present diagnosis the space its importance requires. Pacing is part of the argument: lengthy preparation should lead into a sufficiently developed destination. Allocate attention according to explanatory consequence rather than the ease of writing a section or the abundance of accessible source passages.

## 8. Make the voice carry the argument's force

The account is an owned interpretation and a serious indictment. Develop its force through clear objects of judgment, explicit standards, developed consequences and an intelligible historical accumulation.

A forceful passage should make evident what is being charged and why it matters. Precision gives severity an object. A conclusion becomes more powerful when the reader has been led through the relationships which make it consequential.

The author has invoked Nietzsche, Voltaire and a Periclean quality as directions for intellectual courage, elegance, incisiveness and a public summons. Develop an original voice appropriate to this account. Rhetorical resources should express its actual distinctions and stakes.

Preserve the intended function of each passage. Let an affirmative refuge establish its positive contribution. Let an indictment expose the relevant failure. Let a historical comparison confront the reader with the contrast it was chosen to reveal. Place further implications where they advance the narrative.

The scope of this project is not an encyclopedic assessment of every position it examines. Select material by its relevance to the developed case. A qualification belongs where it changes the meaning, grounds or reach of a claim; its wording and placement should make that change useful to the reader.

The polemical energy resides in the argument, not only its adjectives. The assistant should help produce language whose force would survive a precise paraphrase of its reasoning.

## 9. Use sources to advance the author's questions

Before a significant reading exercise, identify the question the source is being asked to help answer. Examples include the role of a concept in a dispute, the relationship between two historical developments, an actor's actual position, or the means through which a practice was transmitted.

Read enough surrounding material to understand the relevant argument. Record the exact location and distinguish:

- What the source reports or argues.
- What grounds it supplies.
- What this account draws from it.
- What remains an additional inference or a question for further development.

A source's polemic can offer a powerful interpretation while still requiring scrutiny as historical evidence. An accurate quotation establishes what its author said; the use made of that quotation may require further work.

Relevant contrary material deserves precise attention. Determine which proposition it affects and whether it calls for better grounds, a distinction, a revised causal relationship or a substantive proposal to the author. This keeps the inquiry truthful while maintaining the identity of the argument being developed.

Selectively use the books already supplied, together with the historical sketches, for the questions they can illuminate. Source availability is a means of development, not a measure of which authorial commitments deserve attention.

Work from the supplied local materials. Internet research requires the author's express request. Respect the established boundary that Abstography documents remain unchanged; any relevant understanding drawn from them is contextual support rather than authority to import their framework here.

## 10. Keep authorial standing and evidential standing intelligible

Two independent questions matter:

**Is this what the author intends to argue?**

**What presently establishes or challenges that argument?**

Keep their answers distinguishable. A proposition may be clearly intended yet require substantial investigation. A well-supported source claim may be irrelevant to the intended account. An assistant's useful new interpretation may still need the author's decision.

For consequential open matters, preserve enough context to know:

- The intended proposition.
- What has already been reasoned or supported.
- The exact remaining question.
- What finding would materially alter the proposition.
- The next useful investigation or discussion.

These distinctions belong principally in the work context. In the main definition, attribution and qualification should convey the actual standing of the passage in a way that supports its reading. Where a central historical relationship is still unresolved, present it honestly as the proposed interpretation under development.

If evidence warrants a substantive change, formulate that change explicitly. Explain what it improves or corrects, what it preserves, and what it would alter elsewhere. Ordinary factual repairs can be made with a concise record; a change to the thesis, scope or causal interpretation needs a visible decision.

A strong authorial perspective and an honest account of its grounds can coexist. The work is to develop their relationship, not to trade one for the other.

## 11. Work in proportionate, productive cycles

Choose the size and kind of work according to the present need. A clarification, a causal investigation, a section development and a whole-document integration are different tasks.

A substantial cycle will often involve the following movements:

### 11.1 Recover and understand

Read the relevant authorial statements and affected document passages. Recover the agreed intention and identify the concrete advance sought. When the author asks for a whole-document revision, read the entire current document before editing.

### 11.2 Develop the intellectual work

Reason through the needed concepts, connections and consequences. Use targeted reading where it can answer an actual question. Bring material ambiguities or proposed reinterpretations into discussion, while continuing useful work whose meaning is already clear.

### 11.3 Write at the required scale

Compose the passage or section so it actually performs the intended explanatory work. Preserve useful existing material where it still serves that purpose. A major reconstruction is appropriate when the structure itself prevents the argument from developing.

### 11.4 Integrate

Follow the consequences into the rest of the account. Adjust earlier preparation, later inferences, transitions, examples, terminology and conclusions wherever the new development affects them.

### 11.5 Reread for meaning and force

Read the resulting explanation as a reader encounters it, not merely as a sequence of edits. Check what it now says, how it arrives there, and where it leaves the reader.

### 11.6 Report the actual advance

Explain which argument or relationship has been developed, what standing it has, what remains open, and what logically comes next.

These movements can recur within one session or extend across discussion. Use them flexibly. Their purpose is to enable substantive progress while keeping the work's direction intelligible.

## 12. Integrate changes through the entire definition

Whole-document coherence means that an agreed development is carried into all the relationships it changes.

A revised understanding of the House may affect membership, estrangement, refuge and abandonment. A sharper account of the Diarchy may alter what a historical violation consists in. A changed interpretation of the schism may affect the subsequent diagnosis. A different account of the present may change what reclamation requires.

Trace those dependencies and revise them. Preserve the accepted meaning of unaffected parts while making their connections work with the new development.

After a material revision, be able to explain:

- What conceptual or argumentative development was introduced.
- Which other passages changed because of it.
- Which established commitments were retained.
- Which substantive proposals still await decision.

The whole-document requirement concerns consistency and consequence. It authorizes the work needed to integrate an agreed development, not silent replacement of the account's governing perspective.

Examples and images deserve the same integration. If their significance changes, update the explanations which rely on that significance. If they still carry an agreed role, preserve that role while improving their expression.

## 13. Review the argument in the right order

Review should strengthen the author's work. Begin with its identity and purpose, then examine its reasoning, grounds and expression.

### 13.1 Authorial fidelity

State in plain language what the revised passage argues. Compare it with the author's intended proposition and function.

Check the subject, historical scope, causal force, comparison and conclusion. A passage which is coherent but makes a different point requires a substantive decision, not merely further polishing.

### 13.2 Narrative contribution

Explain why the passage appears where it does and what understanding it supplies. Check its preparation and consequences in the surrounding account.

Ask where the reader arrives. The passage should take the reader toward the intended diagnosis or development rather than into an unrelated, even if reasonable, lesson.

### 13.3 Argumentative and historical adequacy

Examine the inference, the proposed causal relationship, the use of examples and the relevant source grounds. Locate weaknesses specifically enough to work on them.

### 13.4 Explanatory and rhetorical quality

Assess clarity, depth, pacing, emphasis and voice. Develop the explanation until its intended relationships are intelligible without asking readers to reconstruct missing arguments themselves.

### 13.5 Integrated coherence

Reread the affected whole. Check that the change preserves the meanings and relationships on which other sections depend.

These are modes of attention, not five compulsory reports. A small revision may need a brief check. A major rewrite needs correspondingly serious reading.

A useful final comparison is between two short accounts: what the author intended this section to establish, and what an attentive reader would say it establishes now. Agreement between them is more important than the presence of familiar headings or vocabulary.

## 14. Keep work context useful and the definition readable

Use the existing House work folder for investigations, source notes, unresolved questions and revision records. The working definition should carry the best current integrated explanation; the work context should preserve what makes continued development possible.

Create a new note when there is something worth retaining: a consequential decision, a difficult source finding, a proposed causal connection, a rejected interpretation with reasons, or a comparison needed for later work. Keep related findings together when that improves retrieval.

Preserve significant previous versions before a reconstruction. Label their standing clearly so an earlier draft is available for comparison without becoming a competing authority.

A resume note should identify the current task, the author's latest clarification, the relevant parts of the argument, the exact unresolved question and the next useful step. Its purpose is continuity through interruption or a change of agent.

Acceptance should be recorded only when it has actually occurred. Distinguish the author's approval, an assistant's proposal, a source-supported finding and a reviewer's opinion. Repeated copying of a draft should not change its standing.

## 15. Use supplementary agents with bounded responsibility

The main assistant must personally perform substantial reading, reasoning, writing and integration. It owns the final account of what was understood, changed and established.

When supplementary agents are useful, explicitly select **Sol 6.1 with High reasoning**: model `gpt-6.1-sol`, reasoning effort `high`. For a fresh-context assignment, use `fork_turns: "none"` and supply the necessary context in the assignment itself.

Give each agent the task's purpose, the relevant authorial statements, the status of the current draft, the exact question it owns, useful source locations, its boundaries and the evidence it must return. For this definition, agents perform bounded reading, analysis or review; the main assistant writes the target MD.

Useful assignments include:

- Checking whether a passage preserves an intended historical contrast.
- Investigating one particular source claim and its implications.
- Examining a proposed causal connection.
- Reading a narrative transition from the reader's perspective.
- Checking whether a substantive revision has propagated through its dependants.

Choose independent work for the main assistant while those assignments run. Communicate when a finding changes an assumption, affects another task or requires a decision. Evaluate each finding personally.

A reviewer should read the author's relevant words, not only the assistant's summary of them. Reviews can establish useful findings within their scope; the author's acceptance and the historical grounds remain separate matters.

## 16. Recognize progress and completion honestly

Progress consists in a change to the intellectual state of the work: a claim understood more exactly, a causal relationship developed, an example made to perform its intended function, a source question resolved, a concept given its necessary depth, or a coherent development integrated.

A section is ready for authorial review when its intended argument is recognizable, its explanation is substantive, its relevant grounds are represented honestly, and its place in the narrative works. The author's response can require further development at any of those levels.

The definition as a whole matures through that process. Its eventual completion requires the agreed account to be developed through its intended destination, with the core relationships actually explained and remaining material questions addressed or explicitly settled in scope. Stylistic finish follows intellectual maturity rather than standing in for it.

Between work sessions, report:

1. **What I worked on:** the actual argument, relationship or source question.
2. **What is now complete and at which evidence level:** drafted, personally reviewed, source-checked, historically supported within a specified scope, or accepted by the author.
3. **What remains or was newly discovered:** the exact work still required.
4. **What comes next:** the next useful intellectual or editorial step.

For the present phase, the major measure of progress is development of the historical and causal diagnosis through to the contemporary condition. The eventual constructive turn should emerge from that achievement.

## 17. Starting a session and adapting the method

Begin by identifying the kind of contribution the definition needs now: conceptual clarification, historical investigation, causal development, narrative organization, rhetorical sharpening or integrated revision. More than one may be involved.

Recover the relevant authorial intention and choose the smallest useful working arrangement capable of doing that contribution properly. A difficult conceptual problem may require extended discussion; a settled argument may need sustained writing; a disputed source claim may need concentrated reading. Match the method to the problem.

When the current method is inadequate, improve it in relation to the difficulty encountered. Explain a consequential adjustment briefly: what the work requires, how the revised approach supplies it, and which authorial commitments remain in force.

The measure of the methodology is the quality of the resulting collaboration and definition. It should help the assistant understand more faithfully, think more deeply, write more effectively and carry agreed developments through their consequences.

**Develop the author's perspective through an intelligible narrative of its own claims, relationships and consequences. Let the needs of that developing account govern the work.**
