# Reclaiming the House of Philosophy — Working Definition

**Status:** Initial working definition — 8 October 2026.

**Purpose:** To define the philosophical undertaking named by this expression: its objects, responsibilities, constructive and critical work, and relationships to knowledge and productive capability. This is a conceptual foundation for an eventual essay, not the essay itself. It develops a proposed orientation; it does not claim to settle the nature of all philosophy or establish a historical account of its decline.

The discarded sketches supplied questions and motivations, not governing definitions. The following account is an initial proposal for development with the author. Philosophical doctrines, institutional arrangements and historical judgments require their own decisions and grounds.

## 1. The central subject

**“Reclaiming the House of Philosophy” names the deliberate assumption of responsibility for developing and criticizing the means through which objects become intelligible, knowledge becomes possible, purposes become examinable, and new forms of inquiry and construction can be constituted.**

The subject is the work involved in forming those means and making their relationships explicit. Concepts, accounts of identity and meaning, forms of inference, models of knowledge, and distinctions between possible and actual structures affect what can be understood and undertaken. Their adequacy is itself a subject of inquiry and construction.

This orientation brings two responsibilities together. Philosophy can examine the commitments already organizing a field: what it assumes, what those assumptions permit, and whether its claims exceed their grounds. It can also develop concepts, relationships and instruments through which an inadequately understood subject becomes available for better work. Criticism and construction continually inform one another.

The intended scope reaches beyond immediate industrial applications. Inquiry can be warranted by a fundamental question before its uses are known. A productive difficulty can also expose a fundamental question that an established vocabulary conceals. The proposed relationship permits movement in both directions.

## 2. What the “house” and its reclamation mean

The *house* names a common intellectual undertaking in which the means of understanding, knowing, judging and constructing can themselves be questioned and developed. It gives those responsibilities a connected subject without requiring that they become the territory of one profession, institution or school.

To *reclaim* it is to accept responsibility for performing that work explicitly. It means making consequential assumptions available for examination, developing missing distinctions and relationships, and allowing criticism to reach the principles through which an undertaking proceeds. It also means sustaining the connection between foundational questions and the activities those foundations enable.

The metaphor does not settle a history. An eventual argument about which responsibilities were neglected, by whom, or during which periods would need independent historical development. The present definition can stand as a positive undertaking without presupposing those conclusions.

Its unity concerns questions and responsibilities, rather than the ownership of every contribution. Philosophical work can occur within mathematics, engineering, scientific inquiry, design or other practices when their participants examine and develop the foundations of their objects and activities. Recognizing that work need not reclassify their entire disciplines as philosophy.

## 3. The means of thought as objects of development

An instrument of thought is a means through which a subject can be distinguished, related, investigated or transformed. A concept can make a difference intelligible; a representation can preserve it; an inference rule can establish what follows from it; a method can organize its examination. These contributions are connected but not interchangeable.

The principal dimensions presently relevant are:

| Dimension | What it concerns | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual constitution | The distinctions through which a subject and its questions become intelligible. | What cannot be distinguished may be silently conflated in all subsequent work. |
| Ontology | What kinds of objects and relationships an account admits, including their identity, dependence and change. | A field needs to know what its claims and transformations are about. |
| Semantics | What expressions and structures mean, and what correspondence between representations preserves. | A transfer of symbols need not preserve the subject or significance of a claim. |
| Epistemology | How claims are formed, criticized and warranted; their scope, uncertainty and revisability. | The existence of an answer is different from grounds for relying upon it. |
| Logical and mathematical instruments | Exact forms of relation, inference, construction and demonstration. | Precision can expose consequences and incompatibilities that informal accounts leave unresolved. |
| Purposes and evaluation | What an undertaking seeks, why its criteria matter, and how different goods or requirements relate. | The capacity to achieve an outcome does not by itself establish that it is the right outcome to pursue. |

These are dimensions of the proposed work, not an exhaustive classification of philosophy. Their overlap is substantive. An account of identity can change what evidence is relevant; a semantic distinction can reveal that an apparent disagreement concerns different subjects; a purpose can expose the inadequacy of an otherwise useful representation.

## 4. Epistemic construction and technē

*Epistemic construction*, in this working account, means deliberately developing structures and instruments through which something can be understood, investigated or known more adequately. Its products can include a distinction, a connected conceptual account, an ontology, a formal language, a method of comparison, or a relationship among previously disconnected accounts.

Construction does not make an empirical proposition true by definition. We can design a concept of a material defect; whether a particular component has that defect remains a further question. We can define a mathematical structure and establish consequences under its premises; whether it represents a physical situation faithfully requires an appropriate correspondence. The work must preserve those different responsibilities.

*Epistemic technē* names such knowledge and organization considered as developed means of inquiry or constructive work. A concept becomes an instrument when it enables a distinction to be made and used reliably; a formal account becomes an instrument when its relationships support an identifiable class of reasoning or construction. Its use can then reveal further questions about its reach and adequacy.

This does not require each philosophical contribution to become software or a commercial product. An explanation can improve inquiry; a distinction can prevent a persistent mistake; a criticism can show that an intended objective is incoherent. Their value depends on what they establish or enable, not on one obligatory form of embodiment.

## 5. The constructive and critical relationship

Constructive work asks what account or instrument would make a subject adequately accessible. Critical work asks where that account fails, what it leaves unresolved, and whether its apparent achievements support its claims. A mature relationship between them preserves what survives criticism while allowing foundational revision when needed.

Consider a domain using one label, “approved,” for both a technical conclusion and permission to act. A result may satisfy a technical requirement while still lacking authorization for use. The problem is not merely an ambiguous word: different relationships have been compressed into one status. Separating them can require new objects, different evidence and distinct consequences in the surrounding account.

That illustration shows several levels of work. Conceptual analysis identifies the conflation. Ontological and semantic work define the separate relations. Epistemological work distinguishes their grounds. Formal or technical construction makes those distinctions usable in a particular system. Further criticism asks whether the new account fits the cases it claims to explain.

A useful instrument can remain partly inadequate. Success at one task can justify continued use within that scope while leaving its explanation, generality or application elsewhere unsettled. Conversely, exposing one limit need not defeat everything the instrument accomplishes. The relevant judgment concerns the specific relationship between claim, scope and grounds.

## 6. Reason, reality and the kinds of claim

The proposed orientation requires reasoned articulation and susceptibility to correction. A claim must be understandable enough for its consequences, premises and possible failures to be examined. Confidence attaches to what those grounds support, rather than to the prestige of a person, discipline or instrument.

Different subjects require different forms of answerability:

- A formal derivation must follow from its stated premises and rules.
- A claim about an empirical object must answer to relevant evidence and to the adequacy of the model connecting that evidence with the object.
- A designed structure must meet its constitutive requirements; a claim that it can be realized requires further support concerning its realizing means.
- A normative proposal must make its reasons, values and commitments available for examination; technical feasibility alone cannot establish them.

These forms can interact in one undertaking. A formally valid model can have a mistaken empirical application. A successful implementation can realize a specification that omitted the relevant distinction. A worthwhile purpose can depend on means that are not yet available. Keeping these relations explicit makes criticism more discriminating.

This is an orientation toward warranted, revisable understanding. It does not announce a finished epistemology or select a complete philosophical school. In particular, treating the absence of a current instrument as an open constructive problem is different from assuming that every imagined problem must have a solution.

## 7. Relations with specialized inquiry and construction

Philosophy contributes to the formation and examination of subjects, meanings, purposes and grounds. Mathematics develops exact structures and establishes their consequences. Empirical inquiry investigates aspects of the world through appropriate evidence. Design constitutes intended arrangements; engineering develops means that must function under specified conditions.

These descriptions identify contributions, not impermeable departmental boundaries. Mathematics can create new concepts; science can transform ontology; engineering can expose an invalid assumption; philosophy can require substantive mathematical or empirical work to advance. No discipline supplies every relevant answer merely by naming the others as subordinate.

Integration requires explicit relationships between contributions. What does a formal object represent? Which empirical premise does an argument depend upon? What must an implementation preserve? Which design choice expresses a purpose rather than a discovered fact? Such questions often arise precisely where specialized results meet.

The constructive role proposed here is therefore neither an external commentary upon completed disciplines nor a claim to replace their expertise. It concerns participating in the development of adequate foundations and connections, with the depth of knowledge that the actual question requires.

## 8. The orientation toward operability

*Operability* concerns whether an account has adequate means for the work it claims to enable. A procedure, model or conceptual instrument can be explained without those means yet existing. Establishing them is a distinguishable achievement.

Where a programme undertakes to create a productive capability, its philosophical and formal work must remain connected to that responsibility. A refined account may need further development, but refinement alone cannot indefinitely substitute for realizing the capability it promises. The relevant question is whether a conceptual improvement resolves a consequential limitation or merely extends an account beyond the work presently owed.

The relationship is reciprocal. An attempted realization can expose a missing concept, an unjustified identity assumption or a false boundary between activities. Such a discovery may require returning to foundational work, rather than compensating with an implementation convention whose meaning remains unexplained.

This orientation does not make immediate usefulness the measure of truth or require every fundamental inquiry to begin with a known application. It makes claimed constructive responsibilities explicit. A contribution can establish a valuable understanding before practical realization; an undertaking promising realization has an additional obligation.

## 9. Accessibility and cumulative development

If the means of thought are to support work across domains, their principles must be explainable as well as exact. Accessibility begins with showing what a distinction means, which problem calls for it, and what it makes possible. Technical language should carry a relationship already made intelligible, or help make a previously inaccessible relationship intelligible.

Practical accessibility also requires methods, examples, education and instruments through which others can use and criticize the work. A general principle may travel across domains while its application requires substantial specialist development. Increased accessibility makes rigor usable; it does not erase the knowledge needed to apply a principle responsibly.

Cumulative development preserves more than conclusions. It preserves the reasons a distinction was introduced, the scope of a method, and the defect a successor repaired. Those relationships allow later thinkers to improve the instruments rather than repeatedly recovering their purpose or treating their inherited form as final.

That work gives philosophical questions a concrete field of development while drawing on other disciplines as required.

It is a separate, preserved reference. This document neither revises it nor makes Abstography the exhaustive form of philosophical activity.

These are connected subjects. Their relationship is not a once-only sequence in which philosophy finishes before the other work begins.

## 11. What this initial definition establishes and leaves open

The present proposal establishes a positive centre: deliberate responsibility for the critical and constructive development of the means of understanding and productive possibility. Its philosophical scope includes inquiry into those means themselves, their relationships and their adequacy. It connects foundational work with realization without reducing the former to immediate utility.

Further development must decide the depth and precise commitments of this position. Its account of knowledge, explanation, truth, causality, purpose and value remains to be developed; the present document supplies working distinctions, not completed theories of those subjects. The relationship between this constructive orientation and the wider fields of philosophy also requires a fuller account.

The title's historical force remains a separate question. A future essay may argue for particular intellectual inheritances, failures or responsibilities, but those claims should follow research and explicit judgment rather than enter through the metaphor unnoticed. Likewise, institutional or educational proposals would be realizations of the orientation to be designed and assessed, not conclusions already entailed by this definition.

The central commitment can nevertheless be stated clearly: **the structures through which people understand and construct are themselves objects of responsible inquiry and construction. Developing them, exposing their limits and making better successors possible is substantive work in its own right.**
