## Candidate governing argument

Industrial possibility is limited not only by available physical resources and technical power but by the epistemic means through which subjects and possible work can be conceived. Developing those means can open different productive possibilities. Formal constitution and suitable realization can make the resulting understanding part of the operative basis of whole classes of activity. Deliberately developing this relationship across domains would make the creation of productive capability itself a principal industrial undertaking. Its growth remains conjectural, corrigible and capable of changing the next frontier.

## Sequence to attempt

1. State the proposed future and its name through a consequential claim about epistemic creation and industrial power.
2. Recover the historical analogy: formal and explanatory advances changed material possibility; organize the ambition, not merely apply finished science. Identify the neglected shared responsibility as the author's thesis.
3. Establish today's different inheritance and problem: immense intellectual and technological resources, but inadequate means for some emerging undertakings.
4. Show how those demands require semantic/ontological expansion and particular/general epistemic instruments, including adaptations of mathematical resources. Explain changes in intelligibility and constructive possibility.
5. Make the whole epistemic–technical relation reciprocal. Introduce domain modelling as work generated by those needs, not as the premise from which the civilization is deduced.
6. Develop the industrial consequence: capabilities spanning classes of activity; epistemic systems and productive arrangements themselves become products; new industries become conceivable. Distinguish this from automating a fixed inventory of jobs.
7. Integrate fallibility as responsibility for the relation between claims and actual capability, including adequate standards of accomplishment and means of correction.
8. Give the century horizon a cumulative consequence: successors inherit more powerful means of constituting and revising productive domains, hence a different frontier of possible work.
