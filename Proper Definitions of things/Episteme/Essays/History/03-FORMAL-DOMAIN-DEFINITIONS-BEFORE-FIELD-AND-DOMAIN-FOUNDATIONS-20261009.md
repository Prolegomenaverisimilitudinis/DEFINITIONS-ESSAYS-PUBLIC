# Formal Domain Definitions — Working Definition

**Status:** Initial working definition — 8 October 2026.

**Purpose:** To define what a formal domain definition is, what kind of whole it constitutes, why that whole requires more than separately formalized fragments, and how it relates to productive capability. This is a conceptual foundation for a later essay, not an essay, a software specification or a completed methodology.

The account develops the questions exposed by the earlier sketches without adopting their definitions or claims wholesale. Its primary concern here is domains constituted in relation to intended productive capabilities. Whether the phrase *formal domain definition* should also name a broader class of accounts serving other purposes remains a terminological question to settle.

The existing [Abstography definition](<../Abstography/01-ABSTOGRAPHY-WORKING-DEFINITION.md>) is an established reference for this work and must remain untouched. This document develops a related subject in depth; it does not redefine Abstography or reduce it to a later execution layer.

## 1. Central definition

**A formal domain definition is the explicit, coherently connected constitution of a domain's subjects, meanings, relationships, admissible formations and possible work at a declared scope. It establishes what its objects and constructions are, how their parts and contexts relate, and what must hold for their use, transformation and composition to have the intended meaning.**

For a domain intended to support productive capability, this includes the constitution of possible tasks, products, constructive means and their relationships. The depth required follows from what the capability must distinguish and accomplish. A collection of object names and a separate list of activities do not supply that constitution merely by appearing in the same document.

The definition can organize knowledge drawn from several fields and can introduce objects or relationships that none of those fields already contains. Its unity comes from the connected subject and work being constituted, not from belonging to one inherited discipline.

The resulting definition is one achievement. A realizing apparatus and grounds for its adequacy are further achievements. They inform the definition during its development: intended work can expose a missing object, and an attempted realization can expose an underdefined transformation. Their distinction does not imply that domain constitution must finish once and for all before realization is considered.

### 1.1 Field, domain and purpose

A *field* ordinarily names an established organization of inquiry or practice. The word *domain* can also be used in ordinary language for a subfield. Here its intended constructive sense is different: a domain is a designed, connected field of subjects and possible work whose boundary is related to an intended capability.

A domain can draw upon parts of many fields, preserve their differences and require new connections among them. It need not represent every subject in any contributing discipline. Conversely, a disciplinary label does not determine which distinctions the intended work requires.

Purpose helps reveal the required constitution. It does not uniquely determine it or prove that the desired capability can be realized. Several organizations may be adequate; a proposed organization may fail; the purpose itself may require clarification as its implications become explicit.

An individual requirement supplied to a capability is also distinct from the purpose of constituting that capability. Designing a particular arrangement and establishing a capacity to design an admitted class of arrangements are different objects of work.

### 1.2 Formal constitution and local formal knowledge

A formal result can be exact within a question whose relevant objects and assumptions have already been supplied. A domain definition also concerns how those objects, assumptions, questions and results acquire their connected places.

For example, a calculation can correctly determine a value for a specified configuration without establishing that the configuration is the one currently under consideration, that its premises apply, or that a later construction may use the result unchanged. The missing relationships do not become formal merely because the calculation does.

Formal constitution therefore concerns the organization through which the domain's consequential differences are expressible and its constructions interpretable. This can include local theories, their interfaces and the laws governing their joint use. It is not synonymous with one universal formalism or a requirement that every contribution have the same representation.

Explanation and formal expression have connected responsibilities. Explanation makes the intended objects and relationships intelligible. Formal expression supplies the exact structure needed to distinguish and reason about them. Their correspondence must itself be adequate; neither a formula without an interpretation nor an explanation that leaves consequential distinctions indeterminate completes the account.

## 2. What is constituted, and what already exists

Domain definition can concern things whose nature is not chosen by the designer, as well as objects and arrangements deliberately created for the work. Those cases must be related without confusing their grounds.

| Contribution to a domain | What the definition does | What warrants its use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account of an existing subject | Selects and expresses the distinctions needed to represent it at the declared scope. | Adequacy of that account to the subject and intended use. |
| Adopted formal structure | Gives an existing mathematical or logical construction a specified domain interpretation. | Its formal properties and the suitability of that interpretation. |
| Reconstructed connection | Makes explicit a relationship dispersed across sources or practice. | Grounds for the reconstruction, including the assumptions it introduces. |
| Designed constituent | Defines a new object, relation or arrangement required by the intended work. | Its coherent constitution and its adequacy for the stated constructive role. |
| Operative realization | Gives a defined responsibility a means of actual performance. | Correspondence between the defined responsibility and the realizing behavior. |

Calling a domain *designed* does not make external facts arbitrary. It identifies responsibility for the domain's constitution: what is represented, what is newly formed, how those parts relate and what claims are being made about them.

The distinction also prevents a different error. Newly required objects need not be extracted from a pre-existing source. A comparison basis, a family of supporting configurations or a joint subject spanning worlds can have a legitimate place because the intended work requires its construction.

## 3. The connected dimensions of a definition

The dimensions below are questions the account may have to answer. They are not mandatory software components or a claim that every domain needs an equally elaborate treatment of each.

| Dimension | Definitional responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Ontological | What kinds of subject exist, how they are individuated and what constitutes a composite. |
| Semantic | What objects and relations mean, in which contexts, and how that meaning bears on their use. |
| Structural and logical | Which formations and combinations are admitted, which laws apply and which combinations are excluded. |
| Constructive | Which task families, transformations, products and means of construction belong to the capability. |
| Interfacial | How constituents become available for internal, external or cross-world use, with what premises and guarantees. |
| Situational and developmental | Which states, changes and configurations matter, and how they affect possible work or existing results. |
| Realizational | How the domain's constructions acquire operative forms and how their effects correspond to the account. |
| Epistemic | Which claims are definitions, consequences, assumptions, observations or unresolved questions where that difference affects use. |

The most consequential structures often occur at their intersections. The same object's identity can matter to a transformation, the interpretation of a result and the propagation of a change. Defining these independently without their connecting laws can leave the overall construction underdefined.

## 4. Objects, composites and interfaces

An object requires more than a label. Depending on its nature, its constitution can include identity, type, constituents, relationships, scope, admissible states and laws of participation in further constructions.

A composite likewise requires more than a collection of exports. Its parts have relationships that make them parts of this object. A consumer may need one constituent, an indexed family of constituents, their supports or the entire connected structure. An interface specifies the access and what it preserves.

Consider a construction selecting components within assemblies. It can constitute a family of qualifying configurations, a family of selected assembly–component units, and the supporting configurations belonging to each unit. Counting the supports, counting those units and counting distinct components are different operations. One component may participate in several assemblies; a unique assembly accessor on its global identity would then require justification rather than follow from its name.

The point is general: **the objects a construction generates, the relations connecting them and the interfaces through which they can be used all belong to its definition.** A final count or truth value may be one result without exhausting the structure required for further work.

Availability belongs to this account where it matters. A defined empty family, an unavailable measurement and a defined false statement can imply different continuations. Which distinctions a particular domain needs, and how they propagate through its interfaces, must be established from the actual construction rather than imposed through one universal status vocabulary.

## 5. Worlds, contexts and shared identity

A domain's formal universe can contain several differently organized worlds. One world can emphasize structural relations, another functional organization, and another a substrate's objects and operations. Their contributions need not be reducible to one identical schema.

The same formal object can participate in more than one world, with different local relationships. Its shared identity does not require copying every local property into every participation. Conversely, similar names do not establish shared identity between distinct objects.

The definition must explain what is common and what remains local. Depending on the intended organization, this can concern shared populations, common properties, context-dependent relations or explicit correspondences between distinct subjects. Joint use requires those relationships to be available with the meaning the construction needs.

Compatibility of separate local accounts does not automatically establish a coherent whole. The account must state how the relevant contributions connect and which assembly or preservation properties hold. Coverings, sheaves or other mathematical structures may be useful where their actual laws fit; their names are not substitutes for defining the relationship.

Processes and Conditions can concern configurations spanning several worlds, including constituents generated by other constructions. Such use can be genuinely joint rather than a sequence of isolated local questions. The general definition must leave room for that depth even where an initial realization supports only narrower cases.

## 6. Possible work is part of the domain

Possible work develops with possible objects. A task needs meaningful subjects and circumstances; its product needs a constitution and an intended use. A proposed object can enable new work, while a task can expose a previously missing subject or intermediate structure.

The account must distinguish:

- The accomplishment required by a task.
- The admitted situations in which it is meaningful.
- The means or family of routes through which it can be performed.
- The object or change that counts as its result.
- The preservation obligations and permissible effects of its performance.

A constructor's task repertoire, its configuration and a particular occurrence of its work are consequently different matters. A fixed program can support different constructive courses as its input composition changes. The standing definition concerns the meanings and powers through which that variation is possible, not an enumeration of every future occurrence.

Different kinds of constructor can supply different powers. The domain must identify those powers where they are required, along with their inputs, effects and interfaces. Naming a technology does not fill in a missing transformation account.

This is part of the relationship to Abstography. Our established account treats domain constitution and possible constructive activity together. The present definition must therefore not describe a finished passive ontology to which Abstography is subsequently attached as an unrelated activation mechanism.

## 7. Possibility space, actuality and change

The definition of admitted kinds and formations is distinct from a current population. A domain can specify objects, relationships and task families before their particular future instances exist.

General formation laws can admit infinitely many constructions, including arbitrarily large permitted finite structures. The relevant boundary concerns the kinds of objects and work, their composition laws and the premises of their realization. An infinite possibility space does not eliminate resource requirements or establish that every imagined task is attainable.

When new objects appear, they can create new subjects for existing task families. An arrangement may introduce comparison questions or dependencies that did not previously have instances. That can exercise a standing capability rather than extend its defining laws.

Change also raises identity and dependency questions. Which transformations preserve an object? Which create a successor or a different object? When does a result remain applicable after its inputs change? Which consequences reach another world or consumer? These questions need their own domain-relative laws.

The existence of a result, the state from which it was constructed and the conditions under which it can now be used are connected but distinct. Their representation should follow the constructive responsibility, not automatically collapse into a timestamp or one undifferentiated status.

## 8. Domain mapping and domain definition

*Domain mapping* names the developing work through which an adequate constitution is found and formed. A *domain definition* names the resulting connected account at a particular stage. Mapping can include reconstruction, invention, formalization, criticism and revision; it is not merely describing a completed territory.

Several directions inform one another:

1. An intended capability reveals subjects and differences that matter.
2. Proposed tasks reveal inputs, intermediate achievements and constructive powers they require.
3. Proposed products reveal the constituents and interfaces their later use needs.
4. Sources and existing means reveal knowledge that can be adopted, limits that must be respected and gaps that remain.
5. Composition and realization expose relationships absent from the initial plan.
6. Those findings revise the relevant definitions and their actual dependents.

These are directions of reasoning rather than compulsory phases. A missing distinction need not always be repaired by adding a new object; an existing relation, interface or interpretation may need correction instead. The chosen repair should explain what it makes possible and why its added commitments are needed.

Mapping also needs a disciplined relationship to its purpose. The intended capability may require legitimate revision, but a failed attempt cannot silently redefine that intention as whatever happened to succeed. The changed purpose and its consequences should be explicit.

This document establishes the nature of that work. Its detailed methodology, instruments and evaluation procedures remain to be developed separately or in later sections as needed.

## 9. Adequacy has several meanings

| Question | What must be established |
| --- | --- |
| Is the account intelligible? | Its objects, meanings and relationships can be understood without guessing consequential missing commitments. |
| Is it formally coherent? | The relevant definitions, laws and combinations have the claimed consistency or other appropriate formal properties. |
| Is it adequate to the intended domain? | It preserves the distinctions and relationships required by the subject and capability being claimed. |
| Is its work constructively supported? | Required inputs, intermediate objects, transformations and interfaces have adequate formation and use accounts. |
| Is it operatively realized? | Actual means perform the required constructions and preserve the defined relationships. |
| Is its claimed reach established? | The evidence and general arguments support the scope asserted, rather than only selected successful cases. |

These achievements interact without collapsing into one certification. A checked formal expression can have the wrong interpretation. A successful operation can leave the general scope unestablished. A good conceptual definition can still lack a suitable realizing apparatus.

For an abstographic capability, the established definition supplies the stronger ambition of attaining admitted requirements throughout its bounded possibility space. Merely representing those requirements is insufficient. The present document must explain its contribution to that ambition without claiming that a domain definition alone proves attainment.

## 10. Completeness relative to an intended scope

Completeness should concern the responsibility being claimed. It is not the accumulation of everything that can be said about a field, nor a convenient cutoff at what current tools can express.

A consequential gap exists when the claimed work requires a distinction, constituent, relationship or power that the account does not adequately supply. That includes cases where separate parts are each described but their necessary combination has no defined meaning.

For example, a product may depend on an intermediate structure whose formation has never been established; a consumer may require an identity that an earlier passage discards; or a task may presuppose its own future result as an initial input. These are gaps in the construction, not just missing documentation.

A definition can be valuable while incomplete. The claim should identify what is established, what remains open and how the open matter affects intended work. Future discoveries can legitimately deepen a domain or change its scope. They should not erase the reason the predecessor was revised.

The precise notion of completeness appropriate to different kinds of domain is itself a subject for further definition. No universal decision procedure for domain adequacy is asserted here.

## 11. Why making domain definition explicit matters

Practitioners often supply relationships that the materials they use leave unstated. They determine the relevant occurrence, select an interpretation, recognize a missing premise and connect one result to another responsibility. This can be sophisticated, justified work.

Making the relevant constitution explicit can preserve more of that work, expose disagreements, support reuse and reduce the need to reconstruct the same relationships for every occurrence. It can also reveal that the desired capability requires newly created structures rather than merely more detailed documentation of current practice.

The aim is to make this constitutive activity increasingly understandable and practicable across domains. Appropriate explanation, mathematical instruments and constructive means can help. Their purpose is to support the domain's required depth, not force its nature into the representation most convenient to an early implementation.

Human judgment, situated knowledge or authority may be necessary constituents in some domains. They should receive exact places where needed. They are not a reason to make every possible formal construction pass through an identical assessment-and-approval architecture.

These are substantive issues to develop, not permission to treat all existing distinctions as undecided. They identify where this initial account needs further depth and where examples can test whether our explanations preserve the intended meaning.

## 14. Development discipline and source status

Improve definitions and explanations together. A changed concept must be reconciled with its dependents, examples and claims of adequacy. Preserve distinctions that perform necessary work; remove duplicate or superseded formulations rather than accumulating them.
